James and Hannah launch the Rider Panel, the market research part of Manual Focus Light Meter. It tests a product launch in a simulated world of AI agents, each with a life of their own. In the first study, a made-up folding smart trainer at £499 sold to 26 agents in 500 with reviews out at launch, and to 3 without them. A £399 Black Friday price took it to 41. They go through why agents bought or walked away, with the reach cap behind the Black Friday result. They finish on two jobs to hand to AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. Both need file uploads or pasted text, and the second also needs an assistant that can browse the web.

### 1. Rank the reasons people don't buy

```text
I run marketing for [brand], which sells [product] in [market].

I want to know why people who considered our product didn't buy it, ranked
by how often each reason comes up.

Attached or pasted below are:
- customer reviews of our product and our closest rivals
- returns or cancellation notes for the last [6] months
- notes from sales calls or customer service chats where a sale didn't happen

Group the comments by the reason behind them, not by their wording. Typical
reasons include waiting for a sale, price, not trusting a new brand, missing
features, and fit or space.

Give me a table with the columns: Reason | How many comments mention it |
One representative quote | Which source it mostly comes from.

Then list the three reasons we could most easily act on, with one line each on
what we could test.

Say which comments you couldn't place, and don't invent quotes.
```

### 2. Draft a launch-day review plan

```text
I'm planning the launch of [product] from [brand] in [market] on [date]. I want
independent reviews published on launch day.

Search the web for the reviewers, buyer's guides and specialist sites that
buyers of [product category] in [market] read most. Include written
publications and video reviewers.

Give me a table with the columns: Outlet or reviewer | Format | The guide or
review series it would sit in | Typical lead time before launch (if stated) |
Contact route shown publicly.

Then draft a timeline working back from [date]: when samples should go out, when
embargo briefings happen and when reviews should go live.

Mark anything you couldn't confirm, such as lead times or contact routes,
rather than guessing. Don't include private contact details.
```
