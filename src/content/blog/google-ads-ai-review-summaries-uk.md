Google can now write a positive-only AI summary of what reviewers across the web say about your business and place it inside your Search ad, in the UK, US, Australia and India. Its help page, first reported on 24 October 2026, says advertisers can't edit the text and names no opt-out, so your third-party reviews have become ad copy.

## Key takeaways

- Google's Ads help page "About Review summaries" describes AI-powered summaries of online reviews that appear directly as part of Search ads, in English in the US, UK, Australia and India.
- Google says the summaries are positive only and shown only when it predicts they will improve ad performance.
- Advertisers cannot edit or customise the generated text, because Google says it represents user feedback.
- The help page lists no opt-out, no reporting and no named review sources, and PPC News Feed first reported it on 24 October 2026.
- Rule 3.46 of the UK CAP Code bars publishing consumer reviews in a misleading way, including showing positive reviews while leaving out negative ones.

## What Google's help page says

The [About Review summaries](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/18760807) page in Google Ads Help says "Google AI automatically aggregates online reviews from across the web to generate review summaries", which "appear as short AI-generated commentary" on Search ads. Google lists four benefits, building trust, improving click quality, improving ad performance and strengthening brand trust, and gives no figures for any of them.

The eligibility list is short. Search campaigns qualify, in all ad positions on Google.com, on all supported devices, in English in the four markets. Every vertical qualifies except sensitive categories, with pharmaceuticals the only one named. Under key considerations Google says "advertisers cannot directly edit or customize the generated text", that summaries "are only positive", and that "Google AI uses grounding standards to ensure content reflects real feedback".

The page carries no date. [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-ai-writes-positive-only-review-summaries-into-search-ads-in-4-markets/) says PPC News Feed found it on 24 October, and [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-ai-review-summaries-doc-42370.html) covered it on 26 October.

## How did Google get here?

Google has tested review content in Search ads for almost a year. PPC Land lists review excerpts in mostly ecommerce ads from 3 December 2025, AI summaries under ad descriptions with a "can make mistakes" disclaimer from 3 July 2026, a "What people are saying" label in August and a "What customers love" block on a sponsored Semrush result on 5 September. The help page turns those tests into a documented ad feature in four markets, and the disclaimer from July doesn't appear in it.

## Does a positive-only summary fit UK rules?

Rule 1.8 of the [CAP Code](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/01.html) says "primary responsibility for observing the Code falls on marketers", with publishers and other suppliers also accepting an obligation. Section 3 of the [CAP Code](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html) then deals with reviews directly. Rule 3.46 says marketers must not publish consumer reviews, or "consumer review information", in a misleading way, and its examples include publishing positive reviews while leaving out negative ones and giving positive reviews greater prominence. The Code defines consumer review information as information derived from, or influenced by, consumer reviews, which describes a summary of them.

A summary Google writes and places in your ad without your approval is a new case for that rule, and the ASA hasn't ruled on it. The help page doesn't say how Google handles a business whose reviews are mostly poor, or whether a two-star brand can still be shown a glowing line. Rule 3.47 also asks marketers to hold evidence that testimonials are genuine, which is hard when Google doesn't say which reviews it read.

## What it means for paid and brand teams

For brand teams, reviews on sites you don't own now feed copy that runs in your paid search ads. If your Trustpilot, Google and marketplace reviews sit with customer service, they now also sit in the ad brief, because Google's AI reads them as source material and picks the positive themes it predicts will win the click. Our post on [earned channels in the AI era](/blog/earned-channels-pr-in-the-ai-era/) makes the same case for press coverage and AI answers.

For paid teams the problem is the read on performance. Google shows a summary only when it predicts better results, and the help page describes no report showing which impressions carried one, so a jump in click-through rate this quarter could come from a summary you never saw. The [attribution tear-down playbook](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) covers keeping platform-driven changes apart from your own tests.

Google is the second ad platform this month to put words into ads that the advertiser never approved, after the change we covered in [what ChatGPT Ads' automatic rewrites mean for UK advertisers](/blog/chatgpt-ads-creative-enhancements-default/). Google's source material is less within your reach, because it comes from other people's reviews rather than your own ad and landing page.

Our view at Manual Focus is that UK brands should treat review summaries as copy they are answerable for, and get the control question settled with Google in writing now. Brands in regulated or claims-heavy categories, where a positive line about results or safety would need substantiation, have the most to lose and should raise it first.

## Five checks for this week

1. **Ask Google who controls it.** Email your Google Ads representative to ask whether account-level automated asset settings switch review summaries off, which review sources feed them, and whether reporting will show when one ran. File the reply with the date.
2. **Read your reviews as Google will.** Pull the last six months of reviews from the sites that rank for your brand name and list the positive themes. Those are the lines Google is likely to write, so check that each one is a claim you would make in an ad.
3. **Brief legal on rule 3.46.** Send them Google's help page and the CAP Code text, and ask whether your category needs Google's summaries switched off or your review profile fixed before they run.
4. **Search your own ads.** Have someone in the UK search your top ten brand and category terms each week and screenshot any ad showing a summary. The [evaluation frameworks playbook](/lens/ops/evaluation-frameworks/) shows how to score a sample consistently.
5. **Mark a change date in reporting.** Annotate 24 October 2026 in your Google Ads and analytics reports, so a later shift in click-through or conversion rate on Search campaigns can be checked against it.

## Gaps Google hasn't filled

Google hasn't said when review summaries started serving in the UK, how often they appear, or which review sites it reads. It hasn't said whether a business can dispute a summary that misstates its reviews, what "grounding standards" means in practice, or whether summaries will reach Performance Max and AI Max placements. The help page also says nothing about review summaries in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Recheck the page before you report on it, because Google updates help pages without notice.
