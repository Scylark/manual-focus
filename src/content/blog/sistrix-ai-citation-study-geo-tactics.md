SISTRIX published a test of which on-page GEO tactics go with AI citations on 22 October 2026, comparing 96,418 URLs across ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews. Pages meeting many of its eleven features were cited 2.1 times as often as barely optimised ones, and llms.txt showed no measurable effect, so content teams should fix page structure before adding files.

## Key takeaways

- SISTRIX compared 48,527 pages cited by ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews with uncited pages from the same domains, 96,418 URLs from 17,380 domains in all.
- A table of contents scored 1.7 and Article schema 1.6, where 1.0 means no difference, yet only 14% of pages have a table of contents.
- Domains with an llms.txt file supplied 11.7% of cited URLs against 14.0% of all domains, so SISTRIX found no measurable effect.
- Only 10% of cited pages met many of the eleven features SISTRIX combined into an overall score.
- Advice and how-to content made up 48.7% of cited pages, and brand websites supplied 38.7%.

## How SISTRIX ran the test

Johannes Beus, Julia Weissbach and the SISTRIX content team took 48,527 cited URLs from the SISTRIX AI Index for Germany, Austria and Switzerland in the second quarter of 2026, about 16,000 from each of ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews. They paired each one with a page from the same domain that wasn't cited, then checked 43 features on every page.

Comparing pages within one domain takes domain authority out of the result. The [SISTRIX write-up](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/citation-worthiness-study/) reports each feature as an odds ratio, so a score above 1.0 means cited pages had that feature more often and below 1.0 means less often.

SISTRIX also shows why small studies mislead. It ran the FAQ schema test on samples of three sizes and got three different answers, 23% less common on cited pages at 833 URLs, 31% more common at 4,600 and 19% more common at 96,418. The direction only settled from about 5,000 URLs, and the authors advise ignoring GEO studies smaller than that.

## Which tactics showed an effect?

Most of the positive signals fall into two groups, pages that are easy to extract from and pages that show a real person stands behind them and keeps them current.

| Feature | SISTRIX score | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Table of contents | 1.7 | Add one to long advice pages, since only 14% have one |
| Article schema | 1.6 | Mark up guides and advice pages |
| Five or more H2 headings | 1.6 | Break long pages into clear sections |
| FAQPage schema | 1.5 | Use it only for genuine questions |
| Table with a header row | 1.5 | Put comparisons and specs in a real table |
| Author box with photo | 1.4 | Name the author and show who they are |
| Question as an H2 | 1.4 | Phrase some headings as the buyer's question |
| Visible last-updated date | 1.3 | Show it, and only change it when the content changes |
| Breadcrumb, HowTo or QAPage schema | 1.0 | Keep them for other reasons, expect nothing here |
| Recipe schema | 0.1 | No action, SISTRIX thinks AI prefers topic pages |

The platforms differ at the edges. ChatGPT scored 1.9 for question-style titles and 1.8 for tables with header rows. AI Mode scored 1.8 for a table of contents and for five or more H2s. AI Overviews scored 2.0 for Article schema and 0.5 for product pages.

## Does llms.txt help AI citations?

Not in this data. Domains with the file supplied a smaller share of cited URLs than their share of the sample, and SISTRIX says llms.txt shouldn't be a priority. Its comparison works at domain level, so it can't rule out other differences between the sites that have the file and those that don't.

Google says the same about its own products. Its [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) tells site owners they "don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup" to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and that there's "no special schema.org structured data" to add. Article schema still scored well in the SISTRIX data, which probably says more about the care behind pages that carry it than about the markup itself.

## Should shops change product pages?

SISTRIX's split by site type points online shops somewhere else. For shops, Product schema scored 0.6 and a visible last-updated date 0.7, while brand websites scored 1.6 for both Article and FAQ schema and editorial sites scored 2.5 for a table of contents. SISTRIX's advice to shops is an advice section with Article schema, rather than more work on product pages. Product schema scored 1.7 for SaaS providers, so the answer depends on what you sell.

## Where content budgets should go

Our view at Manual Focus is that the SISTRIX study lets content teams drop the AI-only extras and put their GEO hours into page craft that Google Search rewards anyway, meaning clear sections, real tables, named authors and honest update dates on the advice pages buyers' questions land on. These are the same habits we set out in [our SEO cluster generator](/lens/content/seo-cluster-generator/), and none of them needs a separate AI budget line.

The study also measures something narrower than visibility. A page can be cited without your brand being recommended, and AI answers increasingly cite pages Google doesn't rank, which we covered in [the citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/). Page structure decides whether an engine can lift your answer. Whether buyers then see your brand in those answers needs its own tracking.

The title result needs care. Pages with 2025 or 2026 in the title scored 2.7 and pages showing only an older year 0.7, but SISTRIX reads the year as a sign of fresh content and says it can't confirm that changing the year alone helps. A team that edits the year on stale pages risks a misleading title and gains nothing the data supports.

## Five fixes for your top pages

1. **Score your 20 most important advice pages.** Check each for a table of contents, five or more H2s, a question heading, a table with a header row, an author box with a photo and a visible update date. Our [site audit and refresh playbook](/lens/ops/site-audit-and-refresh/) covers how to run the sweep and ship fixes.
2. **Add Article schema to guides, and FAQPage only where the questions are real.** SISTRIX's 1.5 for FAQ schema held only for genuine questions, not token ones written for markup.
3. **Refresh before you re-date.** Update the facts on pages with an old year in the title, then change the title and the visible date together.
4. **Take llms.txt off the roadmap.** Leave an existing file in place, since other tools may read it, but Google says Search doesn't need one, so don't spend sprint time on it.
5. **Find the questions where rivals get cited.** Bing's [Topics Portfolio report](/blog/bing-topics-portfolio-ai-citation-gaps/) lists AI answer queries where other sites are cited and yours isn't, which tells you which pages to fix first.

## Where the study runs out

The study shows correlation, and SISTRIX says so. A table of contents may sit on pages that are better in ways the 43 features don't capture. The data comes from Germany, Austria and Switzerland in one quarter, and UK results could differ. Perplexity, Claude and the Gemini app weren't included, though SISTRIX plans a follow-up. Gemini 3.5 Flash classified domain and content types, with 84% agreement in a cross-check against Gemini 3.1 Pro, so the site-type splits carry more noise than the headline figures. The title findings were analysed after the fact and are labelled initial indications. The scores describe the second quarter of 2026, and the engines change often.
