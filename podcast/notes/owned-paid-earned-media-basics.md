A Manual Focus Basics episode, and the foundation for our three earlier pieces
on owned, paid and earned channels in the AI era. James and Hannah explain
owned media (what you control), paid media (attention you buy) and earned media
(what others choose to say about you), starting from Forrester's 2009 report No
Media Should Stand Alone. They cover Seth Godin's permission marketing, why a
social profile is closer to rented than owned, Gini Dietrich's PESO model, the
UK CAP Code rules that keep paid content identifiable, and how to measure each
type on its own terms.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. All three work with
pasted text or uploaded files.

### 1. Audit your owned, paid and earned mix

```text
I lead marketing at [company]. I want to audit our media mix using the owned,
paid and earned framework.

Definitions:
- Owned: channels we control and where we keep the audience (website, email
  list, app, blog, podcast).
- Rented: our own profiles on platforms that control who sees them (social
  profiles, marketplace listings).
- Paid: attention we buy (ads, sponsorship, paid creator posts).
- Earned: what others choose to say or share about us (coverage, reviews,
  mentions, citations).

Attached is a list of every channel we use, with spend and results where we
have them.

Give me a table with the columns: Channel | Owned / rented / paid / earned |
Spend | The job it's doing (permission and conversion, reach, or
credibility) | How it's measured today | Better measure, if any.

Then list any type with no clear job, and any channel you couldn't classify
from the information given, rather than guessing.
```

### 2. Check paid campaigns grow the owned audience

```text
I want to know which of last quarter's paid campaigns left us with audience we
own, such as email subscribers or registered users.

Attached are:
- the paid campaigns, with dates, spend and channel
- our email list or registered-user growth by week for the same period, with
  sign-up source where we track it

Give me a table with the columns: Campaign | Dates | Spend | New owned
audience attributed (where sign-up source shows it) | New owned audience in
the same weeks (where it doesn't) | Cost per new owned contact.

Be clear about which numbers come from tracked sign-up sources and which are
only timing coincidences. If the data can't connect a campaign to list
growth, say so in that row.
```

### 3. Score your emails against permission marketing

```text
Seth Godin describes permission marketing as delivering messages that are
anticipated, personal and relevant to people who want to get them.

Attached are our last ten marketing emails, and a description of how people
join our list: [describe sign-up routes, such as checkout box, competition,
newsletter page].

For each email, give me: Subject line | Anticipated? (would the subscriber
expect it, given how they joined) | Personal? | Relevant? | Score out of 3 |
One change that would most improve it.

Then say which sign-up routes are least likely to carry real permission, and
why. Base it only on the emails and sign-up routes provided, and say where you
can't judge without engagement data.
```
