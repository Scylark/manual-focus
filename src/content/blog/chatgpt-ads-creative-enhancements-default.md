On 29 October 2026, OpenAI will switch on creative enhancements for every new and existing ChatGPT Ads campaign that hasn't opted out, letting its system rewrite headlines, translate copy, resize images and change landing pages. Those versions can run without anyone at the brand approving them, so UK advertisers have eight days to choose which campaigns keep the setting.

## Key takeaways

- OpenAI's help centre says creative enhancements will be enabled automatically for new and existing ChatGPT Ads campaigns starting Thursday 29 October 2026.
- Enhancements can change four things, the headline and description, the language, the image size and the destination URL, using the existing ad, its landing page and the conversation.
- OpenAI says enhanced versions can run without review or approval, and Ads Manager won't preview every variation.
- Existing account and campaign opt-outs are kept, and a campaign setting overrides the account default, so a brand can run enhancements on some campaigns and not others.
- When OpenAI launched the feature on 16 September 2026 it was something advertisers had to opt into, and it covered text and translation only.

## How OpenAI changed the setting

OpenAI announced "AI-powered text customization" on [16 September 2026](https://openai.com/index/reimagining-advertising-with-ai/), alongside Sponsored Agents and the Shopify app we covered in [our post on ChatGPT Ads for UK ecommerce](/blog/chatgpt-ads-shopify-uk-ecommerce/). Advertisers had to opt in, and it adapted existing headlines and descriptions to the conversation and translated them into the user's language. We suggested leaving it off until a brand had read what it wrote.

The new [creative enhancements page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001614-creative-enhancements-for-chatgpt-ads) in OpenAI's help centre widens the feature and flips the default. It adds image resizing and destination URL changes, says variations "may be prepared in advance or created in real time based on the conversation", and states that "when enabled, these ads can run automatically without requiring you to review or approve each version."

## Which date, 29 or 30 October?

The help centre says Thursday 29 October. The email OpenAI sent advertisers, shown by [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/openai-chatgpt-ads-enabling-creative-enhancements-42344.html) on 20 October, says Friday 30 October and refers to self-serve campaigns, where the help page says new and existing campaigns. OpenAI hasn't explained the gap, so set your choice before the 29th and treat accounts with a sales contact as covered unless OpenAI tells you otherwise.

## What can OpenAI change in your ad?

| Element | What OpenAI says it may do | Where it can go wrong | Who should check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline and description | Write new or updated text to fit the conversation or user interests | A claim, price or tone your brand wouldn't sign off | Brand and legal |
| Language | Translate ad text into another language | Copy in a language nobody on the team reads | Brand |
| Image | Resize to fit the ad format | Cropped logos, text or product detail | Creative |
| Destination URL | Send the click to a "relevant page" on your site | Clicks land on a page with no offer, an old promotion or no tracking | Paid and analytics |

OpenAI's own example turns "Fresh groceries delivered" into "Groceries for quick weeknight dinners" for a conversation about weeknight cooking, which is the harmless end of the range. The page also asks advertisers to keep prices, promotions, product details and availability current on their landing pages, because the system draws on them. A sale page left live after the sale ends becomes source material for an ad.

## Who answers for the rewritten ad?

In the UK, the brand does. Rule 1.8 of the [CAP Code](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/01.html) says "primary responsibility for observing the Code falls on marketers", and rule 1.10 gives marketers primary responsibility for making sure their ads are legal. Agencies and publishers also accept an obligation under 1.8, but nothing in OpenAI's help page moves responsibility for an enhanced headline off the advertiser whose name sits on it.

That matters most where one word changes the claim, such as a price, a "free" offer, a health benefit or a comparison with a rival. A system writing in real time from a conversation can produce a line no compliance reviewer saw, and in a regulated category that line is the brand's problem.

## What it means for paid and brand teams

Paid teams lose a clean read on their own creative. OpenAI's reporting covers impressions, clicks, spend, click-through rate and attributed conversions at campaign, ad group and ad level, and its help page warns that "changes over time do not, on their own, establish the effect of creative enhancements." A campaign that improves in November may owe the gain to rewritten copy, a new landing page OpenAI chose, or the season, and Ads Manager won't separate them. The [attribution tear-down playbook](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) sets out how to keep a platform's claims apart from your own evidence.

For brand teams the loss is the wording itself. Until now, a brand had to opt in before any ChatGPT ad ran with copy nobody at the brand or its agency had approved, and OpenAI's own [16 September announcement](https://openai.com/index/reimagining-advertising-with-ai/) said advertisers could review and edit Ads Manager's suggested copy before using it. Our [brand guardrails as code playbook](/lens/ops/brand-guardrails-as-code/) turns the rules an approver applies in their head into checks, and OpenAI's system will apply none of them unless they are already visible in the ad and the landing page.

Our view at Manual Focus is that UK brands should keep creative enhancements off at account level and switch them on for one test campaign each, where the claims are simple, the landing pages are current and a person reads a sample of the ads every week. Turn them on more widely only once a properly split test shows lower cost per conversion with enhancements on. Meta already generates creative from a brand's own assets in Advantage+, as our guide to [Meta and TikTok's Advertising Week tools](/blog/meta-tiktok-advertising-week-ai-ad-tools/) shows, but OpenAI's version can write the copy in real time from each conversation.

## What to set before 29 October

1. **Set the account default to off.** Do it in Ads Manager under Manage settings, and record the date and who changed it.
2. **Pick one test campaign.** Choose a product with no regulated claims and a stable price, and turn enhancements on at campaign level, since that setting overrides the account default.
3. **Plan a fair comparison.** A duplicate campaign with enhancements off is a rough guide at best, because the two compete in the same auctions and the platform can deliver them to different conversations. Treat it as directional, and before you widen the setting agree a holdout or geo-lift design with finance. Our [ChatGPT Ads measurement post](/blog/chatgpt-ads-measurement-visual-ads/) covers the geo-lift partners OpenAI named and the conversion setup a test needs.
4. **Audit the landing pages.** Remove expired offers, out-of-stock products and pages without tracking from anywhere an enhanced URL could send a click, because OpenAI says the destination may move to "a relevant page on your website".
5. **Write down your no-go claims.** List the prices, comparisons and phrases your brand can't use, and check a sample of live ads against the list each week. Our [evaluation frameworks playbook](/lens/ops/evaluation-frameworks/) shows how to score a sample consistently.

## Gaps in OpenAI's explanation

OpenAI hasn't said whether UTM parameters on your creative URLs carry over when the system changes the destination, which affects every analytics report that relies on them. It doesn't say whether Ads Manager will show which version of an ad ran, or let you see the rewritten text after the fact. The help page doesn't say how translated ads are chosen for UK users, or whether a brand can limit the languages. The 29 and 30 October dates still disagree, and OpenAI hasn't confirmed whether accounts managed by its sales team follow the same default.
