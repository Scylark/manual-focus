For the first time, Google has told site owners about a core update before it starts. On 24 November 2026 it listed the December 2026 core update on its Search Status Dashboard, aimed at longer, complex questions and personalised results, with no start date. SEO teams now get a warning before ranking shifts hit, thanks to UK competition rules.

## Key takeaways

- Google pre-announced the December 2026 core update on 24 November 2026 and said only that it is scheduled for release in December.
- The update targets how Google ranks content for longer and more complex questions, and personalised results for users with personalisation turned on, in every language and location.
- Google's new Stay informed page ties advance notice of UK ranking shifts to the CMA's fair ranking conduct requirement, which the CMA imposed on 17 June 2026.
- Verified site owners can now send feedback on a listed update from Search Console, but Google says it won't adjust rankings for individual URLs as a result.
- The November 2026 core update, which began on 10 November, ran for 13 days and 20 hours before the December notice went up.

## What Google published on 24 November

The notice went up on the [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/AXrqxXXdtSjj3672ux7X) at 07:03 Pacific time. Google wrote that the update "focuses on multiple improvements to how our systems understand and rank relevant content", including for longer and more complex questions, and that it also improves personalised results for people who have personalisation switched on. Barry Schwartz at [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-december-2026-core-update-42615.html) reported that the dashboard briefly showed the update as active. A Google spokesperson told him it was "not imminent" and that Google would announce the rollout the day it starts.

Google shipped a set of new documentation the same day, and each page carries a last-updated date of 24 November 2026.

| Page | What it says | Who should read it |
|---|---|---|
| Search ranking hub | One home for ranking principles, update notices, feedback and troubleshooting | Head of SEO |
| Stay informed | Google gives notice of core updates and actionable policy changes, with an RSS feed of the dashboard | Whoever owns reporting |
| Submit feedback | Verified owners can report issues on a listed update in Search Console, tagged with its hashtag | SEO lead, after an update ends |
| Attribution and factuality | AI Overviews and AI Mode run on core ranking and quality systems and use query fan-out | Content and SEO teams |
| Generative AI controls | Google-Extended covers training and grounding of some products and is not a ranking signal | Whoever owns robots.txt |

## Why is Google giving notice now?

The [Stay informed page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/ranking/stay-informed) says so directly. Where UK users may see a significant ranking shift, Google gives advance notice under the CMA's fair ranking conduct requirements. The [CMA imposed that requirement](https://www.gov.uk/find-digital-markets-measures/google-search-fair-ranking-conduct-requirement) on 17 June 2026. It asks Google to rank organic results on "objective and non-discriminatory criteria, including in search generative AI features", to give publishers "sufficient notice and information about material changes that could affect publishers", and to let them raise concerns effectively.

The rule is British and the notice is global, because Google announced the December update for every location and language. Schwartz notes that Google had previously said it doesn't pre-announce core updates. The same CMA regime is behind the choice screen proposals we covered in [what the CMA's AI choice screen plan means for UK marketers](/blog/cma-google-choice-screen-ai-assistants/).

## What changes for SEO and content teams?

The biggest change is to planning. Core updates used to arrive unannounced, so a site migration, a template rebuild or a big content prune could land in the middle of one and nobody could separate the two effects afterwards. With notice, a team can keep large changes out of the update window. November showed the cost of overlap, when the core update began on 10 November and Gemini 4 Hydrogen reached Search two days later, as we set out in [what Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Google Search means for SEO teams](/blog/gemini-4-hydrogen-google-search-seo-teams/).

The December brief points at a particular kind of content. Longer and more complex questions are the conversational queries people type into AI Mode, and Google's new [attribution and factuality page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/gen-ai-attribution-factuality) says its generative AI features are "integrated directly with Google's core web ranking, information retrieval, and quality systems." A core update about complex questions will probably move both the blue links and the pages AI Overviews cite, since Google describes one ranking system feeding both. The same page tells site owners to "avoid changing a page's date unless the content has substantially changed", a direct warning to teams that refresh dates to look current.

Personalisation is the part teams can't see. Rank trackers check results logged out, and the December update changes results for people who are logged in with personalisation on. For anyone with personalisation on, a tracked position now tells you less about what that customer sees.

Our view at Manual Focus is that the notice is worth more than the hub, because a known window lets a team keep its own changes out of the update and read the results cleanly afterwards, which nobody could do before. The feedback route is useful only for reporting a pattern, since Google says plainly that it won't change rankings for individual URLs.

## Does Google-Extended affect rankings?

No. Google's [page on generative AI controls](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/gen-ai-controls) says "Google-Extended status is not used as a ranking signal in Search", and it now separates training, teaching future models, from grounding, which happens after a model is deployed. Google-Extended limits both for certain Google products. To keep a page out of AI Overviews and AI Mode inputs, Google points to `nosnippet` and `max-snippet` instead, which also change the snippet in normal results. Teams that set up the shared training token we covered in [what the shared AI training opt-out means for brand websites](/blog/shared-ai-training-opt-out-robots-txt/) should check that their snippet controls do what they intended in AI features.

## What to do before the December update

1. **Subscribe to the dashboard feed.** Add the Search Status Dashboard RSS feed to the SEO team's channel, so the rollout start reaches you the day Google posts it.
2. **Freeze big changes.** Hold migrations, template changes and large content prunes until the December update has finished, or ship them this week if they can't wait.
3. **Take a baseline now.** The November update has ended, so export Search Console clicks, impressions and the generative AI report by page type today. The [site audit and refresh playbook](/lens/ops/site-audit-and-refresh/) covers how to group pages for this.
4. **Audit your complex-question pages.** List the pages that answer long, multi-part questions and check they are current, sourced and written by someone named. Our [SEO cluster generator](/lens/content/seo-cluster-generator/) maps question intent to pages.
5. **Stop date-only refreshes.** Tell writers and agencies that a page's date changes only when its content does, as Google's attribution page now asks.

## What we don't know yet

Google gave no start date and no lead time, and its Stay informed page doesn't say how much notice future updates will get. It hasn't said how much the December update weighs complex questions against other changes, or how personalisation will differ, and no published report will show personalised results separately. Google also hasn't said whether changes to AI Overviews and AI Mode, as opposed to core updates, will get the same advance notice, although the CMA requirement covers generative AI features. The feedback route is new, so nobody yet knows what Google does with what it collects or whether it will publish anything back.
