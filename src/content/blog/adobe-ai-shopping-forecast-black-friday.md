AI assistants will send 130% more shoppers to US retail sites this holiday season than last, according to a forecast Adobe published on 28 September 2026. The figure counts clicks from AI chat and browser services, and it gives UK ecommerce teams about eight weeks before Black Friday on 27 November to measure the channel properly.

## Adobe's numbers for November and December

Adobe's [holiday forecast](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-us-holiday-shopping-season-to-hit-record) expects US online spending to reach $275.1 billion between 1 November and 31 December, up 6.7% on 2025. The AI figures sit inside that. Adobe defines AI traffic as shoppers clicking a link from an AI service to a retail site, and it expects the steepest rise on Thanksgiving (up 159% year on year), with Black Friday up 95% and Cyber Monday up 88%.

The forecast draws on more than 1 trillion visits to US retail sites and 100 million products across 18 categories, backed by a survey of 5,000 US consumers in July. Among people who had already shopped with AI, 77% said it made them more confident in a purchase and 69% said they were less likely to send an item back. Adobe says it first saw shoppers using AI chat and browser services for deals and product searches in the 2024 holiday season.

Vivek Pandya, Adobe's director of digital insights, said consumers are "getting much more comfortable having dialogues with an agent, getting recommendations and going to the retail site from those conversations", and that the use now reaches "beyond big-ticket items to everyday essentials", [as the Spokesman-Review reported](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/sep/28/ai-assisted-shopping-to-rise-130-during-holidays-a/).

## Why a US forecast should change a UK plan

Adobe's data is American and Thanksgiving means little to UK shoppers, but Black Friday and Cyber Monday are fixed points in the UK retail calendar, and the assistants driving the US numbers are the same ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot products British shoppers use. A UK brand that can't see its own AI referrals today will go into the busiest trading weeks of the year without a baseline.

US shoppers are handing product research to these assistants. When a shopper asks an assistant to compare three running jackets under £150, the assistant reads product pages, reviews and retailer listings, then sends a click to whichever page answered best. Pages with thin descriptions, sizing hidden in images or prices that load late give the assistant less to quote. Your product catalogue now does part of the job a comparison site or a paid search ad used to do.

Our view at Manual Focus is that UK retail and DTC brands should treat AI referrals as a named channel in their peak trading report this year, with its own baseline and targets, even while the numbers are small. We'd set the targets on conversion and returns rather than volume, because Adobe's survey points at a shopper who arrives further along in the decision. Paid placement in these assistants is arriving too, which we covered in [what ChatGPT Ads on Shopify means for UK ecommerce brands](/blog/chatgpt-ads-shopify-uk-ecommerce/).

## Four jobs to finish before 27 November

1. **Build an AI referral channel in analytics this week.** Group traffic from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and copilot.microsoft.com into one channel, then pull September and October as your pre-peak baseline.
2. **Compare it with paid search and organic.** Put conversion rate, average order value and 30-day return rate for AI referrals next to your other channels. The [attribution tear-down](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) playbook covers how to avoid double counting when the same shopper also clicks an ad.
3. **Fix the pages assistants would quote.** Take your 20 best-selling Black Friday products and make sure sizing, materials, compatibility, price and stock appear in plain text on the page.
4. **Check nothing blocks the assistants.** Ask whoever runs your CDN and robots.txt to confirm that bot-protection rules haven't shut out the AI search crawlers that fetch your product pages.

## Gaps in the forecast

Adobe's release gives growth rates but not the share of all retail visits that AI referrals make up, so a 130% rise could still be a small slice of traffic. The count covers clicks only, which misses every purchase an AI answer shaped without sending a visit. The survey is self-reported and limited to people already shopping with AI. And the forecast covers only the US, so British brands have to build the evidence from their own data.
