James and Hannah talk through Claude Duet, the proactive agent Anthropic opened to
a US waitlist on 21 October 2026. It runs on its own cloud computer. It messages
you when it decides something needs you. It asks before it sends a message or
makes a payment, but its approval list says nothing about publishing a post or
editing a live page. The early users will be on personal plans that no company
admin controls. So the hosts argue for writing agent rules before the invitations
arrive. They finish with three jobs to hand to AI and one for you.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for your
own details. The first needs a file upload. The second needs a recurring schedule.

### 1. Write the approval rules Anthropic left out

```text
I run marketing at [company]. We're preparing for staff using personal AI agents
that can act in our tools on their own judgement.

Attached is a list of the tools my team uses, with the actions each person
takes in each one. Publishing a social post is one example. Changing a
HubSpot list is another.

For every action, draft one approval rule using one of these options:
- the agent may do it alone
- the agent prepares it for a person to approve
- the agent must never do it
Name the role who approves.

Give me a table with the columns: Tool | Action | Agent may... | Approver |
Reason.

Flag any action where you're unsure of the risk instead of guessing.
```

### 2. Score a Monday performance brief for two weeks

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every Monday for two weeks.

Each Monday I'll paste in the performance brief an AI agent wrote for my
marketing team, plus the raw numbers it was based on.

Score the brief against this checklist:
- Did it flag the changes a marketing lead would act on?
- Did it miss anything important in the numbers?
- Is every figure traceable to a source?
- Did it raise anything that didn't need attention?

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Flagged correctly | Missed |
Untraceable figures | False alarms | Score out of 10.

After the second week, tell me in two sentences whether the brief has earned
enough trust to stop checking it line by line.
```

### 3. Compare two agents on the same job

```text
I gave two AI agents the same brief: [paste the brief].

Here is what the first agent produced: [paste output A].
Here is what the second agent produced: [paste output B].

Compare them. List what each flagged and what each left out. Mark any claim
that isn't supported by the brief or the data.

Give me a table with the columns: Item | Agent A | Agent B | Which is right?

End with the three differences that matter most for a marketing team. Don't
pick a winner on style alone. Say what you couldn't check
rather than guessing.
```

### And one for you

Ask your IT admins whether staff can connect third-party apps to Google Workspace
or HubSpot with their own accounts. Find out who gets told when they do.
