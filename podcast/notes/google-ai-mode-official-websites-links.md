title: "What Google's new AI Mode links mean for brand websites"
---

James and Hannah talk through the five link changes Google announced for AI
Mode and AI Overviews on 15 October 2026. The biggest one for brands is a test:
a section at the top of some answers for the one or two official websites that
can complete a task, such as a return or a booking. They cover what counts as
official, who should care about Preferred Sources, why UK teams should watch
the creator test rather than act on it, and three jobs to hand to AI this
month.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first two need
file uploads; the third needs an assistant that can browse the web and run on
a schedule.

### 1. Find your top customer tasks

```text
I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell] at [website], selling
mainly in [market].

Google is testing an "official websites" section at the top of AI Mode and AI
Overviews for tasks where only one or two official sites can help, such as a
return, a warranty claim or a booking. I want to know which of our customer
tasks could trigger it.

Attached are:
- a Search Console Performance export of queries for the last 3 months
- our help-desk ticket counts by tag or category for the same period

Find the ten customer tasks people search for or contact us about most (for
example returns, delivery, warranty, sizing, booking, account help). Group
queries and tickets by task, not by wording.

Give me a table with the columns: Task | Example searches | Monthly searches
(from Search Console) | Tickets in the period | The URL on our site that
currently answers it (or "none found").

Rank by combined demand. If a query or tag could belong to more than one task,
say which you chose and why. Don't estimate any number you can't see in the
files; mark it "not in data" instead.
```

### 2. Audit the page for each task

```text
Here are our ten most common customer tasks and the URL we think answers each
one: [paste the table from the first prompt].

For each task, check our site at [website] and tell me:
1. How many pages answer this task. List every URL you find, including help
   centre, regional and campaign pages.
2. Whether the answer is on a crawlable HTML page, or stuck in a PDF, behind a
   login, or inside an app.
3. Whether the page title says plainly what the page does.
4. Whether the first paragraph answers the question directly.

Then, for each task, recommend the single page that should be the official
one, list which duplicates to merge or redirect into it, and draft a new
opening paragraph (under 60 words) that answers the task in plain language.

Give the audit as one table with a row per task, followed by the drafted
paragraphs. Flag anything you couldn't open or check rather than guessing.
```

### 3. Check AI Mode every fortnight

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every other Monday morning.

Our ten customer tasks, and the official page for each, are: [paste the task
and URL list].

For each task, search Google in AI Mode from a UK location, using the
plainest phrasing a customer would use (for example "[brand] returns").
Record:
- which page or pages the AI answer links to, and in what order
- whether an "official websites" section appears at the top
- if our official page isn't the first link, whose site is

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Task | Search used | First link |
Our page linked? (yes/no, and position) | Official section shown? (yes/no) |
Competitor or retailer that outranked us.

Compare it with the previous run and list anything that changed at the top.
If AI Mode wouldn't load or showed no AI answer for a search, say so in that
row rather than leaving it blank.
```

### And one for you

Add an annotation dated 15 October 2026 in your analytics. Every link inside
an AI Overview shares the same position in Search Console, so this change
won't show up as a ranking move. The annotation is how you'll tie any change
in clicks back to it.
