James and Hannah introduce Manual Focus Light Meter, the tool behind the
Manual Focus AI Shortlist research, and explain why measuring AI visibility
needs three things a rank tracker doesn't do. Repeated runs, because in the
UK indoor cycling study an engine named the same brands across three runs of
a question only 20% of the time. Separate engines, because Van Rysel appeared
in 43% of Claude's trainer answers and 5% of Google AI Mode's. And 95% ranges,
because Rouvy and MyWhoosh look like second and third but overlap. They finish
with three jobs to hand to AI, what Light Meter can't tell you, and the next
edition, trail running kit in January 2027.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first needs file
uploads. The second needs an assistant that can run searches on other engines
and a schedule, or a person to paste the answers in. The third needs web
browsing.

### 1. Write your question set

```text
I run marketing for [brand], which sells [category] in the UK.

I want the 10 unbranded questions buyers ask most before they choose a brand
in my category, so I can check which brands AI engines name in reply.

Attached are:
- a Search Console export of queries for the last 3 months
- our customer service tags or ticket subjects for the same period

Write the 10 questions the way a UK buyer would type them into an AI
assistant, with no brand names in them. Tag each with its buying moment:
value, beginner, performance or problem (something isn't working).

Give me a table with the columns: Question | Buying moment | Evidence (the
queries or tags it came from).

Don't invent search volumes or demand. If the files don't support a
question, leave it out and say why.
```

### 2. Run the questions on a schedule

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs once a quarter, on the same questions
each time.

Here are 10 unbranded buyer questions for [category] in the UK:
[paste questions]

Ask each question three times on each of these AI engines, in a fresh
conversation each time and set to a UK location where possible: [list at
least three engines you can reach]. If you can't reach an engine yourself,
say so and give me a simple form so a person can paste the answers in.

Record every brand each answer names. Then give me a table with the columns:
Engine | Question | Brands named in run 1 | Run 2 | Run 3 | Times [brand] was
named (0 to 3) | Times [competitor 1], [competitor 2] and [competitor 3] were
named.

Finish with our shortlist rate on each engine (answers naming us divided by
all answers), the change since the last run, and a note that rates from 30
answers are rough, so small gaps between brands may be noise. List any runs
you couldn't complete.
```

### 3. Find the sites the engines cite

```text
I run marketing for [brand] in [category], UK.

For these buyer questions: [paste 5 of your questions], find the websites AI
engines cite when they answer. Where you can see an engine's cited sources,
use those. Otherwise find the buyer's guides, reviews and round-ups that rank
for the questions.

Give me a table with the columns: Site | Type (editorial, retailer, brand,
forum, video) | Guide title | URL | Date last updated | Is [brand] included? |
Who is listed instead of us.

Then give me a hit list of the five guides where getting included or
corrected would matter most, with the ones that leave us out at the top. If
you couldn't open a page, mark it "not checked" rather than guessing what it
says.
```
