Keep AI away from the parts of marketing where a specific person's judgement is the product. Five jobs should stay human, from the line that defines your brand to the reply an upset customer gets. Once people have set the position, use AI to produce the work and spread it across every surface.

## Key takeaways

- Write the line that defines your brand yourself, then let AI carry it across every surface.
- People could not reliably tell AI-written self-presentations from human ones in six experiments with 4,600 participants, published in PNAS in 2023.
- People who disclosed using AI were trusted less across 13 experiments reported in 2025. Being exposed by someone else cost even more trust.
- A Canadian tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's wrong bereavement-fare advice on 14 February 2024, ruling the airline responsible for all the information on its website.
- Pricing pages and the thinking behind a repositioning stay human, because both need exact judgement the model averages away.

Most AI-marketing advice on the internet is sales copy for AI-marketing tools. This post makes the case for using AI less in five specific places, where reaching for the model actively hurts the brand.

## The line that defines your brand

The brand-defining piece, such as the headline that defines the company or its position statement, doesn't get drafted by AI. The model can produce something passable. You'll know it's passable. So will the next senior marketer who reads it.

The opening line is the one piece of writing that has to feel like a specific human with specific conviction wrote it. AI averages, while the brand-defining sentence is the opposite of an average. In a [2024 Science Advances study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/), stories written with ideas from a language model came out more similar to each other than stories by people alone. Write the line yourself, painfully, until it's right. After that AI can help you carry it across every surface, with a [brand voice profile](/lens/brand/brand-voice-extraction/) keeping the drafts on tone. The line itself stays human.

## Should AI write your CEO's LinkedIn?

If your CEO or founder has a real perspective worth distributing, don't have AI write their LinkedIn for them. The bandwidth gain is small and the downside is large.

Most readers won't spot it on sight. In six experiments with 4,600 participants, Maurice Jakesch and colleagues found people [could not reliably detect AI-written self-presentations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07271). Trust falls once readers know. Across 13 experiments, Oliver Schilke and Martin Reimann found that [people who disclosed AI use were trusted less](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v188y2025ics0749597825000172.html), with a bigger penalty for people exposed by someone else. When a post does read as AI, the tells are usually opinions that are slightly too balanced and a hedge in the wrong place. Once a senior voice loses its authenticity, you can't get it back without a long, public reset.

AI can help with research, or with repurposing a strong post into other formats. The original take stays in the senior person's own words. The [founder and institutional voice](/lens/brand/founder-and-institutional-voice/) playbook in The Lens maps which surfaces belong to which voice. Our post on [earned channels](/blog/earned-channels-pr-in-the-ai-era/) explains why senior opinion now carries so much of earned media.

## Can AI handle unhappy customers?

Automated support is fine for routine cases. The moment a customer is unhappy, they need a fast way out of the bot. When a customer realises a bot handled them in a moment of real distress, the loyalty cost dwarfs the labour saving, however polite the bot was.

Accuracy is the other risk. In [Moffatt v. Air Canada](https://decisions.civilresolutionbc.ca/crt/crtd/en/item/525448/index.do), decided on 14 February 2024, a customer used the airline's website chatbot while researching flights after their grandmother died. The chatbot said they could apply for a bereavement fare retroactively, which Air Canada's policy didn't allow. The British Columbia Civil Resolution Tribunal rejected Air Canada's argument that the chatbot was responsible for its own actions. It ordered the airline to pay C$812.02.

A workable rule is that AI handles category one (routine) and category two (informational) freely. Category three, the unhappy or complex case, gets a human in under two minutes or the brand pays for it later in churn and word of mouth.

## Pricing pages and high-trust copy

AI is great at high-volume, mid-trust content. It's distinctly worse at low-volume, high-trust copy such as the pricing page or legal-adjacent commercial claims.

Those pages have to be exact because every word gets examined. The model's habit of adding a softening clause or a slightly imprecise claim kills the credibility you're trying to build. The Air Canada tribunal put the stakes plainly, finding that the airline is responsible for all the information on its website, whether it comes from a static page or a chatbot.

Write those pages by hand. The volume is small and the stakes are high, so the AI productivity gain isn't worth the precision tax.

## Repositioning and the pivot

When the brand or the strategy is changing, such as a repositioning or a move into a new market, don't lean on AI to find the new direction by drafting the materials. The model will produce on-brand copy for the current brand because that's what its context contains. It's a regression machine, biased toward what's already there.

The pivot has to be designed by humans with conviction. AI is useful before the decision, for example in a [positioning audit](/lens/brand/positioning-audit-pipeline/) of what you say today, so the people setting the new position start from evidence. Once the new position is set, AI can produce the work to ship it everywhere, but the pivot itself is a thinking task for people.

## Where should AI sit in marketing?

Our view at Manual Focus is that AI belongs on the production layer and people belong on the brand-defining layer. The teams getting AI right have a clear map of where the model belongs and where it doesn't.

| Job | What AI does | What stays human |
|---|---|---|
| Brand-defining line | Carries it across surfaces | Writing the line |
| Senior people's posts | Research and repurposing | The take and the words |
| Customer support | Routine and informational cases | Unhappy or complex cases |
| Pricing and trust pages | Very little | Every word |
| Repositioning | Audit and roll-out | Choosing the new position |
