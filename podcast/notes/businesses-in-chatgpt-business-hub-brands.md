James and Hannah talk through Businesses in ChatGPT, which OpenAI launched in
beta on 10 November 2026. Business Hub lets any eligible business where ChatGPT
is available, UK brands included, claim its profile, correct its details and
see mentions, website clicks and discovery topics. Business Agents, for
eligible US ecommerce and online service businesses, let shoppers chat with a
brand inside ChatGPT and can be sponsored as the destination for ChatGPT Ads.
They cover what the metrics do and don't tell you, why the profile needs one
named owner, the rules agents have to follow, and three jobs to hand to AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for
your own details. All three need file uploads, and the second works best in an
assistant that can run on a weekly schedule.

### 1. Audit your ChatGPT profile against your own site

```text
I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell] at [website], selling
mainly in [market].

We have claimed our business profile in ChatGPT through OpenAI's Business Hub.
OpenAI's policy says profile information must be accurate and kept current,
and that a profile may also include information from other sources. I want to
find every place where the profile disagrees with our own website.

Attached are:
- the details shown in our Business Hub profile (name, category, address or
  service area, hours, contact details, products or services, links)
- our product feed export with prices and availability
- the text of our site's contact, opening hours, pricing and returns pages

Compare them field by field. Give me a table with the columns: Field | What
the profile or feed says | What our site says | Which is correct, if you can
tell | Source page.

Then list any links in the profile that point somewhere unexpected. If a field
is missing from either side, or you couldn't tell which version is correct,
mark it in the table rather than guessing.
```

### 2. Build a weekly ChatGPT visibility baseline

```text
I run marketing for [brand] at [website]. Each week I want one table that
tracks how our business shows up in AI assistants.

Each week I will attach:
- the Business Hub figures for the last 7 days (mentions, website clicks,
  profile views if shown, and the top discovery topics with their mentions)
- our analytics export of sessions referred from ChatGPT for the same 7 days
- the Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance export for the same period, if we
  have it

Add this week's figures to the running table I paste below, with the columns:
Week ending | Mentions | Website clicks | Clicks as % of mentions | ChatGPT
referral sessions | Sessions as % of clicks | Bing AI citations | Top three
discovery topics.

Under the table, write no more than five lines on what changed since last
week. Flag it if Business Hub clicks and ChatGPT referral sessions move in
different directions or differ by more than a third. If a figure is missing or
below the reporting threshold, mark it "not shown" rather than estimating it,
and tell me which comparisons you couldn't make.

[paste last week's table here]
```

### 3. Turn discovery topics into a content brief

```text
I run content for [brand] at [website], selling mainly in [market].

OpenAI's Business Hub shows our discovery topics, the common themes across
ChatGPT conversations where our website is cited. I want to know which of our
pages those topics rely on and where we have no strong page.

Attached are:
- our top discovery topics from Business Hub with their mention counts
- our sitemap or a list of our URLs with page titles

For each topic, find the one or two pages on our site most likely to be the
ones cited. Then give me a table with the columns: Discovery topic | Mentions |
Likely page | How well the page answers the topic (strong, partial, none) |
What the page is missing.

Rank the table by mentions. Finish with a list of the topics that have no
strong page, each with a suggested page title and the three questions it
should answer. Say where you are guessing which page is cited, since Business
Hub doesn't show the cited URL, and list anything you couldn't check.
```
