On 19 October 2026 Manual Focus launched Manual Focus Light Meter, the instrument behind its AI Shortlist research. It asks six AI engines the questions UK buyers ask and measures which brands they name, with a 95% range, and which sites they cite. Asked one question three times, an engine named the same brands only 20% of the time.

[See what Light Meter measures](/light-meter/), open the [indoor cycling report](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/), or, if your brand is in that study, [request your free AI Shortlist card](/contact/?offer=ai-shortlist-card).

## Key takeaways

- Manual Focus Light Meter measures six engines separately, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode, from a UK location.
- Every question runs three times per engine, because in the indoor cycling study an engine gave exactly the same brands across all three runs only 20% of the time.
- Every rate carries a 95% range, so Wahoo's 83% share of UK smart trainer answers reads as 79% to 87%.
- The engines split on challengers, with Van Rysel named in 43% of Claude's trainer answers and 5% of Google AI Mode's.
- Editorial sites supplied 51% to 56% of citations in indoor cycling, against 3% to 5% for Reddit, YouTube and forums.

## What is Manual Focus Light Meter?

We built Light Meter to answer one question for a brand team, whether your brand makes the shortlist when a UK buyer asks AI what to buy. We write 100 to 300 questions the way buyers ask them, tag each with the buying moment behind it, from value and beginner to performance and problem questions, then put every question to six engines from a UK location, three times each. The answers are collected through [DataForSEO](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/ai_optimization/overview/).

Light Meter records every brand named and every site cited, then works out each brand's shortlist rate, the share of answers that name it, with a 95% range. The first study, [which indoor cycling brands AI recommends in the UK](/blog/which-indoor-cycling-brands-does-ai-recommend/), used 309 questions and produced 5,562 answers. The full [interactive report](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/) is open to everyone.

## How is it different from rank tracking?

A rank tracker tells you where a page sits in a list of results for a keyword. In an AI answer there's no list to sit in, only a short set of brands the engine chose to name and the sources it leaned on. We argued in June that [the citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/), and Light Meter is our attempt to measure where it went.

| Question | Rank tracker | Manual Focus Light Meter |
|---|---|---|
| What it counts | Your page's position for a keyword | Whether an AI answer names your brand |
| Unit of measure | One position per keyword | The share of answers that name you |
| Repeat runs | One reading per keyword per check | Three runs of every question per engine |
| Engines | One search engine at a time | Six AI engines, reported separately |
| Uncertainty | None shown | A 95% range on every rate |
| Questions | Keywords you choose | Unbranded buyer questions, tagged by buying moment |
| Sources | The ranking URL | Every site the engine cites, by type |

## Why ask the same question three times?

Because AI engines don't give the same answer twice. In the indoor cycling study, an engine named exactly the same set of brands across all three runs of a question only 20% of the time.

| Engine | Same brands in all three runs |
|---|---|
| Perplexity | 48% |
| Claude | 20% |
| ChatGPT | 13% |
| Google AI Overviews | 12% |
| Gemini | 11% |
| Google AI Mode | 11% |

So one check of what ChatGPT says about your category is one draw from a moving set. A [SparkToro study reported by Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/) in January 2026 gave brands the same advice, to track how often they appear, not where.

## Why measure each engine separately?

At the top of a category the engines agree. All six put Wahoo first for smart trainers and Zwift first for training apps. Below the leaders they behave like different shops with different buyers. Van Rysel, Decathlon's own brand, appeared in 43% of Claude's trainer answers and 5% of Google AI Mode's. JetBlack ranged from 47% on Claude to 11% on AI Mode, and Saris showed up in 40% of Claude's trainer answers and under 10% of every other engine's.

Part of that gap is how each engine answers. Claude named 4.2 brands per answer on average in the study, against 2.9 to 3.3 for the other engines. Google AI Overviews appeared on only 64% of the searches, which pulled down its rate for most leading brands.

Our view at Manual Focus is that one blended "AI visibility" score hides the most useful finding for a challenger brand, which is the engine where it's already close and the one where it's missing.

## Why does every number carry a range?

A shortlist rate is an estimate from a sample of answers, and Light Meter shows its margin as a 95% range, which tells you whether a gap between two brands is real or could be noise from the sample.

In indoor cycling, Wahoo's smart trainer rate of 83% has a range of 79% to 87%, well clear of Tacx at 65%. Rouvy at 71% and MyWhoosh at 69% are a different story. Their ranges overlap, and the report gives both a rank range of second to third, so calling either one second would claim more than the data shows. Wahoo's 64% against Wattbike's 58% for smart bikes is closer than it looks, because those two ranges overlap as well. A brand team that knows this saves itself from spending a quarter's budget closing a gap that may not exist.

## Why track the sites AI cites?

The sources tell you where to do the work. In indoor cycling, editorial sites supplied 56% of citations for trainers, 53% for smart bikes and 51% for apps, led by BikeRadar, Cyclingnews and Cycling Weekly. Reddit, YouTube and forums supplied 3% to 5%. Brand websites mattered most for apps, at 30% of citations.

For most brands in that category the job is earned media, getting into the buyer's guides the engines quote, more than rewriting their own pages. ## Where should a brand team start?

1. Write down the ten unbranded questions your buyers ask most, and tag each with its buying moment. The [category entry points playbook](/lens/demand/category-entry-points/) is the method.
2. Put those questions to at least three engines, three times each, and count how often you're named on each one before you draw any conclusion.
3. List the sites the engines cite for your category, then check whether you're in them and whether the details are current.
4. Set a date three months out to run the same questions again, so you can tell whether anything you changed moved the rate.

## What Light Meter can't tell you

Light Meter counts whether an engine names a brand, not whether it praises it, and not whether anyone buys. Logged-in users with chat history may see different answers. Perplexity and Claude are measured through their APIs, which can differ from their apps. Each study is a snapshot of the month it ran, so a category needs repeat editions before it shows a trend.
