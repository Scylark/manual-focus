James and Hannah talk through OpenAI's ChatGPT Ads app for Shopify, which
began reaching merchants outside the US on 23 September 2026. The UK already
has self-serve access, so a Shopify brand can advertise in ChatGPT without an
agency or a new product feed. They cover who sees the ads (only Free and Go
users), why paid and organic stay separate efforts, why the catalogue now does
the job of ad creative, how Manual Focus would run a small test before Black
Friday, and three jobs to hand to AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. All three need file
uploads, and the third needs an assistant that can run on a weekly schedule.

### 1. Tidy the catalogue for your test category

```text
I run ecommerce marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell], on
Shopify, selling mainly in the UK.

We're about to test ChatGPT Ads through the Shopify app. The ads draw on our
Shopify catalogue, so product titles, descriptions, images and prices do the
job ad creative used to do. We're testing one category: [category], where
shoppers ask detailed questions before buying, such as [sizing /
compatibility / use cases].

Attached is a product export for that category with titles, descriptions,
image alt text and prices.

For each product, check whether:
- the title says plainly what the product is
- the description answers the questions shoppers ask in this category
- titles and descriptions are consistent across similar products
- image alt text describes the product

Give me a table with the columns: Product | Problem | Suggested new title |
Suggested new description (under 80 words).

Don't add specifications, sizes or claims that aren't in the export; write
"needs input from [team]" where a fact is missing. List any products or
columns you couldn't read.
```

### 2. Review the automatic ad copy

```text
We're testing OpenAI's optional setting that rewrites our ChatGPT Ads
headlines and descriptions to fit each conversation and translates them into
the user's language. I want to check what it writes in our name before we
let it run more widely.

Attached are:
- the rewritten headlines and descriptions from our test ad group
- our original ad copy
- our brand guidelines: [file name]

Go through the rewritten copy line by line and flag anything that:
1. changes or adds a product claim compared with our original copy
2. drifts from the tone in our brand guidelines
3. looks mistranslated or unnatural in the target language

Give me a table with the columns: Original line | Rewritten line | Issue
(claim / tone / translation / none) | Why | Suggested fix.

Finish with a one-paragraph summary of whether the rewrites are safe to keep
running. If you can't judge a translation with confidence, say which language
and leave that row for a native speaker to check.
```

### 3. Compare ChatGPT Ads with Google Shopping every week

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every Monday morning.

We're testing ChatGPT Ads for [brand] in the UK against our Google Shopping
campaigns for the same products in [category]. Every click is tagged.

Each week I'll attach:
- a ChatGPT Ads results export for the past week
- a Google Shopping results export for the same products and dates
- our orders export with a returning-customer flag

For each channel, work out cost per order and returning-customer rate, by
product and in total.

Give me a table with the columns: Product | ChatGPT Ads cost per order |
Google Shopping cost per order | ChatGPT Ads returning-customer rate | Google
Shopping returning-customer rate | Change since last week.

Under the table, list what moved most since the previous week in three lines
or fewer. If an order can't be matched to a channel, or a product appears in
one export and not the other, list it separately rather than guessing.
```
