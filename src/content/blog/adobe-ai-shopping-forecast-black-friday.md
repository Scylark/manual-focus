AI assistants will send 130% more shoppers to US retail sites this holiday season than last, according to a forecast Adobe published on 28 September 2026. The figure counts clicks from AI chat and browser services. UK ecommerce teams should measure the channel properly before Black Friday on 27 November.

## Key takeaways

- Adobe forecast on 28 September 2026 that AI traffic to US retail sites will rise 130% year on year between 1 November and 31 December, counted as clicks on a link.
- It expects the biggest rise on Thanksgiving, up 159%. Black Friday should be up 95% and Cyber Monday up 88%.
- Adobe expects US online holiday spending to reach $275.1 billion, up 6.7% on 2025.
- In Adobe's July survey of 5,000 consumers, 77% of people who had shopped with AI said it made them more confident in a purchase.
- The forecast covers the US only and gives growth rates, not AI's share of retail traffic.

**Update, 11 November 2026.** Adobe published [new figures on 29 October](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-higher-revenue-per-visit-retail-holiday) that strengthen the case for targeting conversion. In September 2026 AI traffic to US retail sites grew 71% year on year. It converted 90% better than non-AI traffic, against 9% worse in August 2025. AI visits also earned 83% more revenue per visit. Adobe now forecasts that this advantage will double over the holiday season. Its results for Black Friday itself are not out yet.

## What did Adobe forecast?

Adobe's [holiday forecast](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-us-holiday-shopping-season-to-hit-record) expects US online spending to reach $275.1 billion between 1 November and 31 December, up 6.7% on 2025. The AI figures sit inside that. Adobe defines AI traffic as shoppers clicking a link from an AI chat or browser service to a retail site. It expects the steepest rise on Thanksgiving.

| Date in 2026 | Forecast rise in AI traffic | Relevance in the UK |
|---|---|---|
| Thanksgiving, 26 November | 159% | US only |
| Black Friday, 27 November | 95% | Major UK trading day |
| Cyber Monday, 30 November | 88% | Major UK trading day |
| 1 November to 31 December | 130% | Benchmark for the season |

The forecast draws on more than 1 trillion visits to US retail sites and 100 million SKUs across 18 product categories, backed by a survey of 5,000 consumers in July. Among people who had already shopped with AI, 77% said it made them more confident in a purchase and 69% said they were less likely to send an item back. Adobe says it first saw shoppers using AI chat and browser services for online shopping in the 2024 holiday season.

Vivek Pandya, Adobe's director of digital insights, said consumers are "getting much more comfortable having dialogues with an agent, getting recommendations and going to the retail site from those conversations", [as the Spokesman-Review reported](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/sep/28/ai-assisted-shopping-to-rise-130-during-holidays-a/). He said the use now reaches "beyond big-ticket items to everyday essentials".

## Does a US forecast apply to the UK?

Adobe's data is American and Thanksgiving means little to UK shoppers, but Black Friday and Cyber Monday are fixed points in the UK retail calendar. The assistants driving the US numbers, such as ChatGPT and Gemini, are the same products British shoppers use. A UK brand that can't see its own AI referrals today will go into the busiest trading weeks of the year without a baseline.

US shoppers are handing product research to these assistants. When a shopper asks an assistant to compare three running jackets under £150, the assistant reads product pages and reviews, then sends a click to whichever page answered best. Pages with thin descriptions or sizing hidden in images give the assistant less to quote. Your product catalogue now does part of the job a comparison site or a paid search ad used to do. Smaller brands start behind, because a [Lightspeed study](/blog/ai-shopping-assistants-favour-big-retailers/) found assistants answering from their training data picked national chains over smaller rivals up to 94% of the time.

Our view at Manual Focus is that UK retail and DTC brands should treat AI referrals as a named channel in their peak trading report this year, with its own baseline and targets, even while the numbers are small. We'd set the targets on conversion and returns rather than volume, because Adobe's survey points at a shopper who arrives further along in the decision. Paid placement in these assistants is arriving too, which we covered in [what ChatGPT Ads on Shopify means for UK ecommerce brands](/blog/chatgpt-ads-shopify-uk-ecommerce/). The [first returns study on ChatGPT ads](/blog/chatgpt-ads-returns-vs-google-search-study/) suggests a paid visit there earns less than one from paid Google Search for now.

## What to finish before Black Friday

1. **Build an AI referral channel in analytics this week.** Group traffic from AI assistant domains such as chatgpt.com and perplexity.ai into one channel, then pull September and October as your pre-peak baseline.
2. **Compare it with paid search and organic.** Put conversion and return rates for AI referrals next to your other channels. The [attribution tear-down](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) playbook covers how to avoid double counting when the same shopper also clicks an ad.
3. **Fix the pages assistants would quote.** Take your 20 best-selling Black Friday products and make sure every fact a shopper asks about, from sizing to stock, appears in plain text on the page. Adobe's October report found that the average US retail homepage scored 63% on its Citation Readability Score, so more than a third of a typical homepage was not visible to AI systems.
4. **Check nothing blocks the assistants.** Ask whoever runs your CDN and robots.txt to confirm that bot-protection rules haven't shut out the AI search crawlers that fetch your product pages. Our post on the [shared AI training opt-out](/blog/shared-ai-training-opt-out-robots-txt/) sets out which robots.txt rules affect AI training and which affect AI search.

## Gaps in the forecast

Adobe's release gives growth rates but not the share of all retail visits that AI referrals make up, so a 130% rise could still be a small slice of traffic. Its October update doesn't give that share either. The count covers clicks only, which misses every purchase an AI answer shaped without sending a visit. The survey is self-reported and limited to people already shopping with AI. And the forecast covers only the US, so British brands have to build the evidence from their own data.
