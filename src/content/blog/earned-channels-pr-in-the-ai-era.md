Earned media still works in the AI era, though the generic pitch has stopped working. In Muck Rack's 2026 survey, 82% of journalists used AI tools and 88% said they immediately disregard pitches that miss their beat. Coverage and AI citations now go to brands with sharp senior opinions and original data.

## Key takeaways

- 82% of journalists had used at least one AI tool in their work in the past year, up from 77%, in Muck Rack's State of Journalism 2026 survey of 897 journalists.
- 88% of journalists in the same survey said they immediately disregard pitches that miss their coverage area, ahead of overly promotional pitches at 71%.
- 40% of journalists want original data or research with a pitch, against 14% who want pre-written quotes.
- 76% of PR professionals use generative AI, mostly for editing and writing, in Muck Rack's State of AI in PR report from January 2026.
- Earned media now compounds through senior people's own opinions and original data, which journalists and AI answers both reuse.

The take that PR is dead is wrong. So is the take that PR works the way it did in 2020.

Journalists use AI now. In Muck Rack's [State of Journalism 2026 survey](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/State_of_Journalism_2026_1.pdf), 82% had used at least one AI tool in their work over the past 12 months. Google says Discover [uses many of the same signals and systems as Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover) to pick stories. The earned media surface is still there, running at machine speed underneath. Brands that understand that win it.

This is the second of three posts on how AI changes each channel, after [owned channels](/blog/owned-channels-website-to-platform/) and before [paid channels](/blog/paid-channels-bidding-agents/). For the definitions, start with our [basics guide to the three media types](/blog/owned-paid-earned-media-basics/).

## What has changed in earned media?

**Pitch volume hit a wall.** Three in four PR professionals now use generative AI, most of them for writing, according to Muck Rack's [State of AI in PR](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/State_of_AI_in_PR_2026.pdf) survey of 564 PR professionals in December 2025. Journalists' inboxes fill with polished pitches that miss the point. The State of Journalism survey found 88% of journalists immediately disregard pitches that miss their beat, so generic outreach is functionally dead. Half are put off by anything that looks like a mass email. [PR 101](/blog/pr-101-basics/) covers how a pitch that works is put together. If your PR team or agency pitches journalists, the [earned-media pitch generator](/lens/content/earned-media-pitch-generator/) playbook in The Lens matches each story to the journalists who cover that beat.

**Quotes get found online.** Journalists can find usable quotes on the web without waiting for a reply. If your senior people publish sharp opinions in long-form, those quotes get picked up. If they don't, you're invisible. Only 14% of journalists in the Muck Rack survey wanted pre-written quotes with a pitch, so a quote written for a press release does little on its own.

**LLM-cited matters more than journalist-cited.** Being named in a chat answer when someone asks a model about your category is the new version of being in the trade press. The mechanism is different, because answer engine optimisation matters here, but the value is similar. You get third-party validation and a lift in brand search. Google rankings no longer predict who gets cited, as [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/) sets out. [Light Meter](/blog/manual-focus-light-meter-ai-visibility/) is how Manual Focus measures which brands AI engines recommend.

**Social platforms are aggregating taste signals.** Algorithmic feeds use AI to predict what to surface, so branded content that doesn't earn engagement gets buried faster. The bar for "good enough" has risen.

## Where does earned media compound now?

Three places are working harder than they used to.

**Founder and operator voice.** Senior people publishing real opinions in their own name, on LinkedIn or in podcasts, generate the citations and pull-quotes that get reused everywhere else. It works when the perspective is specific and hard-won, because generic posts don't pull. LinkedIn is also the platform journalists trust most, with 58% rating it trustworthy in the Muck Rack survey. Senior social posts are one of the five places we say [AI shouldn't touch](/blog/when-not-to-use-ai-in-marketing/). The [founder and institutional voice](/lens/brand/founder-and-institutional-voice/) playbook sets out who speaks for the brand where.

**Original data.** Publish your own survey results or benchmarks. Journalists need the data and LLMs need the source, so both link back. In the Muck Rack survey, 40% of journalists said a pitch should come with original data or research. If you can publish something novel every quarter, you out-earn every press release.

**Partnerships with edge.** A partnership where two brands make something new together earns coverage because it's a real story. A routine co-marketing swap rarely does.

## Which earned tactics still work?

Each tactic below is rated on the Muck Rack evidence and on how AI answers reuse it.

| Tactic | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Senior opinion in their own name | Compounding | Journalists and AI answers reuse it |
| Original data or research | Compounding | 40% of journalists want it with a pitch |
| Partnership with a real story | Working | It gives a journalist news |
| Pitch matched to the beat | Working | 86% of journalists say pitches start some stories |
| Mass pitch | Deleted | 88% disregard pitches off their beat |
| Press release with no news | Ignored | There is nothing to report |

Press releases issued for the sake of it stop working, as do "X best of Y" round-ups built for links. So do generic op-eds drafted by an AI and edited by another AI.

## How should AI fit into PR work?

Earned channels still work, because what earns coverage hasn't changed. Real opinions and real data still win, distributed at speed. AI helps with the work around them, such as research and monitoring, without diluting the substance. The production layer is broken down in [five practical ways to improve content production with AI](/blog/five-ways-improve-content-production-ai/).

Our view at Manual Focus is that the brands losing earned media have automated the wrong layer. They let AI write the substance and kept the workflow human, which is backwards.
