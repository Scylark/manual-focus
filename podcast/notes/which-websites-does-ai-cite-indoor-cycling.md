James and Hannah go through the second cut of the Manual Focus AI Shortlist
study of UK indoor cycling: the websites six AI engines cited in 4,379 answers.
One BikeRadar buyer's guide was cited in 55% of smart trainer answers, but each
engine leans on different sources. Google AI Overviews cited YouTube in 65% of
its answers while Claude never did, and ChatGPT cited brand websites more than
any other engine. They cover Rouvy's own blog, where retailers come in, and
three jobs to hand to AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for
your own details. The first two need file uploads or pasted text. The third
needs an assistant that can browse the web and run on a schedule.

### 1. Audit the buyer's guides AI cites

```text
I run marketing for [brand], which sells [product category] in [market].

AI engines lean heavily on a small number of buyer's guides when they answer
shopping questions in our category. I want to know whether our products are in
the guides that matter, and whether what those guides say about us is current.

Attached are:
- a list of the buyer's guide URLs AI engines cite most for our category
- our current product range, with model names, launch dates and UK prices

Open each guide and check it against our range.

Give me a table with the columns: Guide | Publisher | Last updated (as shown on
the page) | Our product included (yes or no) | Model named | Price shown |
What's out of date or missing.

Then list the three guides where getting in, or getting updated, would matter
most, with one line on why for each.

If a page won't open, or doesn't show a date or price, say so in the table
rather than guessing.
```

### 2. Draft a fair comparison page

```text
I run marketing for [brand], which makes [product]. I want to publish a
comparison page on our site that a buyer, or an AI engine, would trust as much
as an independent buyer's guide.

Attached are:
- our spec sheet and UK prices for [product]
- the public product pages or spec sheets for [rival 1], [rival 2] and
  [rival 3]

Draft a comparison page of 800 to 1,200 words for the question "[the buyer
question you want to answer]". Include:
- a short answer at the top, saying which product suits which kind of buyer
- a comparison table with the columns: Product | Price | [three specs that
  matter to buyers in this category]
- one section per product, including ours, with its genuine strengths and
  weaknesses

Be fair to the rivals. Where a rival beats us on something, say so. Use only
facts from the attached files, and mark any spec you couldn't confirm as
"to check" rather than filling it in.
```

### 3. Run a monthly engine check

```text
Each month, run this check for [brand] in [market]. This needs web access and
a schedule.

Here are our ten most important buyer questions:
[question 1]
[question 2]
...
[question 10]

For each question, ask it as a UK shopper would, in a fresh conversation with
no brand named, in each AI engine you can reach.

Give me a table with the columns: Question | Engine | Brands named, in order |
Whether [brand] was named | Websites cited.

Then give me:
- how many answers named [brand], by engine
- the five websites cited most often, and whether each one mentions [brand]
- what changed since last month's table, if I paste it in

Say which engines you couldn't reach or which answers showed no sources,
rather than leaving gaps unexplained.
```
