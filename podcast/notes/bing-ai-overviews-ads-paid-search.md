James and Hannah talk through Microsoft Advertising's confirmation on 19
November 2026 that ads now appear in Bing's AI Overviews in every market except
China, the UK included, through existing Performance Max and AI Max campaigns
with no new set-up. They compare it with Google's plans for ads in AI Overviews
and AI Mode, explain why under-watched Microsoft accounts carry the most risk
over Black Friday, and hand three jobs to AI, an account inventory, a baseline
and a draft negative keyword list.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for
your own details. All three need file uploads, and the second works best in an
assistant that can run on a schedule.

### 1. Inventory the campaigns that can serve in AI Overviews

```text
I run paid search for [brand], [one line on what you sell], advertising in
[market] on Microsoft Advertising.

Microsoft Advertising confirmed on 19 November 2026 that ads can now appear in
Bing's AI Overviews through existing campaigns, including Performance Max and
AI Max, with no separate set-up. I want to know which of our campaigns are
exposed.

Attached is a campaign export from Microsoft Advertising with campaign name,
campaign type, status, created date, last edited date, AI Max setting (if
shown) and spend for the last 30 days.

Give me a table with the columns: Campaign | Type | Uses AI Max (yes/no/not
shown) | Spend last 30 days | Last edited | Flag.

In Flag, mark "eligible" for Performance Max or AI Max campaigns, and "not
reviewed" for any campaign not edited in the last 90 days. Rank by spend.
Then list, in two lines each, the five campaigns I should review first and
why. If a column is missing from the export, say so instead of guessing.
```

### 2. Build a weekly baseline before Black Friday

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every Monday morning until the end of
January.

I run paid search for [brand] on Microsoft Advertising in [market]. Ads
started appearing in Bing's AI Overviews around November 2026, and our
reports don't separate that placement out. I want a baseline so I can spot a
shift.

Attached are:
- the search terms report for the last 8 weeks, with impressions, clicks,
  cost and conversions
- the campaign performance report for the same weeks

Each week:
1. Band every search term by length (1 to 3 words, 4 to 6, 7 or more) and by
   whether it is phrased as a question.
2. Give me a table with the columns: Week | Campaign | Impressions | Clicks |
   Avg CPC | Conversion rate | Share of spend on 7+ word terms | Share of
   spend on question terms.
3. Compare the latest week with the 8-week average and list any campaign
   where CPC, conversion rate or the share of long or question terms moved
   by more than 15%.

Don't attribute any change to AI Overviews, since the data can't show that.
Say plainly where the data is incomplete.
```

### 3. Draft negatives for question-style searches

```text
I run paid search for [brand], [one line on what you sell], on Microsoft
Advertising in [market].

Attached is a search terms report for the last 90 days with cost, clicks and
conversions, plus our current negative keyword list.

Find search terms that are informational or comparison questions (for
example "what is", "how to", "vs", "best", "is it worth") and that spent
money without converting.

Give me a table with the columns: Search term | Cost | Clicks | Conversions |
Suggested negative | Match type | Reason.

Group the suggestions by theme, and leave out anything already covered by
our negative list. Mark any term that looks like an early buyer question
(close to our products or pricing) as "review", not "add", so a person
decides. If the report doesn't cover a column, say so.
```

### And one for you

Add an analytics annotation dated 19 November 2026 for Microsoft Advertising,
so any change in Bing results over peak can be checked against the date the
placement was confirmed.
