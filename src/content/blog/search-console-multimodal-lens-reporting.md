Google added a multimodal filter to Search Console on 24 September 2026, so site owners can see which of their pages appear when people search with a photo through Lens, Circle to Search, an image upload or Chrome's Search this image option. For product and retail brands, camera-led discovery becomes a channel you can measure by page.

## Google started counting searches that begin with a photo

Google [announced the reporting](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc) on its Search Central blog, with the rollout starting globally the same day. In the performance report the Web search type now splits into "Text-based" and "Multimodal", and the same filter appears in the report for generative AI features, which Google [launched in June](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) and extended to every site worldwide by 31 August. That report covers AI Overviews, AI Mode and generative AI features in Discover.

The new data covers four entry points, which are Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android, image uploads to Google Search and the right-click Search this image option in Chrome. The filter gives you pages, countries, devices and dates to export, but no queries, because Google says these searches mostly use images rather than text.

The data is new as well as newly visible. When an SEO asked whether multimodal impressions had been grouped under Web all along, Google's John Mueller checked with the Search Console team and replied that "the data wasn't previously in the counts, the report has new data", [as Search Engine Roundtable reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-console-multimodal-search-type-filter-42156.html).

## Who gains most from camera-led search

Someone points a phone at a pair of trainers on a stranger's feet, a bottle on a shelf, or a jacket in a shop window, and Google decides which page answers. Before 24 September a brand had no direct way to see whether that page was its own product page, a retailer's listing or a review site. The new filter answers that question at page level for every site that gets this traffic.

Physical products with a recognisable look gain the most, meaning fashion, footwear, cycling and outdoor kit, beauty, drinks and homeware. Premium brands whose products get photographed in the wild should look first, which is the audience we wrote the [SEO playbook for premium endurance brands](/blog/seo-playbook-premium-endurance-brands/) for. B2B services and software will see little here.

Our view at Manual Focus is that the multimodal filter matters more for what it shows about where Google sends camera traffic than for its volume, and a brand that finds its product photos sending shoppers to a reseller's page has a merchandising problem it can now put a number on. We'd also treat it as another sign that visibility in Google is splitting into streams that need separate reporting, as AI citations already have, which we covered in [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/).

## Five checks for the next SEO report

1. **Rebase your comparisons.** Apply the text-based filter whenever you compare September and October with earlier months, so the new multimodal counts don't read as a ranking gain.
2. **Export the multimodal pages.** Pull the page list for the last 28 days and sort by impressions, then note which are product pages, which are category pages and which are articles or image-heavy guides.
3. **Look for the wrong landing page.** If Lens sends people to a blog image or an old product rather than the current product page, fix the image and page pairing on your own site first, and then ask retail partners how their listings show up.
4. **Split it by country and device.** Circle to Search runs on Android, so a mobile-heavy market with high multimodal impressions tells you where camera search is already a habit for your customers.
5. **Check the generative AI view too.** Apply the multimodal filter in the generative AI features report and see whether AI Overviews or AI Mode show your pages for image searches. Record what you find so you have a baseline to measure against.

## Gaps in the new data

Without query data you can see which page appeared but not what the person photographed, so you infer intent from the page itself. The generative AI report still shows impressions without clicks, which leaves you unable to tell whether an AI answer for an image search sent anyone to your site. Google says the metrics appear only if your site receives this traffic, and a staged global rollout means some properties may see the filter days after others. Google hasn't said how multimodal searches that turn into text follow-up questions are counted, or whether it will add query-style descriptions later.
