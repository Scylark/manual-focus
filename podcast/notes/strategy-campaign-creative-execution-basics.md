title: "Strategy vs campaign vs creative vs execution, explained"
---

A Manual Focus Basics episode. James and Hannah separate four words that
marketing teams use interchangeably: strategy, the choice of who you serve and
why they should pick you; a campaign, a time-bound push that carries part of
the strategy; creative, the idea the audience meets; and execution, the
delivery. Hannah explains three of Seth Godin's ideas from first principles,
the smallest viable audience, "people like us do things like this" and Purple
Cow, and they bring in Binet and Field's IPA work on short-term metrics. They
finish with how to walk back up the chain when a result disappoints.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. All three work with
pasted text or uploaded files.

### 1. Check every campaign traces back to the strategy

```text
I lead marketing at [company]. Our marketing strategy, in two or three
sentences, is: [paste strategy, including the audience, the change we want and
why they should choose us].

Attached are this quarter's campaign briefs.

Give me a table with the columns: Campaign | Its stated objective | The line
of the strategy it serves (quote it) | Audience as written in the brief | Is
the audience specific enough to brief creative against? (yes/no, and why).

Then list any campaign that doesn't connect to a line of the strategy, and
any line of the strategy that no campaign serves. Quote the briefs' own
words. If a brief is missing an objective or audience, say so rather than
inferring one.
```

### 2. Sort what went wrong after a disappointing campaign

```text
Our campaign [name] didn't hit its objective of [objective]. I want to know
whether the problem was execution, creative, the campaign itself or the
strategy behind it, before we decide what to change.

Attached are:
- the campaign plan and brief
- the delivery log, showing what went live, where and when, against the plan
- the results, by channel and by week

Work through it in this order: execution (was it on time, on brief and
error-free?), creative (did the idea get noticed and understood, where the
data shows it?), campaign (were the objective, budget and timing right?), and
strategy (was the audience and reason to choose right?).

For each, give a verdict (likely cause / possible cause / not the cause) and
the evidence from the files. Where the data is too thin to judge, say "can't
tell from this data" and name the data that would settle it.
```

### 3. Draft tighter audience choices

```text
Our current target audience is defined as: [paste definition].

Attached is our customer research: [surveys, interviews, reviews or CRM
segments].

Seth Godin argues for choosing a smallest viable audience, the smallest group
you could serve so well that they recommend you, and for understanding
audiences by the group they see themselves in ("people like us do things like
this").

Using only what the research shows, draft three tighter audience choices. For
each give: who they are in one sentence | the group they see themselves as
part of | what people in that group are expected to do, buy or know | why
they might choose us | the evidence in the research.

Don't invent customer quotes or numbers. If the research doesn't support a
tighter audience, say so and suggest what research would.
```
