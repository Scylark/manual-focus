On 23 September 2026 the Competition and Markets Authority proposed that Google offer UK Android and Chrome users a choice of search service at set-up and every year after, with AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Perplexity eligible. For marketers, it puts AI citations on a par with Google rankings for part of the UK audience.

## What happened

The CMA published [strengthened proposals](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-strengthens-proposals-allowing-people-choice-over-their-search-service) for its user choice conduct requirement, one of the rules it can impose on Google since designating it with strategic market status in general search in October 2025. Under the revised plan, Google would have to:

- show a search choice screen when someone sets up an Android phone or opens Chrome for the first time, followed by a prompt every year to choose their default
- let AI assistants onto those screens where they meet the eligibility criteria, including technical and security requirements
- require every provider on the screen to attribute publisher content clearly, so people can reach the source and see where an answer came from

The consultation document fills in the detail. Chrome is covered on desktop, mobile and tablet, including Windows and iOS, wherever Google Search is the default. Providers are listed in a fully random order and users must scroll the whole list before choosing. If more than twelve qualify, each screen shows the five most popular plus seven picked at random. To qualify, an assistant must handle the full range of general search tasks, search the web for current information, and link clearly to the content behind its answers.

CMA chief executive Sarah Cardell said the regulator is responding to AI assistants "which people are increasingly using as their search service, but aren't included on Google's choice screens." Google had argued against annual prompts, [according to The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/cma-google-choice-screens-ai-assistants), proposing a permanent setting instead. The consultation closes on 9 October and the CMA expects a final decision by the end of the year.

## What it means for marketing teams

Defaults decide where most searches start, and for years Google has been the default everywhere that matters in the UK. This proposal leaves Google in place but re-opens the decision once a year on every covered phone and browser, with assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity potentially on the list.

Don't expect a stampede. The CMA's own [user research](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab2903ba9c3d267bccced0e/user_research_on_choice_screens_in_search.pdf), 80 user tests run by Thinks Insight & Strategy, found people rushed the screens, skimmed the information and mostly picked the service they already used. AI assistants did make people slow down and look, and some chose one. That's the pattern to plan for: slow, compounding drift, concentrated among early adopters.

The detail that matters most for marketers is attribution. An assistant only earns a place on the screen if it credits and links to its sources. So for UK users who switch, being the page an assistant quotes becomes a direct route to traffic as well as a brand mention. We wrote about how far citations and rankings have already diverged in [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/). This proposal would give that gap a regulatory push.

Our view at Manual Focus is that this won't shift UK search share much in 2027, but it will reward the brands that are already easy for AI assistants to quote, and it makes the cited link the thing worth competing for. Paid search plans that assume Google is where every UK search begins should start carrying that assumption as a risk.

## What to do this week

1. **Find out where you're cited now.** Put your 20 most commercially important buyer questions into ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Claude. Record who gets cited and linked. That's your baseline.
2. **Measure AI referrals.** Segment traffic from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and claude.ai in your analytics, so you can see the drift when it comes.
3. **Check the assistants can read your site.** Review robots.txt and your CDN's bot rules for AI crawlers, and make sure key pages render as plain HTML.
4. **Rewrite your top pages answer-first.** A direct answer in the opening paragraph, specific numbers, and sources a model can check. The [SEO cluster generator](/lens/content/seo-cluster-generator/) playbook shows how to structure this at scale.
5. **Respond to the consultation if it affects you.** Publishers and brands with a view on attribution can reply to searchsms@cma.gov.uk before 5pm on 9 October 2026.

## What we don't know yet

The proposal can still change before the final decision, and there's no implementation date yet. We don't know which assistants will pass the eligibility tests, which Google applies every six months under the CMA's rules. The user research was qualitative, so it shows how people behave on the screens, not how many will switch. And annual prompts will be tied to major Chrome and Android updates, so the timing will vary by device.
