## What is Manual Focus Light Meter?

Light Meter is the tool we built to show a brand team whether it makes the shortlist when a UK buyer asks AI what to buy. It is also the instrument behind the Manual Focus AI Shortlist research.

For each study we write 100 to 300 questions the way UK buyers ask them. Each one is tagged with the buying moment behind it, such as value or performance. Light Meter puts every question to six engines from a UK location, three times each.

- ChatGPT
- Gemini
- Claude
- Perplexity
- Google AI Overviews
- Google AI Mode

The answers are collected through [DataForSEO](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/ai_optimization/overview/). Light Meter records every brand named and every site cited. From those it works out each brand's shortlist rate, the share of answers that name it.

## What does Light Meter measure?

A rank tracker tells you where a page sits in a list of results for a keyword. An AI answer has no list to sit in, only the brands the engine chose to name and the sources it leaned on. We argued in June that [the citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/). Light Meter measures where it went.

| Question | Rank tracker | Manual Focus Light Meter |
|---|---|---|
| What it counts | Your page's position for a keyword | Whether an AI answer names your brand |
| Unit of measure | One position per keyword | The share of answers that name you |
| Repeat runs | One reading per keyword per check | Three runs of every question per engine |
| Engines | One search engine at a time | Six AI engines, reported separately |
| Uncertainty | None shown | A 95% range on every rate |
| Questions | Keywords you choose | Unbranded buyer questions, tagged by buying moment |
| Sources | The ranking URL | Every site the engine cites, by type |

## Example study, indoor cycling

The first Light Meter study shows what a brand gets. It covered indoor cycling kit, from smart trainers to training apps. Light Meter ran 309 UK questions and collected 5,562 answers. The [interactive report](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/) has every brand and question. The [article on the findings](/blog/which-indoor-cycling-brands-does-ai-recommend/) walks through them.

Wahoo was named in 83% of smart trainer answers. Zwift was named in 88% of training app answers. Cycling media supplied over half the citations. The sections below show the four things the study measured.

## Why ask each question three times?

AI engines don't give the same answer twice. In the indoor cycling study, an engine named exactly the same set of brands across all three runs of a question only 20% of the time.

| Engine | Same brands in all three runs |
|---|---|
| Perplexity | 48% |
| Claude | 20% |
| ChatGPT | 13% |
| Google AI Overviews | 12% |
| Gemini | 11% |
| Google AI Mode | 11% |

So one check of what ChatGPT says about your category is one draw from a moving set. A [SparkToro study reported by Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/) in January 2026 gave brands the same advice, to track how often they appear, not where.

## Why measure six engines separately?

At the top the engines agree. All six put Wahoo first for smart trainers and Zwift first for training apps. Below the leaders they split. Van Rysel, Decathlon's own brand, appeared in 43% of Claude's trainer answers but 5% of Google AI Mode's. JetBlack ranged from 47% on Claude to 11% on AI Mode.

Part of the gap is how each engine answers. Claude named 4.2 brands per answer on average, against 2.9 to 3.3 for the other engines. Google AI Overviews appeared on only 64% of the searches.

Our view at Manual Focus is that one blended "AI visibility" score hides the most useful finding for a challenger brand, which is the engine where it's already close and the one where it's missing.

## Why every number has a range

A shortlist rate is an estimate from a sample of answers. Light Meter shows its margin as a 95% range. Wahoo's smart trainer rate of 83% has a range of 79% to 87%, well clear of Tacx at 65%.

Rouvy at 71% and MyWhoosh at 69% overlap. The report gives both a rank range of second to third, so calling either one second would claim more than the data shows. A brand team that knows this won't spend a quarter's budget closing a gap that may not exist.

## Why the cited sites matter

The sources show where to do the work. In indoor cycling, editorial sites supplied more than half the citations in every part of the study, from 51% for apps to 56% for trainers. The most cited were cycling titles such as BikeRadar and Cyclingnews. Community sites such as Reddit and YouTube supplied 3% to 5%. Brand websites mattered most for apps, at 30% of citations.

For most brands in that category the job is earned media. That means getting into the buyer's guides the engines quote. ## What Light Meter can't tell you

Light Meter counts whether an engine names a brand. It can't tell you whether the engine praised it or whether anyone bought. Logged-in users with chat history may see different answers. Perplexity and Claude are measured through their APIs, which can differ from their apps. Each study is a snapshot of the month it ran, so a category needs repeat editions before it shows a trend.
