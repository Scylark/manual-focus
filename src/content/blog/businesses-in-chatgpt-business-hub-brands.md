OpenAI opened Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on 10 November 2026, so any eligible business can now claim its ChatGPT profile, correct its details and see how often ChatGPT mentions it and how many people click through. For brands trying to reach ChatGPT's 1.2 billion weekly users, these are the first figures OpenAI has given them about their own presence.

## Key takeaways

- OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on 10 November 2026, with Business Hub for claiming and managing a profile and Business Agents for conversations with a brand.
- Any eligible business located where ChatGPT is available can claim its profile, which includes UK businesses.
- Business Hub reports mentions, website clicks and discovery topics, aggregated and shown only above thresholds OpenAI hasn't published.
- Business Agents are limited to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the United States, and a business can sponsor its agent as the destination for ChatGPT Ads.
- OpenAI's policy says profiles may include information from other sources, and that OpenAI does not verify every detail or resolve every factual dispute.

## What OpenAI launched on 10 November

The [announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) has two parts. Business Hub is the management layer, where a company claims its organisation and, once verified, updates key details and adds self-service product feeds with pricing and availability. Business Agents come second, and an eligible business builds, tests and launches one after its profile is claimed.

There are three ways in. Most businesses claim through Business Hub directly. Existing OpenAI Enterprise customers and advertisers go through their OpenAI representative. Shopify merchants can connect their store from Business Hub or the Shopify admin, and OpenAI says eligible Shopify products are already in ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog, the same route that powers the [ChatGPT Ads app for Shopify](/blog/chatgpt-ads-shopify-uk-ecommerce/).

OpenAI published two policies the same day. The [Businesses in ChatGPT policy](https://openai.com/policies/businesses-in-chatgpt/) says only the owner or an authorised representative may manage a profile, and that OpenAI may decline whole categories until it has the safeguards to support them. The [Business Agents Policy](https://openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/) says verification "alone does not guarantee agent eligibility", because OpenAI reviews both the business category and the agent's intended use before it goes live.

## What does Business Hub measure?

OpenAI names three measures in the announcement, and its dashboard image adds profile views.

| Measure | What OpenAI says it counts | What it leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Mentions | How often a business is mentioned in ChatGPT | No definition of a mention, or whether repeats in one chat count twice |
| Website clicks | Clicks from ChatGPT to the business's site | Whether a click becomes a visit, so check it against your analytics |
| Discovery topics | Common themes across conversations where the business's website is cited | Tied to citations of your site, so a brand mentioned without a link may not appear |
| Profile views | Shown in the dashboard image only | Not described in the text |

OpenAI says it doesn't share users' conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses, and that insights appear only when there are enough conversations or interactions to meet certain thresholds. Small brands may see a claimed profile with no numbers for a while. [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/businesses-gain-chatgpt-click-and-mention-counts-in-openais-business-hub/) looked at two live dashboards on launch day and found clicks of about 3.5% of mentions, with data running a day behind.

## Who can claim a profile, and where?

Claiming is open wherever ChatGPT is available, subject to eligibility, so a UK brand can claim today. Agents are US only for now. The policy also says a profile "may also include information from other sources", so ChatGPT can already be describing your business from what it finds elsewhere, whether or not you claim.

## How Business Agents change the click

When a business appears in a ChatGPT answer, a person can click through to its profile and, if the business has an agent, start a conversation with it. OpenAI's example is a shopper comparing two products and asking which features fit their needs. The agent conversation is labelled, kept separate from the user's original chat and distinct from ChatGPT's independent answers.

The rules for those agents are strict. An agent must say it is an AI representing the business. It can't create false urgency, keep pushing after someone declines, or ask for health details, government IDs or financial credentials. It also can't submit a form, booking or purchase for the user without explicit authorisation through an approved feature. A business can sponsor its agent as the destination for ChatGPT Ads, and the policy adds that further advertising policies may apply when it does.

## What it means for marketing teams

Until now, brand and SEO teams could only estimate their ChatGPT presence from samples of prompts. Business Hub gives them OpenAI's own count, but only for one assistant, only for their own business and only above a threshold. It sits alongside [Bing's Topics Portfolio](/blog/bing-topics-portfolio-ai-citation-gaps/), which shows citation gaps in Microsoft's AI answers, so each engine now reports on itself in its own terms.

The profile is also a brand asset that someone has to own. Its name, category, service area, hours, products and links are what ChatGPT can show at the moment a buyer is comparing options. The policy puts the business on the hook for keeping them accurate and current, and OpenAI won't settle every factual dispute.

Our view at Manual Focus is that every brand should claim its ChatGPT profile this month and give it one named owner, because ChatGPT can already describe an unclaimed business from other sources and the profile is the only part of that answer a brand directly controls. Treat the mention count as a directional signal until OpenAI defines it, and keep measuring ChatGPT and the other engines independently, as [Light Meter](/blog/manual-focus-light-meter-ai-visibility/) does, so you can see competitors and the answers you're missing from.

For paid teams in the US, a sponsored agent makes the ad click land in a conversation rather than on a landing page. The [first outside study of ChatGPT ads](/blog/chatgpt-ads-returns-vs-google-search-study/) found a paid ChatGPT visit returned about a third of a paid search visit, so test a sponsored agent against your current destination before moving budget.

## Five jobs for the next fortnight

1. **Claim the profile.** Use Business Hub, your OpenAI representative if you already advertise, or the Shopify admin if you sell through Shopify. Give it to whoever runs your Google Business Profile, and keep it to people who own the business or are authorised to represent it.
2. **Audit what it says.** Check name, category, address or service area, hours, contact details and products against your own site, and fix prices and availability in the product feed. The policy bans misleading claims about prices, availability or results.
3. **Start a baseline.** Log mentions, clicks and the top discovery topics weekly next to Bing's AI Performance report and your analytics. Compare Business Hub clicks with ChatGPT referral sessions to see how closely they match.
4. **Use discovery topics as a brief.** Topics where your site is already cited show which pages ChatGPT relies on. Strengthen those pages and build the missing ones with the [SEO cluster generator](/lens/content/seo-cluster-generator/).
5. **Write the agent brief before the agent (US sellers).** Set what it may say, which claims need sources and when it hands over to a human, then have legal check it against the Business Agents Policy. The [brand guardrails as code playbook](/lens/ops/brand-guardrails-as-code/) turns that brief into a check.

## What OpenAI hasn't said

OpenAI hasn't defined a mention, published the thresholds or given a price for Business Agents. It doesn't say whether claiming a profile changes how often ChatGPT mentions a business. It gives no date for agents outside the US, no list of the categories it is holding back, and no process for contesting a claim made by someone without authority. It doesn't say how sponsored agents are priced or reported in Ads Manager either. The product is in beta, so the metrics and rules may change before it leaves.
