On 19 October 2026 Manual Focus asked six AI engines 309 UK shopping questions about smart trainers, training apps and smart bikes, three times each. Across 5,562 answers, Wahoo was named in 83% of unbranded smart trainer answers and Zwift in 88% of app answers, and cycling media supplied more than half of every citation.

You can explore every number, every question and every engine's answer in the [full interactive report](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/).

## Key takeaways

- Wahoo is AI's default smart trainer, named in 83% of unbranded trainer answers and placed first by all six engines.
- Zwift leads training apps at 88%, but Rouvy wins scenery questions (97%), MyWhoosh wins value questions (87%) and TrainerRoad leads performance training (84%).
- Smart bikes are close, with Wahoo named in 64% of answers and Wattbike in 58%.
- Challenger brands depend on the engine, as JetBlack ranged from 47% of Claude's trainer answers to 11% of Google AI Mode's.
- Editorial sites supplied 51% to 56% of citations, while Reddit, YouTube and forums supplied 3% to 5%.

## What did we measure?

We wrote 309 questions the way UK riders ask them, such as "quietest smart trainer for a flat" or "best indoor cycling app for students". Of those, 260 name no brand, and only those count towards the headline numbers. The other 49 name brands and sit in the report's question explorer.

Every unbranded question carries a tag for the buying moment behind it, from value and beginner to performance, practical and problem questions. That tag is what lets the report show which brand owns which moment.

The six engines were ChatGPT and Gemini as the public sees them, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Perplexity and Claude with web search, all set to a UK location and collected through [DataForSEO](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/ai_optimization/overview/). Each question ran three times per engine, because AI answers change between runs. A [SparkToro study published in January 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242/) found ChatGPT and Google's AI returned the same brand list less than 1% of the time, and advised tracking how often a brand appears rather than its position. Our data agrees. An engine named exactly the same set of brands across all three runs only 20% of the time, ranging from 13% for ChatGPT to 48% for Perplexity.

So the headline measure is the shortlist rate, the share of answers that name a brand, averaged across the six engines. Every rate in the report carries a 95% range from resampling the questions 2,000 times.

## The AI shortlist by category

| Category | Leader | Second | Third | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smart trainers | Wahoo 83% | Tacx 65% | Elite 39% | JetBlack 26%, Van Rysel 24% |
| Training apps | Zwift 88% | Rouvy 71% | MyWhoosh 69% | TrainerRoad 57% |
| Smart bikes | Wahoo 64% | Wattbike 58% | Zwift Ride 49% | Peloton 40% |

Wahoo's lead in trainers is the clearest result in the study. Its 95% range runs from 79% to 87%, and no other brand gets close. Rouvy and MyWhoosh are effectively tied for second place among apps, and the gap between Wahoo and Wattbike in smart bikes is small enough that a different set of questions could narrow it.

## Who wins which buying moment?

For a brand team, the more useful finding sits below the headline table, because the leader changes with the question behind the search.

| Buying moment | Smart trainers | Training apps | Smart bikes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value | Wahoo 82%, Van Rysel 52% | Zwift 89%, MyWhoosh 87% | Zwift Ride 59%, Wattbike 54% |
| Beginner | Wahoo 73%, Van Rysel 40% | Zwift 93%, MyWhoosh 86% | Peloton 67%, Wattbike and Zwift Ride 52% |
| Performance | Wahoo 93%, Tacx 89% | TrainerRoad 84%, Zwift 82% | Wahoo 89%, Wattbike 73% |
| Scenery | Not asked | Rouvy 97%, Zwift 91% | Not asked |

A brand that wins performance questions can still be missing from the beginner shortlist. Van Rysel, Decathlon's own brand, appears in roughly half of value answers for trainers but in 9% of performance answers. Peloton, a fitness brand more than a cycling one, leads the beginner moment for smart bikes. ![Bubble chart of which training apps AI names for each buying moment. Rouvy leads scenery questions, MyWhoosh value questions and TrainerRoad performance questions.](/images/ai-shortlist/moments.svg)

## Do the AI engines agree?

At the top, yes. Every engine put Wahoo first for trainers and Zwift first for apps. Further down the list the engines behave like different shops with different buyers.

Claude names more brands per answer than any other engine, 4.2 on average against 2.9 to 3.3 for the rest. Saris appears in 40% of Claude's trainer answers and under 10% of every other engine's. Van Rysel appears in 43% of Claude's trainer answers and 37% of ChatGPT's, but in only 5% of Google AI Mode's.

Google AI Overviews only showed up for 64% of these searches. When no overview appeared, no brand got the AI spotlight on that results page, which is why Google AI Overviews gives the lowest rate for most of the leading brands, including Wahoo, Tacx, Zwift and Wattbike.

![Dot plot of how often six AI engines name each smart trainer brand. Engines agree on Wahoo but split widely on Tacx, JetBlack and Van Rysel.](/images/ai-shortlist/engines.svg)

Our view at Manual Focus is that a brand team should treat each engine as a separate channel with its own shortlist, because a single "AI visibility" score averages away the gaps a challenger brand can actually close.

## Which sites decide the answer?

Cycling media supplies most of the sources. Editorial sites supplied 56% of citations for trainers, 53% for smart bikes and 51% for apps, led by BikeRadar, Cyclingnews and Cycling Weekly. Reddit, YouTube and forums supplied 3% to 5%. Brand websites mattered most for apps, at 30% of citations, where Rouvy's and MyWhoosh's own pages were cited in a large share of answers.

![Bar chart of the websites AI engines cite most for indoor cycling questions, led by Cyclingnews, BikeRadar and Cycling Weekly, with the source mix by category.](/images/ai-shortlist/sources.svg)

So the work for a brand team is mostly earned media, in line with our earlier argument that [the citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/). For an endurance brand, the review in a buyer's guide on BikeRadar now does double duty, as the article a rider reads and as the source an AI engine quotes. ## What to do with this

1. Find your buying moments. List the five questions a buyer in your category asks most and check which brands AI names for each one, starting with the moment you most need to win.
2. Check every engine separately. Run your top questions through ChatGPT, Gemini, Google and Claude, because your position on one says little about the others.
3. Audit the cited sources. Note which buyer's guides the engines cite for your category, then check whether your products appear in them and whether the details are current.
4. Fix your own pages for the questions brand sites win. App and software brands get cited directly, so answer value, compatibility and beginner questions plainly on your own site.
5. Measure again in three months, using the same questions, because a single snapshot can't show whether your work moved anything.

## What this study can't tell you

The study counts whether AI names a brand, not whether it praises the brand, and not whether anyone buys. Logged-in users with chat history may see different answers. Perplexity and Claude were measured through their APIs, which can differ from their apps. The engines change often, so this is a picture of October 2026.
