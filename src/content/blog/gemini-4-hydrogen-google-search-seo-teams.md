Google made Gemini 4 Hydrogen available to every Google Search user worldwide on 12 November 2026, two days after its November core update began. Google calls it the biggest model upgrade Search has had and says its wider query fan-out links to more sites, so any AI visibility baseline taken before 12 November is out of date.

## Key takeaways

- Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Search on 12 November 2026 and says it is available globally to all users, not only subscribers.
- Google says Hydrogen widens query fan-out, running simultaneous searches across subtopics to link to a broader range of sites.
- Google also promises more images, structured responses, comparison tables and custom tools in AI answers.
- The November 2026 core update started on 10 November, so two Google changes hit the same week.
- Search Console's generative AI report undercounted impressions from 9 to 11 November 2026 because of a logging error Google says affected data only.

## What Google shipped on 12 November

Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, wrote [Google's Search post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/), which says Hydrogen "is now available globally in Google Search for all users". The post names four improvements, mentions AI Mode languages but not AI Overviews, and gives no figures for links, clicks or answer quality.

The model itself came from Google DeepMind the same day. Koray Kavukcuoglu's [launch post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/) calls Hydrogen Google's "daily driver for agentic workflows", built for fast execution, while Google's frontier Carbon and Argon models handle deep, long-horizon reasoning. Google says Hydrogen cuts fabricated claims by more than five times against Gemini 3.8 Flash, and its Search comparison images set it against Gemini 3.5 Flash. Developers pay $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, and Google says free Gemini app users reach it through automatic model selection.

| What Google says changed | What it means for a brand |
|---|---|
| Better reading of complex, multi-part, typo-filled questions | Long buyer questions get fuller answers, which favours pages that answer the specifics |
| Wider query fan-out across subtopics, with more links | More pages can be cited per answer, including sites that didn't rank before |
| More images, structured responses and comparison tables | Product data, specs and clear tables give Google material to lift |
| More nuanced answers in every supported AI Mode language | UK and non-English answers may change as much as US ones |

## Which searches does Hydrogen change?

Google's post says Hydrogen handles multi-part, typo-filled and highly specific questions better. In a fan-out, Google splits one question into subtopic searches, runs them at once and builds the answer from what comes back, which we explained in [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/) as the reason rankings stopped predicting citations. On that description, a shopper asking which waterproof running shoes suit wide feet on muddy trails under £150 would set off searches on fit, terrain, waterproofing and price, and each one is a chance for a different page to be cited.

Our read is that the searches a buyer types before choosing a brand will change most, while head terms that already return a short answer will change least. Google hasn't published which query types Hydrogen handles in AI Overviews, so treat AI Mode as the confirmed surface and AI Overviews as likely but unconfirmed.

## What it means for SEO and content teams

A wider fan-out rewards coverage of the subtopics buyers care about, written so each section answers one of them on its own. A brand with one long guide that buries fit advice in paragraph nine gives Google less to cite than a brand with a clear section, table or page for each concern. The [SISTRIX citation study](/blog/sistrix-ai-citation-study-geo-tactics/) found structural signals such as tables of contents travelled with more citations, and Google is now promising more tables in its own answers.

Google's October link changes matter more now too. We covered how Google moved [inline links higher in AI Mode](/blog/google-ai-mode-official-websites-links/) on 15 October, and Google says Hydrogen links to a broader range of sites than before. The citation race is wider, so a mid-sized brand has a better chance of a link and a weaker hold on any one of them.

Our view at Manual Focus is that SEO teams should treat 12 November as a new baseline and rebuild their AI visibility tracking around the multi-part questions buyers ask, because Google has changed the model that writes the answers and its fan-out decides which pages get the links.

## How do you measure it this month?

Google's own reporting makes this week hard to read. Its [data anomalies page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/6211453?hl=en) says a logging error lowered impressions in the generative AI report for 9 to 11 November 2026, a window that includes the start of the core update. Google puts the lower figures on those dates down to logging, so don't read them as a Hydrogen or core update effect.

Analytics won't separate it either. Google's new help page on [identifying Search traffic](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/identify-google-search-traffic) says Search sends only the scheme and host, such as `https://www.google.co.uk/`, with no query, and the page describes no separate value for AI Mode. It also says Search Console counts a click when someone selects a result, while analytics counts a visit only once the page loads and its tag runs. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-gemini-4-hydrogen-42526.html) flagged the timing and suggested, without Google confirming it, that Hydrogen touches AI features rather than core rankings.

## Five jobs before December

1. **Mark the dates.** Add 10 November (core update), 12 November (Hydrogen) and 9 to 11 November (logging error) as annotations in GA4, Looker Studio and your SEO reports, so nobody reads a logging dip as a ranking loss.
2. **Rebuild the prompt set.** Write 20 to 30 multi-part questions your buyers ask, with budget, fit and use case in them, and record which brands and pages AI Mode cites for each from a UK location. Re-run them weekly to the end of December. [Light Meter](/blog/manual-focus-light-meter-ai-visibility/) runs this kind of panel across six engines, including AI Mode and AI Overviews.
3. **Map the fan-out gaps.** For each question, list the subtopics Google would search and check you have a page or clear section answering each one. Fill the gaps with the [SEO cluster generator playbook](/lens/content/seo-cluster-generator/).
4. **Give Google tables to lift.** Put specs, sizes, prices and comparisons on your key product and guide pages as plain HTML tables, because Google says Hydrogen returns more comparison tables.
5. **Compare clicks with visits.** Set up a referrer segment for Google domains in analytics, as Google's help page describes, and compare it with Search Console clicks weekly. The [attribution tear-down playbook](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) shows how to keep the two honest.

## What Google hasn't said

Google gives no number for how many more links Hydrogen shows, how often it cites sites outside the top results, or whether clicks rose or fell. It doesn't say whether AI Overviews run on Hydrogen for every query or only some, or which model Search used before for all users, beyond the Gemini 3.5 Flash comparison images. It also doesn't say how Hydrogen interacts with ads in AI Mode, which Google is bringing to the UK this quarter, as we set out in [our AI Mode ads post](/blog/google-ai-mode-ads-uk-paid-search/). When we covered [Lens data in Search Console](/blog/search-console-multimodal-lens-reporting/) in September, the generative AI report showed impressions without clicks, so it can't show whether Hydrogen's extra links send anyone to your site. Until Google publishes more, brands will have to measure the effect on their own accounts.
