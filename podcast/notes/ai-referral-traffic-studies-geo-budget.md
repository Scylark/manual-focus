James and Hannah talk through three studies covered together on 22
October 2026, from Marie Haynes, The SEO Works and Kevin Indig, which all find
that AI assistants send a small share of the traffic and conversions Google
organic search does. They cover whether AI visitors really convert better, why
Google's AI Overviews blur the line between Google and AI traffic, why a spike
in direct traffic is often bots, and how to size and measure AI search work.
Manual Focus sells AI visibility measurement through Light Meter, and James
says so on air. Then three jobs to hand to AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for
your own details. All three need file uploads, and the third works best set up
to run monthly in a tool that can run on a schedule.

### 1. Work out your AI traffic ratio

```text
I run marketing for [brand] at [website], selling mainly in [market].

Studies published in October 2026 found AI assistants sent between about 0.7%
and 2.6% as much traffic as organic search. I want to know where we sit.

Attached is a Google Analytics export of sessions and key events by session
source / medium, by month, for the last 12 months.

1. Treat as AI assistants any source matching this pattern, ignoring case:
   bard|chatgpt|openai|claude|copilot|gemini|perplexity|grok
2. Treat as organic search any row with medium "organic".
3. Give me a table with the columns: Month | Organic sessions | AI assistant
   sessions | AI as % of organic | Organic key events | AI key events |
   Organic conversion rate | AI conversion rate.
4. Add a totals row for the 12 months, and a second table splitting AI
   sessions by assistant.

List any sources you weren't sure how to classify, and don't estimate numbers
that aren't in the file. Note that clicks from Google AI Overviews appear as
organic sessions and can't be separated in this data. If you couldn't check
something, say so.
```

### 2. Clean up direct traffic

```text
I need to know how much of our direct traffic is real before anyone reports
it as an effect of AI search.

Attached is a Google Analytics export of direct / (none) sessions for the last
6 months, by day, with landing page, browser, country, sessions and engaged
sessions.

1. Flag days where direct sessions jumped but engaged sessions didn't.
2. Flag landing pages, browsers or countries with a high share of unengaged
   direct sessions.
3. Leave out home, pricing, login and demo pages, then give me a monthly
   table with the columns: Month | Direct sessions | Engaged direct sessions |
   Engaged share | Change in engaged direct from previous month.
4. Write three sentences I can put in a report on whether engaged direct
   traffic is growing.

Say which patterns you think are bots and why, and mark anything you can't
tell from the data rather than guessing. If you couldn't check something,
say so.
```

### 3. Read what new customers say

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs on the first working day of each month.

Our lead form and post-purchase survey ask "How did you hear about us?" and
include "An AI assistant (such as ChatGPT or Gemini)" as an option, with a
free-text box.

Attached are last month's survey export and last month's AI assistant
sessions from analytics.

1. Count responses by answer, and give AI assistants as a share of all
   responses.
2. From the free text, list every assistant people name, with a count.
3. Give me a table with the columns: Month | Responses | Said AI assistant |
   Share | Assistants named | AI referral sessions in analytics.
4. Compare with previous months if I attach them, and say in two sentences
   whether self-reported AI discovery is rising, falling or flat.

Quote any free-text answers that mention a specific question someone asked
an AI. Don't infer an AI source where the respondent didn't give one, and
if you couldn't check something, say so.
```
