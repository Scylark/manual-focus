James and Hannah go through DemandSphere's data, published on 1 October 2026,
showing Google's AI Overviews on 82.06% of its tracked brand searches by 29
September, up from about 26% earlier in the month. They cover what Chris Long
and Search Engine Roundtable saw, why Google writing a summary of your
company is a reputation question before it's a ranking one, who
should own it, what the evidence doesn't show, and three jobs to hand to AI
this fortnight.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first needs an
assistant that can browse the web and run on a schedule; the second needs
file uploads or pasted text; the third needs a file upload.

### 1. Check your brand searches every fortnight

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every other Monday morning.

I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell] at [website], selling
mainly in [market]. Our main competitor is [competitor].

Search Google for each of these queries, on mobile and desktop, without being
signed in:
- [brand]
- [main product name 1], [main product name 2], [add more]
- [brand] reviews
- [brand] pricing
- [brand] vs [competitor]

For each search, record whether an AI Overview appears, the full text of the
summary, every source Google links beside it, and whether it sits above or
below our own website listing.

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Query | Device | AI Overview shown?
(yes/no) | Position (above or below our listing) | Summary text | Sources
linked.

Then compare it with the previous run and list anything that changed: new or
dropped AI Overviews, changes in what the summary says, new sources, or a
change in position. If a search wouldn't load, or you couldn't see the
results as a normal searcher would, say so in that row rather than leaving it
blank.
```

### 2. Check what the summaries say against your facts

```text
I run marketing for [brand]. Below are the AI Overviews Google showed for
searches on our brand, with the sources it linked beside each one:
[paste the table from the first prompt, or attach screenshots].

Attached are our current facts: our about page, product pages and Google
Business Profile details [attach or paste].

For each summary, mark every statement that is out of date, wrong or
unflattering, and compare it with our current facts.

Give me a table with the columns: Query | Statement in the summary | Problem
(out of date / wrong / unflattering) | What our current facts say | Sources
Google linked beside it | Who fixes it (us: own pages or Google Business
Profile / PR: review site or directory / Wikipedia talk page).

Then draft:
- the updated wording for any of our own pages that is out of date
- a short, polite correction request for each review site or directory
- a Wikipedia talk-page request that discloses our connection to [brand] and
  asks for the change, rather than an edit to the article itself

Don't send anything. If you can't tell which source a statement came from,
say so rather than guessing.
```

### 3. Set a branded click baseline

```text
DemandSphere's tracking showed AI Overviews on most branded searches from
26 September 2026. I want a baseline for our branded clicks either side of
that date.

Attached is a Google Search Console Performance export, filtered to queries
containing [brand] and [main product names], with daily clicks, impressions
and click-through rate from [start date] to [end date].

Compare the weeks before 26 September with the same number of weeks after it.

Give me a table with the columns: Query | Clicks before | Clicks after |
Impressions before | Impressions after | CTR before | CTR after | CTR change
(percentage points). Add a total row for all branded queries, and sort by
CTR change, largest drop first.

Only report what the data shows, and don't say why anything changed. If the
date range is too short for a fair comparison, or some branded queries look
like they've been filtered out, say so before the table.
```

### And one for you

Decide who owns the AI Overview on your brand name and put a name next to it
this week. Our view is that it should be the head of brand or comms, with SEO
supplying the monitoring, because what it says is a reputation question before
it's a ranking one.
