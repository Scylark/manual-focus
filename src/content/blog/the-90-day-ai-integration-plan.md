A marketing team where people use ChatGPT inconsistently has AI usage rather than an AI strategy. This 90-day plan closes that gap without hiring or restructuring. The first month sets a shared voice prompt and tool stack. Two rebuilt workflows follow by week eight, with weekly measurement and an open team conversation in the final month.

## Key takeaways

- Start with a two-week audit of every AI tool the team uses, because 78% of AI users were bringing their own tools to work in Microsoft's 2024 Work Trend Index.
- The brand-voice prompt is the first fix, because every workflow built later inherits it.
- Rebuild one workflow end to end by week six and run it on three real pieces, so the team sees proof before the pattern spreads.
- Two well-run rebuilt workflows by week eight beat five half-built ones.
- BCG's 2025 AI at Work survey found that strong leadership support lifts the share of employees who feel positive about generative AI from 15% to 55%.

The gap between AI usage and AI strategy is where most marketing functions are quietly losing ground right now. The plan below is sequential and concrete. Its shape works in agencies as well as in-house teams.

The difference between usage and strategy shows up in five places.

| Area | AI usage | AI strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Tools | Whatever each person signed up for | Three tools paid for centrally |
| Voice | Adjectives in a Notion page | A prompt with examples and bans |
| Workflows | ChatGPT inside the old process | Two workflows rebuilt end to end |
| Measurement | Nobody tracks the extra output | One dashboard reviewed weekly |
| People | Power users working in the shadows | An open team conversation |

## What should the AI audit cover?

Spend weeks one and two on an audit, because you can't plan the integration until you know the starting position. The audit is two questions.

What is everyone on the team using AI for right now? Survey it. The honest answer is usually unsanctioned ChatGPT use plus a paid subscription or two nobody's tracking. A few power users will be running shadow workflows the rest of the team doesn't know about. Microsoft's [2024 Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part) found that 78% of AI users were bringing their own AI tools to work, so expect the survey to turn up more than you thought.

Is the brand voice doc operational? Pull it up. If it's a Notion page describing tone with adjectives it's not operational, because the voice doc has to be specific enough to be prompted from, with examples and bans. If it's not, that's your first fix.

By the end of week two you should have a one-page picture of current AI usage and spend by team member. Add the three pain points the team would most like to fix.

## The voice prompt and the tool stack

In weeks three and four, get the brand voice prompt right. It's the most valuable thing you can do in the first month, because it propagates through every other workflow you'll set up later.

The prompt needs three positive examples of strong on-brand work and three annotated negative examples that are close but not quite right. Add specific bans and the structural patterns you use. Get a senior writer or editor to own it, then iterate until pasting it into a draft request produces something close to ship-ready. The [brand voice extraction](/lens/brand/brand-voice-extraction/) playbook in The Lens walks through building it.

While that's happening, settle the tool stack at three subscriptions, all paid for centrally.

- One chat model, such as Claude or ChatGPT. Any of the big ones is a defendable choice, so pick one.
- One research tool.
- One media-production tool.

Then kill the unsanctioned shadow stack. BCG's [2025 AI at Work survey](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain) found that when employees don't have the AI tools they need, more than half will find alternatives and use them anyway, so the sanctioned stack has to cover the jobs people use AI for. For a practical way to pick, see [how to evaluate AI tools without falling for the demo](/blog/evaluating-ai-tools-without-falling-for-the-demo/), where the four-week pilot rule saves a lot of budget.

By the end of month one, every team member should work from the same tools and the same voice prompt.

## Rebuild one workflow end to end

In weeks five and six, pick the workflow that's the biggest drag on the team. Usually it's content production, from brief to publication, though sometimes it's reporting. We've broken down [five workflow changes that lift content production](/blog/five-ways-improve-content-production-ai/), so start there if content is your biggest drag.

Whichever it is, rebuild it with AI in the loop properly, which means more than the team using ChatGPT during the old workflow.

- The brief is AI-generated and human-edited.
- The draft is AI-produced against the brief and the voice prompt.
- The edit is human.
- The repurposing into other formats is AI-produced from the long-form.

The [brief-to-ship pipeline](/lens/ops/brief-to-ship-pipeline/) playbook is one worked version of this rebuild. Run the documented workflow on three real pieces and measure the time and quality against the old one.

This is the proof point. If the team can see one workflow that's faster and at least as good, the whole adoption gets easier.

## Roll it out to a second workflow

In weeks seven and eight, apply the same pattern to a second function. Inputs and outputs stay defined, with AI-assisted production in between and editorial decisions owned by people. Pick whichever is the next biggest pain, such as reporting or campaign briefs.

Don't try to do all of them. Two well-running rebuilt workflows beat five half-built ones, because the point of this stage is to prove the pattern travels rather than to maximise coverage.

By the end of week eight you should have two workflows running cleanly, each documented enough that a new hire could pick them up in a week.

## How do you measure the gains?

Measure with one weekly loop, built in weeks nine and ten. A lot of AI rollouts stall here, because the team produces more but nobody tracks whether the extra output is working. Six months in there's no honest answer to "is this paying off". Microsoft's 2024 survey found that 59% of leaders worried about quantifying the productivity gains of AI.

Set up one dashboard as the single source of truth and review it weekly. Tag each piece of work by topic and by workflow, meaning the AI process that produced it. Each week the model summarises what's working, then the senior person reads it and makes the call on what to adjust.

This loop is what makes the engine compound, in the sense we set out in [building content engines that compound](/blog/building-content-engines-that-compound/). Without it, you're producing more output of unclear quality.

## How do you bring the team with you?

By weeks eleven and twelve the rollout has changed what the team does day to day. Some people will have leaned in while others resisted. A few might be checked out because they think their job is about to disappear.

This is when the leadership work matters most.

Run a one-hour conversation with the team rather than a strategy presentation. Ask what's working and where people are nervous. Be specific that AI magnifies the senior marketing problem instead of reducing it. The people who learn to operate the workflow well end up doing more of the strategic work. BCG found that the share of employees who feel positive about generative AI rises from 15% to 55% with strong leadership support.

This conversation is the one most leaders skip. Without it, the people side of the change goes underground and the workflow gains never quite stick. [The hidden cost of AI adoption nobody talks about](/blog/the-hidden-cost-of-ai-adoption/) covers the people problems that stall rollouts.

## What does 90 days get you?

By the end of the quarter, a team that wasn't doing structured AI work can be producing substantially more at brand quality, with two well-run workflows and measurement in place. It's the practical version of AI in marketing, built on tools that exist today. [The three layers of AI](/blog/three-layers-of-ai-basics/) explains where agents fit once those basics are running.

The next 90 days widen the surface with more workflows. The 90 after that add depth through better pipelines and better measurement. Both build on the floor the first 90 set.

The teams that don't do this work will spend the next year being out-shipped by teams that did. Our view at Manual Focus is that the differentiator is disciplined integration, which is a leadership job rather than a tools job.
