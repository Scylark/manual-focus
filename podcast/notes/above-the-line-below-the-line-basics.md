A Manual Focus Basics episode. James and Hannah explain above the line
(mass-media advertising that builds memory), below the line (direct, targeted
activity that prompts action) and through the line (both, under one idea).
They cover where the terms come from and the origin story they couldn't
verify, why digital sits on both sides of the line, what Binet and Field's
IPA work says about short-term metrics, how the split looks in B2B, and who
should own the balance between memory and action.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. All three work with
pasted text or uploaded files.

### 1. Sort last year's budget into memory and action

```text
I lead marketing at [company]. I want to see how last year's budget split
between building memory of the brand (above the line) and prompting action
from people close to buying (below the line).

Definitions:
- Above the line: mass media reaching a broad audience not selected
  individually, such as TV, radio, press, outdoor, cinema and broad-reach
  online video.
- Below the line: direct, targeted activity such as direct mail, email,
  events, trade shows, sampling, promotions, point-of-sale and sponsorship
  activation.
- Digital that could be either: search, social, display and video, depending
  on how tightly it was targeted.

Attached is our spend by line item for [period].

Give me a table with the columns: Line item | Spend | Above / below / could
be either | The job it was doing (memory or action) | Why you classed it
that way.

Then give the totals and the percentage split. If a line item's description
doesn't tell you what it was, mark it "can't classify" and say what I'd need
to tell you.
```

### 2. Check a campaign is genuinely through the line

```text
We're running [campaign] across these channels: [list]. The campaign idea, in
one sentence, is: [idea].

Attached are the assets or copy for each channel.

For each channel, tell me: Channel | Mass or targeted | Does the asset carry
the campaign idea? (clearly / partly / no) | What has drifted (message, look,
offer or tone) | Suggested fix.

Then say whether the mass and targeted pieces would be recognisable as one
campaign to someone who saw both. Quote the assets' own words. If you can't
see an asset, say so in its row.
```

### 3. Draft a measurement plan for both horizons

```text
Our campaign objective is [objective], running from [date] to [date] across
[channels].

Draft a measurement plan with two parts:
1. Short-term response measures for the targeted, below-the-line work, such
   as leads, redemptions or sales, with how often to check them.
2. Longer-term brand measures for the mass, above-the-line work, such as
   awareness or branded search, with when they can first be judged.

Give it as a table with the columns: Measure | Which channels it judges |
Data source | First reading | Review rhythm.

End with a short note on when and how to review both sets of numbers
together. Only use data sources from this list, which is what we have:
[list]. If a measure needs data we don't have, say so rather than assuming
it.
```
