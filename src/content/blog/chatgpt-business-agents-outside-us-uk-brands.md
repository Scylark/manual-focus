OpenAI opened Business Agents to ecommerce and online service businesses outside the US on 19 November 2026, nine days after launching them for US sellers only. A UK brand that passes OpenAI's checks can now publish an agent inside ChatGPT and, as access rolls out, make it the destination of a ChatGPT ad with no separate charge per conversation.

## Key takeaways

- On 19 November 2026 OpenAI updated its Businesses in ChatGPT announcement to say ecommerce and online service businesses based outside the US can now claim their business and publish agents.
- OpenAI names no countries, and its help centre says agent eligibility depends on location, business type and industry, so each brand has to check in Business Hub.
- Sponsored Business Agents are rolling out to eligible advertisers in Ads Manager over the coming weeks, with no separate charge for agent conversations or messages.
- A sponsored agent runs only on a campaign with the conversion optimisation objective, and advertisers see impressions and clicks but never chat transcripts.
- A Business Agent explains products and policies and links to the brand's website, but it cannot complete a purchase, make a booking or check an order.

## What OpenAI added after 10 November

When OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on 10 November, [our post that week](/blog/businesses-in-chatgpt-business-hub-brands/) told UK brands they could claim a profile but not build an agent. On 19 November OpenAI added a note to the top of the [announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) saying "ecommerce and online service businesses based outside the U.S. are now eligible to claim their business and publish agents in ChatGPT." The body of the page still describes agents as a US product, and the [Business Agents Policy](https://openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/), last updated on 10 November, lists no countries at all.

The paid side followed. Search Engine Roundtable [reported on 25 November](https://www.seroundtable.com/chatgpt-ads-updates-42623.html) that OpenAI's latest ChatGPT Ads product email told advertisers outside the US they can now attach an agent to a campaign as a Sponsored Business Agent. OpenAI's [help article on sponsored agents](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001651-setting-up-a-sponsored-business-agent-in-ads-manager) says the feature reaches eligible advertisers in Ads Manager over the coming weeks, and its [availability page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001245-ads-manager-availability) already lists the United Kingdom for self-service ChatGPT Ads.

The new help pages also answer questions OpenAI left open at launch.

| Question | OpenAI's position on 10 November | OpenAI's position now |
|---|---|---|
| Can a business outside the US publish an agent? | No, US sellers only | Yes, for eligible ecommerce and online service businesses |
| Which countries qualify? | The United States | Not named |
| What does an agent conversation cost? | Not stated | No separate charge, normal campaign billing applies to ads |
| What reporting comes with a sponsored agent? | Not stated | Impressions and clicks, no transcripts, common questions after 25 conversations |
| Which campaigns can use one? | Not stated | Campaigns with the conversion optimisation objective |

## Who can build an agent now?

OpenAI's [setup guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt) says a business must be an eligible ecommerce or online service business, have a website and meet the Business Agents Policy, and that "a verified profile alone doesn't establish that you can create an agent." Identity checks run through Persona, which may ask for a government-issued ID, business registration details and proof of authorisation, and OpenAI blocks agent creation while that check is pending or after it fails.

Building the agent is a short loop. You review the information it will use, such as product details and the returns policy, write its instructions, preview it with common customer questions and fix any wrong answer before you press Publish. Sellers can connect a product feed as a CSV or a hosted feed URL, and Shopify merchants can use their existing catalogue. OpenAI warns that a feed doesn't guarantee ChatGPT will recommend the products.

## What changes for UK brand and paid teams?

For the brand team, the agent is a new place where your company speaks in ChatGPT, and you write its brief. It can explain products and policies and send people to your site, and it stops there, because it can't buy, book or check an order. Every answer it gives on delivery times or returns therefore has to match the website word for word, or customers will bring the contradiction to your service team.

For paid teams the change is the ad click. OpenAI says sponsored ads carry Start chat and Visit site controls, so a shopper can talk to your agent before reaching a landing page. The [first outside study of ChatGPT ads](/blog/chatgpt-ads-returns-vs-google-search-study/) found a paid ChatGPT visit returned about a third of a paid search visit, and a conversation could narrow that gap or widen it. You get impressions and clicks but no transcripts, so the only proof will come from conversions you track on your own site, which is the gap we described in [ChatGPT's new ad measurement tools](/blog/chatgpt-ads-measurement-visual-ads/).

For SEO and content teams, Business Hub counts a mention only when ChatGPT references your website, not your brand name alone. A brand that is talked about but rarely cited will see low numbers, which is consistent with [SparkToro's finding](/blog/sparktoro-chatgpt-prompts-study-ai-visibility/) that only a small share of ChatGPT prompts involve choosing a brand to buy from.

Our view at Manual Focus is that UK ecommerce brands should publish an unsponsored agent as soon as Business Hub allows it, and hold paid spend until the agent has answered enough real questions to show where it goes wrong. The Insights tab may show common questions only after 25 conversations, so let organic chats reach that point first and fix the answers it exposes before you pay to send shoppers there.

## What to do before access reaches you

1. **Start Persona verification this week.** OpenAI won't let you create an agent while the check is pending, so gather the ID, company registration details and proof of authority now, then look for the agent option in Business Hub.
2. **Write the agent's instructions from pages you already stand behind.** Use the live product, delivery and returns pages, and have whoever signs off ad copy approve the brief. The [brand guardrails as code playbook](/lens/ops/brand-guardrails-as-code/) turns that brief into a repeatable check.
3. **Connect a feed and check it against the site.** During peak trading a price in the feed that differs from the checkout price is the fastest way to lose a sale the agent started.
4. **Preview with your 20 most common customer questions.** Take them from your service inbox, fix every wrong answer, then publish.
5. **Test one sponsored agent against one landing page.** When Business agent appears in Ads Manager, run the same creative on a conversion optimisation campaign both ways and compare orders in your own analytics. The [attribution tear-down playbook](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) sets up a fair comparison.

## What OpenAI hasn't said

OpenAI hasn't published a list of countries, so it isn't clear whether "outside the U.S." means every market where ChatGPT is available or a smaller set, and the original announcement text still says US. The sponsored rollout runs over "the coming weeks" with no firm date, and there is no data yet on how agent chats convert. OpenAI also hasn't said whether publishing an agent changes how often ChatGPT mentions a business. Search Engine Roundtable reported new reach and frequency metrics in the same advertiser email, but OpenAI's [measurement help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001214-measure-results) doesn't list them yet, so treat them as unconfirmed until they appear in your account.
