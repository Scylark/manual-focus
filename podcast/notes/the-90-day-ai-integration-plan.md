James and Hannah walk through a 90-day plan for moving a marketing team from
scattered AI use to structured AI work, without hiring or restructuring. It
runs in six two-week blocks: audit current usage and the brand voice doc,
write an operational voice prompt and settle on one tool of each kind, rebuild
one workflow end to end, roll the pattern to a second workflow, build a weekly
measurement loop, and hold an honest conversation with the team. They cover
why the voice prompt comes first, why two well-run workflows beat five
half-built ones, and three parts of the plan you can hand to AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. All three need file
uploads, and the third needs an assistant that can run on a weekly schedule.

### 1. Run the week-one audit

```text
I lead marketing at [company], a [type of team: agency, in-house, B2B,
commerce] team of [number] people. I'm starting a 90-day plan to move us from
inconsistent AI use to structured AI work, and I need an honest picture of
where we are now.

Attached are:
- the team's answers to a survey on which AI tools they use, what for, and
  how often (including tools the company doesn't pay for)
- our current software spend, or the list of AI subscriptions on expenses
- our brand voice document

Do three things.

1. Give me a table of current AI usage with the columns: Team member or role |
   Tools used | What they use them for | Paid for centrally? (yes/no/unknown) |
   Monthly cost (from the spend file).
2. Assess the voice document. Is it operational, meaning specific enough to
   prompt from? Check for positive examples, negative examples, specific bans
   and structural patterns, and list which are missing. Quote any lines that
   are only adjectives describing tone.
3. List the three biggest pain points the team raises, with how many people
   mentioned each one.

Finish with a one-page summary I can share: current usage, current tool
spend, voice-doc state, top three pain points. Don't estimate any cost or
count you can't see in the files; mark it "not in data" instead.
```

### 2. Draft the first version of the voice prompt

```text
I need an operational brand-voice prompt for [brand], one we can paste into
any draft request so the output comes back close to ship-ready. Our senior
[writer/editor] will own it and refine it; you're writing version one.

Attached are:
- three pieces of strong, on-brand work: [titles]
- three pieces that were off-brand or close-but-not-quite: [titles], with a
  line from me on what was wrong with each
- our current brand voice document, if we have one

Write the prompt with these sections:
1. Who we are and who we write for, in two or three sentences.
2. Positive examples: a paragraph from each strong piece, with a line on what
   makes it on-brand.
3. Negative examples: a paragraph from each weak piece, annotated with what
   to avoid.
4. Specific bans: words, phrases and habits never to use.
5. Structural patterns: how our pieces open, how long paragraphs run, how we
   use headings, how we close.

Base every rule on the attached examples, not on general good writing advice.
After the prompt, list anything you weren't sure about or couldn't tell from
the examples, so our editor knows where to look first.
```

### 3. Weekly summary of what's working

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every Monday morning.

Each week I'll attach an export from our marketing dashboard. Every piece of
work in it is tagged by workflow (which AI process produced it), by topic and
by format, with its performance figures.

Each week, give me:
1. A table with the columns: Workflow | Pieces published | Main performance
   figure | Change on last week.
2. The same for topic and for format.
3. Three short bullets on what's working and three on what isn't, each tied
   to a number in the export.
4. Anything that moved sharply since last week.

Don't recommend what to change; a person makes that call. If a piece is
missing a tag, or a figure is missing or looks wrong, list it separately
rather than guessing.
```
