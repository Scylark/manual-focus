At its DevDay conference on 29 September 2026, OpenAI opened ChatGPT, which has 1.2 billion weekly users, to plugins that brands can build as native features. It also launched dots, always-on agents that work in the background. Marketing teams now have a new place to be found and a new kind of colleague to govern.

## Key takeaways

- OpenAI made more than 20 announcements at DevDay on 29 September 2026, including plugin extensions and always-on agents called dots.
- Plugin extensions, which Canva and Adobe already use, give a brand's plugin a home in the ChatGPT sidebar with panels beside the conversation.
- OpenAI's developer guide tells teams to treat plugin metadata like product copy, because ChatGPT decides when to call a plugin from the names and descriptions its developer writes.
- Dots run on GPT-6 Astra with their own cloud computer and connect to more than 4,000 apps through plugins.
- In November 2026 OpenAI's help centre said dots on Pro plans are not available in the UK or the European Economic Area, while Business Premium users in supported regions can use them.

**Update, 11 November 2026.** UK Pro subscribers can't use dots yet. OpenAI's [dots help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot) says dots on Pro are rolling out to users aged 18 and over in markets outside the UK and the European Economic Area, while Business Premium users in supported ChatGPT regions can use them. Eligible Enterprise workspaces can preview dots free from 4 to 18 November, after which dots switch on by default unless an admin opts out, with billing from the same day. On the plugin side, OpenAI's [Radisson case study](https://openai.com/index/radisson/), which [our GPT-6 post](/blog/chatgpt-gpt-6-intelligent-ui-marketing-teams/) covers, says ChatGPT now shows an installed plugin whenever it is relevant, without the user typing its name. On 10 November OpenAI also launched [Business Agents](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) for eligible US ecommerce and online service businesses, which gives a brand a second way to hold a conversation inside ChatGPT.

## Plugins and dots at DevDay

OpenAI's [DevDay recap](https://openai.com/index/devday-2026-recap/) lists more than 20 announcements, most aimed at developers. Two of them change marketing work.

The first is plugin extensions. Plugins could already answer inside a ChatGPT conversation, but now a plugin can also live in the sidebar with an interactive panel next to the chat, or act as the viewer for its own file types. OpenAI's [developer documentation](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/extensions) describes four kinds of extension and names a brand already using three of them.

| Extension | What it does | Brand named |
|---|---|---|
| Sidebar tab | Gives a plugin its own home in the ChatGPT sidebar | Canva |
| Composer mention | Lets users find and mention files in the desktop app | Figma |
| File handler | Opens supported file types for editing inside ChatGPT | Adobe |
| Conversation panel | Adds an interactive panel beside the chat | None named |

OpenAI also says improved ranking and recommendations now help people find plugins in the directory and in conversations. Users still choose which plugins to install and approve the access each one gets.

The second is dots. OpenAI's [dots announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-dots/) describes an agent powered by GPT-6 Astra, with its own cloud computer and browser, that connects to more than 4,000 apps and takes messages in ChatGPT or Slack. Two of OpenAI's own examples are marketing jobs, a dot that revises launch materials when a product's scope changes and one that turns an interview transcript into show notes and social drafts for approval. Dots are rolling out to Pro and Business Premium plans in eligible markets, with the first dot included at no extra cost. Enterprise admins can switch on a beta that is off by default.

## Why plugins matter to brands

A brand could already appear in ChatGPT as a cited source or as a plugin that answered inside the chat. At DevDay OpenAI gave plugins a home in the sidebar, then added ranking that puts them in front of people mid-conversation. When someone asks ChatGPT to plan a training block or compare insurance quotes, the plugin ChatGPT recommends gets to do the work while the brand behind it stays in the thread.

## How does ChatGPT choose a plugin?

OpenAI's guide to [optimising plugin metadata](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/guides/optimize-metadata) says ChatGPT decides when to call a plugin's tools from the names and descriptions the developer writes. It tells teams to treat that metadata like product copy and to test it against three kinds of prompt.

- Direct prompts, where the user names your product.
- Indirect prompts, which describe the outcome without naming you.
- Negative prompts, where another tool should answer.

That is keyword research with a new name, so the marketing team should own it alongside engineering.

Our view at Manual Focus is that any brand whose product can do a job in a conversation, such as a booking or a quote, should scope a ChatGPT plugin this quarter and assign its metadata to the same people who write product pages and paid search copy. Brands that sell through a catalogue rather than a tool will get more from product feeds and [ChatGPT Ads on Shopify](/blog/chatgpt-ads-shopify-uk-ecommerce/).

## Should you switch on a dot?

The CMO has a separate decision to make about dots. An agent that drafts social posts overnight and revises launch copy on its own can save hours, but it can also publish off-brand work at the same speed. Custom Rules let you decide which actions each dot may take alone and which need approval, so the brand team should write those rules before anyone switches a dot on. Our [brand guardrails as code](/lens/ops/brand-guardrails-as-code/) playbook sets out how to turn a brand book into rules an agent can check against. Write the same rules for rival agents, such as [Anthropic's Claude Duet](/blog/claude-duet-proactive-agent-marketing-teams/), which opened a US waitlist in October.

## Where to start with plugins and dots

1. **List the jobs your product does.** Write down five tasks a customer could finish with your product inside a conversation. If none come to mind, a plugin isn't for you yet.
2. **Write a golden prompt set.** For each job, collect ten prompts of each of the three kinds above. It doubles as research on how customers phrase the need. Our [evaluation frameworks playbook](/lens/ops/evaluation-frameworks/) shows how to score the results consistently.
3. **Search the directory for rivals.** Look up your category in the ChatGPT plugin directory and note who is already there and how they describe themselves.
4. **Draft agent rules before the pilot.** Decide which actions a dot may take alone and which need sign-off, starting with anything that publishes or spends money.
5. **Check your plan and market.** Dots on Pro plans exclude the UK, so a UK pilot needs Business Premium or an Enterprise workspace. Check OpenAI's help centre before you promise one.

## What OpenAI hasn't said yet

OpenAI hasn't published how it ranks or recommends plugins, or whether paid placement will ever sit next to them. It also hasn't said how often a plugin surfaces without the user asking for it. OpenAI says users will later be able to add more dots and scale their output, without saying what that will cost. Specialist dots, which OpenAI says it has tested internally on email marketing and customer support, are limited to enterprise pilots for now.
