James and Hannah go through a study Vaer AI ran for Lightspeed Commerce,
published on 29 September 2026, which found ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Google
AI Overviews picked a national chain over a smaller retailer 90 to 94% of the
time when answering from training data alone. They cover why smaller retailers
lose most ground on product-level queries, why adding the word "independent"
to a prompt changed the answers so sharply, what Lightspeed's commercial
interest and the North American sample mean for UK brands, and three jobs to
hand to AI before peak trading.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first needs a file
upload or a pasted list; the second needs an assistant that can browse the web
and run on a schedule (or someone to paste in answers from assistants it can't
reach); the third needs browsing.

### 1. Write your 30 product-level test prompts

```text
I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell], at [website]. We
sell mainly in [market], and customers choose us because we are [independent /
specialist / local / expert-led - pick what is true].

Attached (or pasted below) is our list of our ten best-selling products, with
any real customer questions we have from reviews, search queries or the help
desk: [attach or paste].

For each product, write three buying questions a shopper would type into an AI
assistant when they have already decided roughly what they want, phrased the
way a customer would say them, not the way we describe the product. Where
location matters for buying, include [town or region].

Then write a paired version of every prompt that adds our distinguishing
attribute ("independent", "specialist" or whichever applies), changing nothing
else.

Give me a table with the columns: Product | Prompt number (1-3) | Plain
prompt | Paired prompt (with attribute) | Why a shopper would ask this.

That should be 30 rows. Don't invent product details that aren't in what I've
given you; if you need one, put it in [brackets] for me to fill in.
```

### 2. Run the prompts every month

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs on the first Monday of each month, with an
extra run in early November before Black Friday.

Here are our 30 test prompts, each in a plain and a paired version: [paste the
table from the first prompt]. Our brand is [brand] at [website].

For each prompt and each version, check the answer in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode
and Google AI Overviews [from a location in our market]. If you can't reach
one of those assistants yourself, tell me which, and I will paste its answers
in for you to log.

Record which retailers or brands each answer names and links to, in order.

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Product | Prompt | Version (plain or
paired) | Assistant | Retailers named, in order | Are we named? (yes/no, and
position) | Large national chain named first? (yes/no).

Then give a short summary: how often we were named overall, plain versus
paired, broad versus product-level, and what changed since the last run.
Where an assistant showed no answer or wouldn't load, say so in that row
rather than leaving it blank.
```

### 3. Find the pages that win the prompts you lose

```text
Here are the prompts from our latest test run where [brand] was not named,
with the links each AI answer cited: [paste the rows and links].

Go through every cited link and sort them into: editorial round-ups and buying
guides, review sites, directories and listings, retailer or brand pages, and
other.

Give me a table with the columns: Site | Page title | URL | Type | Number of
lost prompts it appeared in | Which products it covers | Are we mentioned on
the page? (yes/no/couldn't check).

Rank by the number of lost prompts. Then list the top ten editorial, review
and directory pages as a brief for our PR team, with one line on what each
page seems to need to include us. Mark any page you couldn't open, and don't
guess at its contents.
```
