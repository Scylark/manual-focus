On 23 September 2026 the Competition and Markets Authority proposed that Google offer UK Android and Chrome users a choice of up to twelve search services at set-up and every year after, with AI assistants like ChatGPT eligible. If the plan goes through, UK marketers will need to be cited by those assistants as well as ranked by Google.

## Key takeaways

- On 23 September 2026 the CMA proposed that Google show UK Android and Chrome users a search choice screen at set-up, followed by a reminder at least once a year.
- AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Perplexity could appear on the screen if they work as a general search service and credit the sources behind their answers.
- When more than twelve providers qualify, each screen would show twelve in random order, made up of the five most popular and seven picked at random.
- The CMA's own user research tested the screens with 80 people and found that many picked the search service they already used.
- The consultation closed on 9 October 2026, with a final decision expected by the end of the year and the rules in force within six months of it.

**Update, 11 November 2026.** The consultation closed at 5pm on 9 October. On 26 and 30 October the CMA [published 28 responses](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/googles-general-search-services-proposed-user-choice-conduct-requirement), including Google's and OpenAI's. [Google's response](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6adb7d232263175ac2aca3d3/Google.pdf), dated 13 October, argues that the revised rules would be unlawful because the CMA excluded AI assistants from the general search activity it designated Google for. The CMA says it is analysing the feedback. Its [case page](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/googles-general-search-and-search-advertising-services) showed no final decision on 11 November, so the plan below is still a proposal.

## What the CMA proposed

The CMA published [strengthened proposals](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-strengthens-proposals-allowing-people-choice-over-their-search-service) for its user choice conduct requirement, one of the rules it can impose on Google since it designated Google with strategic market status in general search in October 2025. Under the revised plan Google would have to do three things.

- Show a search choice screen when someone sets up an Android phone or opens Chrome for the first time, then prompt them every year to choose their default.
- Let AI assistants onto those screens where they meet the eligibility criteria, including technical and security requirements.
- Make every provider on the screen credit publisher content clearly, so people can reach the source and see where an answer came from.

The [consultation document](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab29c76fe72ed1e2b02eebe/consultation_user_choice_conduct_requirement.pdf) goes further than the press release. It extends the screen to Chrome on any desktop or mobile operating system, including Windows and iOS, wherever Google Search is the default. It also has Google list providers in a fully random order that people must scroll through before they choose. When more than twelve providers qualify, Google shows the five most popular and seven picked at random. An assistant only qualifies if it handles the full range of general search tasks with current information from the web. It must also link clearly to the content behind its answers.

CMA chief executive Sarah Cardell said the regulator is responding to AI assistants "which people are increasingly using as their search service, but aren't included on Google's choice screens." Google had argued against annual prompts and proposed a permanent setting instead, [according to The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/cma-google-choice-screens-ai-assistants). The consultation ran until 9 October and the CMA expects to decide by the end of the year.

## Will people switch from Google?

Most people search wherever their phone or browser sends them, which in the UK has meant Google by default for as long as most marketing plans have existed. The CMA would leave Google on the list, but it would make Google ask the question again every year on every covered phone and browser, with ChatGPT and Perplexity potentially sitting next to it.

You shouldn't plan for a rush away from Google, because the CMA's own [user research](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6ab2903ba9c3d267bccced0e/user_research_on_choice_screens_in_search.pdf) points the other way. Thinks Insight & Strategy tested the screens with 80 people and found that many rushed them and picked a service they already used. When people noticed the AI assistants they slowed down to read the options. Some picked one as their default, so expect a slow drift led by early adopters.

## What does it mean for UK marketers?

Attribution is where this touches your numbers. An assistant only gets onto the screen if it credits and links to its sources, so when a UK user who has switched asks a question, the page the assistant quotes gets a visit as well as a mention. We looked at how far AI citations have already drifted from Google rankings in [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/). A regulator requiring those links would widen that gap.

| Team | What would change |
|---|---|
| SEO and content | AI citations count alongside Google rankings |
| Paid search | Some UK searches start outside Google |
| Analytics | AI referrals need a channel of their own |
| Publishers and PR | Eligible assistants must credit and link to sources |

Our view at Manual Focus is that this won't move UK search share much in 2027, but it will reward the brands AI assistants can already quote easily. If your paid search plan assumes every UK search starts on Google, write that down as an assumption and review it each quarter. Google is changing its own results page as well, with [ads planned for AI Mode in the UK](/blog/google-ai-mode-ads-uk-paid-search/) this quarter.

## How to prepare for the decision

1. **Find out where you're cited now.** Put your 20 most commercially important buyer questions into the main AI assistants, starting with ChatGPT and Perplexity. Note which brands each one cites and links to, so you have a baseline to measure against. [Manual Focus Light Meter](/blog/manual-focus-light-meter-ai-visibility/) runs this kind of check across six AI engines.
2. **Measure AI referrals.** Segment traffic from AI assistant domains such as chatgpt.com and perplexity.ai in your analytics, so you can see the drift when it comes.
3. **Check the assistants can read your site.** Review robots.txt and your CDN's bot rules for AI crawlers. Your key pages should render as plain HTML. Our post on the [shared AI training opt-out](/blog/shared-ai-training-opt-out-robots-txt/) explains which robots.txt choices affect AI training and which affect AI search.
4. **Rewrite your top pages to answer first.** Give the direct answer in the opening paragraph, with specific numbers and sources a model can check. The [SEO cluster generator](/lens/content/seo-cluster-generator/) playbook shows how to do this across a whole topic.
5. **Watch for the final decision.** The CMA expects to decide by the end of the year. Google's published response shows which parts of the plan it will contest, starting with whether AI assistants belong on the screen at all.

## What we don't know yet

The CMA can still change the proposal before its final decision. It hasn't set an implementation date, though the consultation proposes that the rules come into force within six months of being imposed. Nobody knows yet which assistants will pass the eligibility tests, which Google would apply every six months under the CMA's rules. The user research was qualitative, so it tells you how people behave on the screens rather than how many will switch. The CMA also expects Google to show the annual prompt as part of major Chrome and Android updates, so the timing will vary from device to device.
