The gravel opportunity

Nearly half of adults say safety on the roads stops them cycling more. Gravel is cycling without traffic, and it is the one discipline still growing in the UK. This report uses five years of data to show where gravel could get more people riding, more active and spending time outdoors.

11 October 2026Global, UK focus2021 to 2026By Manual Focus

00Scope and data sources

This report compares the four disciplines most British riders choose between, which are gravel, road, track and mountain bike, over the five years from autumn 2021. It looks at global data first and then at the UK, and sets out where the opportunity lies to get more people on bikes, more active and outdoors.

01Executive summary

England has 11.8 million inactive adults, a quarter of the population doing less than 30 minutes of activity a week. Leisure cycling has fallen from 7.3 million people in 2019/20 to about 6.1 million. When the Department for Transport asks why people do not cycle more, 48% name safety. When it asks what would get them riding, 52% say off-road and segregated paths. Gravel is riding off-road, away from traffic, in forests and on byways and quiet lanes. It is also the one cycling discipline that has grown through the post-pandemic slump.

In a study of 264,337 UK adults, people who cycled to work had 41% lower all-cause mortality and 46% lower heart disease than those who did not. Natural England finds 89% of people who spend time outdoors say it is good for their mental health, and 62% of those who did not get outside wished they had. Gravel puts both on the same ride, which is hard exercise in green space with no cars.

The UCI Gravel World Series went from 11 events in 2022 to 47 in 32 countries in 2026. UK gravel bike sales are up 284% on pre-Covid, about 30,000 a year, while the wider bike market fell to its lowest level in around 50 years. Scotland's Gralloch grew from 1,230 riders in 2023 to more than 3,000 in 2026, and Dirty Reiver sold out in six hours. Gravel's share of UK search interest in racing across the four disciplines rose from 8% to 42% in five years, and searches for gravel races and gravel events rose by 37% to 49% in three years while road event searches fell by 60% or more.

The opportunity covers people, places and products. The people include would-be riders put off by traffic, the 1.2 million adults who stopped riding for leisure, and the 26% of women who do not cycle but would like to. On places, England's forests take 313 million visits a year, cyclists can legally use only 22% of the rights of way network in England and Wales, and Scotland's access rights already open most land to responsible riding. On products, the UK gravel scene is built around experienced, mostly male riders, with almost nothing designed for beginners.

British Cycling and the other organisations that could grow gravel participation have done little so far. British Cycling recognised gravel as a racing category in mid-2025, held its first National Gravel Championships in August 2026, and does not mention gravel in its strategy to 2029. Its website earns about 333 search visits a month from gravel terms, against 8,815 for mountain biking. The growth so far has come from independent organisers, retailers and brands. Growing participation will need governing bodies, land managers, health partners and the industry to work together.

Health and economic value

  • Health. The Walking and Cycling Index 2025 values walking, wheeling and cycling across 17 UK areas at £9.56bn a year, saving the NHS £346.7m and preventing 28,037 serious conditions. These figures cover all modes, not cycling alone.
  • Rural economy. Scotland's mountain bike sector, the closest off-road precedent, generated £105m of GVA in 2015 and was projected to reach £158m by 2025. Gravel events bring riders to the same kind of rural places. Unbound's 2025 field brought an estimated $21.8m to one Kansas county.
  • Industry. Gravel is the bike category that has held up through the downturn in the UK, Germany and Austria, so growth in riders feeds straight into shops and brands.

04What gravel cycling is and how it grew

Gravel is drop-bar riding on mixed surfaces, including forest roads, byways, farm tracks and quiet lanes. It sits between road and mountain bike. The bike looks like a road bike with wider tyres and more clearance, and it works as an all-rounder. The racing ranges from short UCI qualifiers to 200-mile endurance events, and most events let amateurs start alongside professionals.

How the discipline grew

  • Events came before governing bodies. The format came from the US Midwest. Unbound Gravel had 34 starters in 2006 and has run with a field of about 5,000 since 2024. The UCI created its Gravel World Series and World Championships in 2022, 16 years after Unbound began.
  • Industry investment. SRAM launched its XPLR gravel groupset in August 2021 and Shimano launched GRX Di2 1x12 in June 2025. Specialized topped Strava's gravel bike rankings in 2024.
  • Strava activity. Strava's 2023 report recorded a 55% rise in athletes logging a gravel ride, against 13% for mountain biking and 23% for e-bikes.
  • Professional racing and prize money. The Life Time Grand Prix purse rose from $250,000 in 2022 to $590,000 for 2026. WorldTour riders now race gravel worlds, and in the US the number of UCI-level road teams fell from 19 to 11 over a decade while gravel grew.
!

Signs of slowing growth. Unbound has plateaued at about 5,000 riders. SBT GRVL was capped at 1,800 by its county permit for 2025. The Gravel Earth series cut its calendar from 25 events to 12 for 2026. The 2026 World Championships in Australia drew only three WorldTour men. Some established US events have reached their ceiling, but the UCI series, The Gralloch and German and Austrian gravel bike sales all kept growing in 2025 and 2026, so the growth is now strongest in Europe and the UK.

06Who rides gravel

No one publishes a demographic profile of UK gravel riders, so this section uses event and federation data. It should be tested against British Cycling's own member data and event entries.

  • First-time racers. More than 40% of the 1,230 riders at the first Gralloch in 2023 had never raced gravel before. Those riders are potential new members for any governing body that offers them something.
  • Gender balance. Women were 25% of Unbound's 2025 field. British road racing had 704 women among just under 4,000 racers in 2025, about 18%. SBT GRVL's 2020 edition sold out in under 25 minutes with 75% more women than the year before.
  • British riders at the World Championships. 345 British riders entered the 2025 Gravel World Championships in the Netherlands, more than from Germany, France or the USA. They qualified through UCI series events, and the only one in the UK is The Gralloch.
  • Travel and tourism. The Gralloch drew 47 nations in 2026, The Traka expected riders of 74 nationalities, and Unbound's 2025 field brought an estimated $21.8m of economic impact to one Kansas county. Gravel riders travel for events, which makes them relevant to regional funding and tourism partners.
i

Gap in British Cycling's offer. Gravel riders are part of cycling's existing audience, but British Cycling offers them no gravel membership, series or beginner rides.

The UK's gravel calendar was built by independent organisers, mostly in Scotland and the north, without British Cycling. That is a strength, because the events are good and riders trust them. It is also British Cycling's opportunity, because those organisers face the same problems road organisers do, which are insurance, land access, rising costs and marketing, and they need a governing body that helps rather than charges.

!

Lessons from road racing. Road race organisers have blamed rising costs for safety, insurance and policing, and circuit races fell from about 700 to 600 between 2022 and 2025. Building organiser support into gravel from the start would help it avoid the same decline.

13SWOT analysis of gravel as a route into cycling

Strengths

  • Traffic-free, which answers the safety worry 48% of adults give for not cycling more
  • Exercise and time in nature on the same ride
  • The only bike category above pre-Covid sales in the UK, with flagship events selling out in hours
  • A growing global structure, from 11 to 47 UCI series events in four years

Weaknesses

  • Male-dominated today, with 76% of Scottish forest cyclists male in the last survey
  • Cyclists can use only 22% of rights of way in England and Wales
  • No UK participation data specific to gravel
  • Almost no beginner products, and governing body structure that is one year old

Opportunities

  • 52% of adults say off-road paths would get them cycling more
  • 26% of women would like to cycle but do not
  • 313 million visits a year to England's public forests, an audience already outdoors
  • Health, tourism and land partners all have reasons to back it

Threats

  • Growth stays racing-led and never reaches beginners
  • Access conflicts with walkers, riders and landowners if numbers rise without management
  • Event fatigue and plateaus, as seen with Unbound and the Gravel Earth series
  • Household budgets squeezed, as British Cycling's own membership fall shows

14Recommendations

The five recommendations are in order, because each one makes the next easier. None of them touches the UK Sport funding for the Olympic programme, which is tied to Olympic disciplines.

Priority 1 · Strategy

Add gravel to participation plans with targets

British Cycling and Sport England should name gravel in their participation plans, with measures attached. The right measures are inactive adults brought into regular riding, the share of women and first-time riders, and the number of beginner rides and routes, as well as race entries.

Priority 2 · Access

Open and signpost places to ride

Waymarked beginner gravel loops at public forests, starting from car parks with bike hire. Template agreements with Forestry England, Forestry and Land Scotland and private estates. Support for Cycling UK's case for wider legal access to rights of way in England and Wales, where cyclists can use only 22% of the network today.

Priority 3 · Beginners

Build the beginner pathway

Led rides, skills sessions and women-led groups, run through clubs, shops and forests. The 29% of women who cite confidence as a barrier need skills and company more than they need a race. Shops selling 30,000 gravel bikes a year are the natural place to start.

Priority 4 · Events

Add beginner and non-competitive event formats

Short, non-competitive distances and women's starts at existing events, then a national series for those who want to race, with The Gralloch as the UCI qualifier. Organisers need insurance that fits mixed-surface events and support with land access, so that gravel does not repeat road racing's cost spiral.

Priority 5 · Search

Improve search visibility for gravel

A UK gravel route and event finder, a beginner hub and regional guides. Today the searches where new riders start go to listing sites, organisers and retailers. British Cycling's domain already ranks and earns AI citations when the content exists, so the shortfall is content.
