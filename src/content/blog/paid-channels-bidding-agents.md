Platform bidding agents such as Google's Smart Bidding now set the bid in each auction they enter, so the human job in paid media has moved to the inputs and the goal. Feed the agent varied creative and clean conversion data. Then point it at the deepest outcome you can measure, such as profit margin instead of clicks.

## Key takeaways

- Google's Smart Bidding uses Google AI to optimise for conversions or conversion value in every auction, which Google calls auction-time bidding.
- Performance Max runs one goal-based campaign across all Google Ads inventory, from Search to YouTube.
- Google rates landing page experience as one of three Quality Score components, so the page after the click is part of the paid job.
- Google's value-based bidding lets you bid for profit margin instead of revenue, which makes the choice of goal a strategic call.
- The human job is the negative space the agent can't see, such as budget spent winning brand searches you would win anyway.

Most paid media decisions are now made by machines on both sides of the auction. Your bidding agent faces Google's and Meta's, while your creative competes with thousands of AI-generated variants. The people in the loop set the constraints more than they make the calls.

That changes what's worth focusing on. Most paid teams still spend their time on the wrong layer.

## How do platform bidding agents work?

The platforms run optimisation against signals you barely see. Google's [Performance Max](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10724817?hl=en-GB) is a goal-based campaign type that reaches all Google Ads inventory from one campaign. Its bids come from [Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882?hl=en-GB), which uses Google AI to optimise for conversions or conversion value in every auction. Meta's [Advantage+](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/meta-advantage-plus) applies AI to targeting and budget across Facebook and Instagram.

These systems behave like agents. You give them creative and a goal, then they test constantly and decide where the money goes.

### What do bidding agents reward?

**Creative volume.** The agent needs more variants, more often. Static rotations stall because the model iterates against whatever it's given.

**Signal density.** Conversion events and first-party data feed the auction. Brands with clean data pipes outbid brands with the same budget and worse signal.

**Landing experience.** Smart Bidding bids on the probability that a click converts, so a great ad with a poor landing page is worse than a good ad with a strong one. Google also rates landing page experience as one of three [Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118?hl=en-GB) components, defined as how relevant and useful your landing page is to people who click your ad.

## Why the manual playbook stalls

The old manual playbook of fixed audiences and fixed creative, reviewed weekly, is losing ground. The agents can't optimise against creative that doesn't change. The platforms can also test more variants than a human team can produce alone.

Brands using AI to produce creative at platform tempo are pulling ahead of brands still paying agencies to ship a static quarter's worth of assets. The same logic is spreading to newer surfaces such as [ads in Google's AI Mode](/blog/google-ai-mode-ads-uk-paid-search/). Our post on [the first ChatGPT ads returns study](/blog/chatgpt-ads-returns-vs-google-search-study/) covers what a paid ChatGPT visit is worth against one from Google Search.

## Where should paid teams focus?

**Creative volume at brand quality.** This is the hard part. AI can produce a hundred variants, though most break the brand or break taste. The win is a pipeline that produces a hundred variants above the floor of brand quality you accept. That takes a workflow rather than a tool. The [five workflow changes that get content production to this floor](/blog/five-ways-improve-content-production-ai/) start with the brand-voice prompt, which carries the most weight.

**Feed and signal hygiene.** Check your product feed and your event taxonomy. The agents are only as good as the inputs, which most brands haven't invested in for years.

**Outcome design.** Decide what the agent is allowed to optimise for, from a click down to lifetime value. The further down the funnel you can give the agent a real signal, the better it steers spend. Google's [value-based bidding guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14792795?hl=en-GB) lets you choose the value it maximises, such as sales revenue or profit margins. That's a strategic call rather than a media-buying one. The [paid-search bidding agent](/lens/demand/paid-search-bidding-agent/) playbook in The Lens sets targets on contribution margin rather than revenue.

**The negative space.** Humans add value in what the agent can't see, such as competitor moves and channel cannibalisation. The senior call is "don't let the bidding agent buy you out of your own brand search" or "don't let the optimisation drift the brand for a 2% lift in last-click ROAS". The [attribution tear-down](/lens/demand/attribution-teardown/) playbook shows how to find the channels that overclaim.

## Which bid goal fits your data?

The right goal depends on the deepest outcome you can measure reliably.

| What you can measure | Goal to give the agent |
|---|---|
| No reliable conversions | Fix tracking before Smart Bidding |
| Conversions without values | Maximise conversions or Target CPA |
| Revenue per conversion | Maximise conversion value or Target ROAS |
| Margin per product | Value-based bidding on profit margin |
| Customer lifetime value | Value-based bidding on lifetime value |

## What does a good paid team look like?

A good paid function now pairs a small operator team with a creative AI pipeline, fed by clean data and pointed at clear goals. Our view at Manual Focus is that the agency model where you pay a percentage of media spend for a buyer making manual bid changes is dying. Whatever tools you're considering for the creative pipeline, [run them through a four-week pilot before committing](/blog/evaluating-ai-tools-without-falling-for-the-demo/), because most don't survive contact with a real team.

Spend should compound. Each week the model should learn more as the creative evolves, so a paid function that isn't getting smarter every week has a broken loop.

The brands that win paid in the AI era won't necessarily spend more. They'll have better inputs and senior judgement about what to optimise for.
