A Manual Focus Basics episode. James and Hannah go through public relations
from the start: the CIPR's definition, the disciplines PR covers, how it
differs from advertising, and what makes a story worth covering, with Seth
Godin's Purple Cow as the test. Hannah takes listeners through Muck Rack's
State of Journalism 2026 survey of 897 journalists, including the 88% who
disregard pitches that miss their beat and the four things journalists value
in a pitch. They cover how AI has changed both sides of the inbox, why press
releases have changed jobs rather than died, and how to measure PR by whether
opinion moved.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first and third
need an assistant that can browse the web, and the third needs one that can
run on a monthly schedule, or you can run it by hand.

### 1. Build the beat list before you pitch

```text
I work in PR for [brand], which [one line on what it does]. The story I want
to pitch, in one sentence, is: [story].

Our audience reads these publications: [list].

For each publication, find the journalists who have written about [subject]
in the last three months, by reading their recent articles.

Give me a table with the columns: Journalist | Publication | Beat in a few
words | Two recent articles on the subject (title and date) | Why this story
would matter to their readers | Fit (strong / possible / weak).

Leave out anyone whose recent work doesn't cover the subject. Don't guess
email addresses or beats. If you couldn't open a publication or find recent
articles, say so in a note under the table.
```

### 2. Test whether the story is worth pitching

```text
Before we write a press release, I want an honest check on whether this story
is worth a journalist's time.

The story: [describe it].
The evidence behind it: [data, spokesperson, images, dates].
Who it's for: [the publications or audience].

In Muck Rack's 2026 survey, journalists said the most valuable parts of a pitch
were clear relevance to their beat, access to relevant sources, original
research or data, and high-resolution images.

Score the story from 1 to 5 on each of those four, with a sentence on why.
Then answer plainly: is anything here new and specific enough that someone
outside the company would mention it to a friend? If not, say so, and suggest
what evidence or angle would make it a story. Don't invent data or quotes to
fill a gap.
```

### 3. Check what AI assistants say about the brand

```text
Run this on the first working day of each month.

Brand: [brand], website [website], category [category]. Main competitors:
[list].

Ask each of these questions in the AI answer tools you can reach:
[paste 10 questions customers ask about the category or the brand].

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Question | Tool | How the answer
describes our brand (quote it) | Accurate? (yes / partly / no, and what's
wrong) | Sources cited | Did any recent coverage of ours appear as a source?

Then list anything inaccurate that's repeated across answers. If a tool isn't
available to you or won't answer, say so in its row rather than guessing.
```
