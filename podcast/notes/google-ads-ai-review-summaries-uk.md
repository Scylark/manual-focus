James and Hannah talk through Google's new help page on AI review summaries in
Search ads, first reported on 24 October 2026. Google's AI now writes
positive-only summaries of online reviews into Search ads in the US, UK,
Australia and India, advertisers can't edit them, and the page describes no
opt-out. They cover how the feature grew out of a year of tests, why rule 3.46
of the UK CAP Code raises a question for positive-only summaries, what it does
to brand and paid teams, and three jobs to hand to AI this week.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first two need
file uploads or pasted text. The third needs an assistant that can run on a
schedule and read uploaded screenshots.

### 1. Read your reviews the way Google will

```text
I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell] at [website], selling
mainly in [market].

Google now adds AI-generated, positive-only summaries of online reviews to
Search ads, drawn from reviews across the web. Advertisers can't edit them. I
want to know what those summaries are likely to say about us, and whether each
point is something we would be comfortable claiming in an ad.

Attached are the last six months of our reviews from [list the review sites
that rank for our brand name], as exports or pasted text.

1. Find the ten positive themes that come up most often. Group by meaning, not
   wording.
2. For each theme, give me a table with the columns: Theme | Number of reviews
   mentioning it | Two short example quotes | Would this read as a factual
   claim in an ad (yes/no) | Risk note (for example a price, a result, a
   safety or health point, or a comparison with a competitor).
3. Then list the five most common negative themes, with counts, so we can see
   what a positive-only summary would leave out.

Only use the reviews I've given you. If a review is ambiguous or you can't tell
which product it refers to, say so rather than guessing.
```

### 2. Draft the letters to Google and to legal

```text
I run marketing for [brand] in [market]. Google has published a Google Ads help
page, "About Review summaries", describing AI-generated, positive-only
summaries of online reviews that appear in Search ads. I've pasted the page
below, along with the UK CAP Code rules 1.8 and 3.44 to 3.50.

[paste the help page text]
[paste the CAP Code rules]

Write two drafts.

1. An email to our Google Ads representative asking, in numbered questions,
   whether account-level automated asset settings switch review summaries off,
   which review sources feed them, whether reporting will show when a summary
   ran, and how we can dispute a summary that misstates our reviews. Plain,
   polite, under 200 words.
2. A one-page brief for our legal team that sets out what the help page says,
   quotes the relevant CAP Code rules, and lists the questions we need them to
   answer for our category, [category]. Don't give a legal opinion. Mark
   anything the help page doesn't say as "not stated by Google".
```

### 3. Log the summaries you see each week

```text
I run marketing for [brand] in the UK. Each week a colleague searches our top
brand and category terms on Google and screenshots any of our Search ads that
show an AI-generated review summary. I'll upload those screenshots each week.

Our no-go claims list is below.
[paste the list of prices, comparisons, results and phrases our brand can't
use]

Each week:
1. Read the review summary text in each screenshot and log it in a table with
   the columns: Date | Search term | Summary text | New this week (yes/no) |
   Conflicts with a no-go claim (yes/no, and which).
2. Flag any summary that makes a factual claim about price, results, safety or
   a competitor.
3. Keep a running list of every distinct summary seen so far.

If text in a screenshot is cut off or unreadable, say so rather than filling
it in. Run this as a weekly routine.
```
