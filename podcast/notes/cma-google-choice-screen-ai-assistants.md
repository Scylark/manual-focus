James and Hannah talk through the Competition and Markets Authority's proposal
of 23 September 2026: Google would have to show UK Android and Chrome users a
choice of search service at set-up and every year after, with AI assistants
such as ChatGPT and Perplexity eligible if they search the web and credit and
link to their sources. They cover what the consultation document adds to the
press release, why the CMA's own user research points to a slow drift rather
than a rush away from Google, why the linking requirement makes AI citations
worth more, what's still undecided, and three jobs to hand to AI now.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first works best
with browsing (or with answers you paste in) and should be re-run each
quarter; the second and third need either browsing or the files and page text
uploaded.

### 1. Build your AI citation baseline

```text
I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell], at [website], selling
mainly in the UK.

Here is our product or service range and the questions our sales and service
teams hear most often: [paste or attach].

Step 1: Write the 20 most commercially important questions a buyer would ask
an AI assistant before choosing a supplier like us, in the buyer's own words.
List them and explain in one line why each one matters commercially.

Step 2: For each question, check the answer in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini,
Copilot and Claude. If you can't reach one of these yourself, tell me which,
and I will paste its answers in for you to log.

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Question | Assistant | Brands cited,
in order | Brands linked to | Are we cited? (yes/no) | Are we linked?
(yes/no) | Which of our pages is linked, if any.

Finish with a summary: for each assistant, how many of the 20 questions cite
us, and which competitors are cited most. Don't guess at any answer you
couldn't see; mark it "not checked". I will re-run this every quarter, so keep
the format identical.
```

### 2. Check AI assistants can read your site

```text
Our website is [website]. Below are our robots.txt file and a list of our most
important page URLs: [paste robots.txt and the URL list].

1. Read the robots.txt and tell me which AI crawlers it allows and which it
   blocks, quoting the exact lines that do it.
2. For each URL, check whether the main content is in the HTML itself or only
   appears once JavaScript runs, and whether the page loads for you at all.

Give me two tables:
- AI crawlers: Crawler | Allowed or blocked | Line in robots.txt
- Key pages: URL | Loads? (yes/no) | Main content in plain HTML? (yes/no/
  partly) | Notes

We may also have bot rules set in our CDN, which you can't see. List what I
should check there, and say clearly anything you couldn't open or test rather
than assuming it works.
```

### 3. Rewrite your top pages to answer first

```text
Here are our [number] most commercially important pages, with their URL,
the question each one should answer, and the current page text: [paste or
attach].

For each page, draft a new opening paragraph (under 80 words) that gives the
direct answer to its question in the first sentence, then supports it with the
specific numbers and sources already on the page, so an AI assistant could
quote it and check it.

Give me, for each page: URL | Question it answers | Current opening paragraph
| New opening paragraph | Numbers and sources used, and where on the page
they came from.

Use only facts that are in the page text I've given you. If the page has no
number or source to support the answer, say so instead of inventing one, and
flag it as a gap for us to fill.
```

### And one for you

Set up a segment in your analytics for referral traffic from chatgpt.com,
perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and claude.ai, so you
can see AI referrals grow if people start switching. It needs a sign-in to
your analytics, so it stays with a person.
