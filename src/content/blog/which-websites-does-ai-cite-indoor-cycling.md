On 19 October 2026 Manual Focus asked six AI engines 309 UK questions about indoor cycling, three times each. In the 4,379 answers to unbranded questions, a few buyer's guides carried most of the citations. BikeRadar's smart trainer guide alone was cited in 55% of smart trainer answers, more than any brand's own website.

The [first write-up of the study](/blog/which-indoor-cycling-brands-does-ai-recommend/) covered which brands AI names. When [BikeBiz reported the results on 23 October](https://bikebiz.com/new-study-shows-wahoo-smart-trainers-most-recommended-to-uk-shoppers-by-ai-but-what-does-that-mean-for-your-strategy/), it asked James what they mean for brand strategy. His answer started with the sources AI cites, so this post goes through them page by page and engine by engine, using the same data as the [interactive report](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/).

## Key takeaways

- BikeRadar's best smart trainer guide was cited in 55% of AI answers to unbranded UK smart trainer questions.
- Cyclingnews was the most cited site in the study, in 45% of all answers. At least one of the three biggest cycling titles appeared in 65%.
- Perplexity cited at least one of those three titles in 90% of its answers. Google AI Mode did so in 26%.
- YouTube appeared in 65% of Google AI Overviews but in only 2 of ChatGPT's 780 answers.
- Pages on Rouvy's own blog were cited in 30% of training app answers, more often than Cyclist or road.cc.

## Which pages does AI cite most?

Across the study, 92% of answers cited at least one website. Between them the engines cited 1,112 different domains. Most of that list is long tail, since the ten most cited domains made up 42% of all citations. In each category, one or two buyer's guides turned up in about half the answers.

| Category | Most cited page | Share of answers | Second page | Share of answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smart trainers | BikeRadar, best smart trainers | 55% | Cyclingnews, best turbo trainers | 49% |
| Training apps | Cyclingnews, indoor cycling apps | 64% | Cycling Weekly, indoor cycling apps | 52% |
| Smart bikes | Cyclingnews, best smart bikes | 53% | Cycling Weekly, best smart bikes | 49% |

Each share is the proportion of answers in that category that cited the page at least once.

[![The websites AI engines cite most for indoor cycling questions](/images/ai-shortlist/sources.svg)](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/#sources)

For a trainer brand, the [BikeRadar guide](https://www.bikeradar.com/advice/buyers-guides/best-smart-trainer) is close to a single point of failure. A product missing from it is missing from the page most AI answers lean on.

## Does every engine use the same sources?

No. Perplexity and Gemini cite the most sites per answer and lean hardest on cycling media. Google's two search engines rely on those titles least.

| Engine | Answers citing a site | Sites cited per answer | Cites one of the big three titles | Cites YouTube |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perplexity | 100% | 7.7 | 90% | 0% |
| Gemini | 92% | 6.1 | 83% | 6% |
| ChatGPT | 97% | 5.7 | 73% | Under 1% |
| Claude | 91% | 5.0 | 67% | 0% |
| Google AI Overviews | 99% | 5.2 | 42% | 65% |
| Google AI Mode | 74% | 2.2 | 26% | 20% |

The big three are the most cited titles overall, Cyclingnews first, then BikeRadar, then Cycling Weekly. AI Overviews figures cover the 479 answers where Google showed an overview.

Google AI Mode is the outlier. A quarter of its answers cite no site at all. Its most cited source is Cyclist, in 23% of answers, rather than any of the big three. The BikeRadar trainer guide that Perplexity cited in 71% of trainer answers appeared in 4% of AI Mode's.

## Where do YouTube and Reddit fit?

Mostly inside Google Search. Video and forum sites made up 3% to 5% of all citations in the [original study](/blog/which-indoor-cycling-brands-does-ai-recommend/), but that average hides a split. In Google AI Overviews, community sites made up 21% of everything cited. YouTube was cited in 309 of 479 overviews, Reddit in 94.

Outside Google Search, the picture reverses. Claude cited neither site in any of its 780 answers. Perplexity never cited YouTube. Reddit's own plans make this harder to predict, as we covered in [Reddit's move to a logged-in-only site](/blog/reddit-logged-in-only-search-referrals-marketers/).

So a brand that relies only on written reviews misses much of what AI Overviews cite, while one that relies only on YouTube creators gets little from Claude or Perplexity.

## Can a brand's own site get cited?

Yes, though how often depends heavily on the engine. Brand websites made up 41% of the sites ChatGPT cited, the highest of any engine. It cited zwift.com in 37% of its answers and Wahoo's UK site in 25%. Claude went the other way, with brand sites at 10% of its citations.

Rouvy was the most cited brand website in the study, in 27% of all answers. Pages on its blog were cited in 30% of app answers, more often than Cyclist at 23% or road.cc at 18%. Perplexity cited them in 67% of its app answers and Claude in 52%. Rouvy was named in 89% of the app answers that cited one of those pages, against 68% of those that didn't. That gap is a correlation, since an engine that already favours Rouvy may also pick its pages.

The most cited of those pages reads like a buyer's guide. It compares Rouvy with rivals such as Zwift and TrainerRoad, in the same format as the cycling media guides the engines cite. Structure helps too, as the [SISTRIX citation study](/blog/sistrix-ai-citation-study-geo-tactics/) found tables of contents went with more AI citations across 96,418 URLs.

## Where do retailers come in?

At the point of purchase. Across every other buying moment, retailers supplied 3% to 12% of cited sites. For UK buying questions, such as where to buy a trainer in the UK, their share rose to 31%.

| Retailer | Share of UK buying answers citing it |
|---|---|
| Fitshop | 22% |
| Decathlon | 19% |
| Tredz | 19% |
| Sigma Sports | 18% |
| Halfords | 8% |

A brand's distribution shows up in AI answers through these pages, so current prices and stock on those listings matter most here.

## What should brand teams do?

Our view at Manual Focus is that an indoor cycling brand should treat a short list of buyer's guides as its most important AI channel, because the top guides appear in more answers than any brand site or retailer.

1. List the guides. Take the pages in the tables above and the [report's sources section](/reports/ai-shortlist-indoor-cycling/#sources), then check whether your products are in each one with current models and prices.
2. Get reviewed where it counts. Put review samples and briefings in front of the editors behind those guides before chasing wider coverage. The [earned media pitch generator](/lens/content/earned-media-pitch-generator/) helps plan the story. [Earned channels in the AI era](/blog/earned-channels-pr-in-the-ai-era/) covers how journalists now work.
3. Cover video for Google. If AI Overviews matter to you, make sure honest video reviews of your product exist on YouTube, since 65% of the overviews in this study cited it.
4. Publish one honest comparison page. Rouvy's most cited page compares it with named rivals, which a page about one product can't do.
5. Check by engine. Run your own top questions in Perplexity and Google AI Mode at least, since they cite the most different sources. [Manual Focus Light Meter](/light-meter/) does this at scale, three runs per question, with the sources logged.

Bing's [Topics Portfolio report](/blog/bing-topics-portfolio-ai-citation-gaps/) also shows where other sites get cited and yours does not, which is a free place to start.

## What this cut can't tell you

The data shows which pages AI cites. It can't show why, or whether a citation changes what a rider buys. Logged-in users may see different sources. Perplexity and Claude were measured through their APIs, which can differ from their apps. A buyer's guide can be rewritten next month, so these shares describe October 2026 only.
