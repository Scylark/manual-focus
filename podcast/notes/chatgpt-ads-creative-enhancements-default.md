James and Hannah talk through OpenAI's decision to switch on creative
enhancements in ChatGPT Ads by default from 29 October 2026, which lets the
system rewrite headlines and descriptions, translate copy, resize images and
change landing pages, with no approval step for each version. They cover how
the setting changed since its opt-in launch on 16 September, why the help
centre and OpenAI's email give different dates, why the CAP Code leaves UK
brands responsible for what runs, and why a duplicate campaign isn't a fair
test. Then three jobs to hand to AI before the switch.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first needs an
assistant that can browse the web, the second needs file uploads and works
best set to run weekly, and the third needs file uploads.

### 1. Audit the pages an enhanced ad could land on

```text
I run marketing for [brand], [one line on what you sell] at [website], selling
mainly in [market].

From 29 October 2026, ChatGPT Ads can change an ad's destination URL to
another "relevant page" on our site, and it reads our landing pages for
prices, promotions, product details and availability when it rewrites ads. I
want to find pages that could mislead a customer if an ad sent them there.

Attached are:
- the list of destination URLs in our ChatGPT Ads campaigns, with the
  campaign name for each
- our sitemap, or a crawl export of the site

For each campaign, look at the destination URL and the pages in the same
section of the site (same folder, category or product range). Check each page
and flag any that show:
1. an offer, discount or promotion with an end date that has passed
2. a product marked out of stock or discontinued
3. no analytics or tracking tag that you can detect

Give me a table with the columns: Campaign | URL | Issue (expired offer, out
of stock, no tracking) | What the page says | Suggested fix.

Group the rows by campaign. If you couldn't open a page, or couldn't tell
whether tracking is present, say so in the row rather than guessing.
```

### 2. Check a weekly sample of live ads against your brand rules

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every Monday morning.

We advertise [brand] in ChatGPT Ads with creative enhancements switched on
for one test campaign, so some versions of our ads are written by the
platform and nobody at the brand approves them first.

Here is our list of claims, prices and phrases the brand can't use:
[paste list, for example banned comparisons, words like "free" or "cheapest",
health or performance claims, prices we no longer charge].

Attached is this week's sample of live ads (headline, description, landing
page and the date seen).

Check every ad against the list. Give me a table with the columns: Date seen |
Headline | Description | Landing page | Pass or fail | The words that broke
the rule | Which rule.

Then list any ad that doesn't break a rule but makes a claim our landing page
doesn't support, with the wording and the page. Don't judge tone or style
unless it breaks a listed rule. If an ad is in a language you can't read
reliably, mark it "needs a human check".
```

### 3. Draft the test plan for finance

```text
We want to test whether ChatGPT Ads' creative enhancements lower our cost per
conversion before we switch them on beyond one campaign. A duplicate campaign
with the setting off isn't a fair comparison, because the two campaigns
compete in the same auctions and can be shown in different conversations. I
need a holdout or geo-lift test plan that our finance director will accept.

Attached are:
- our ChatGPT Ads conversions and spend by week for the campaigns in scope
- the regions we sell in, with each one's share of sales

Draft a one-page test plan with these sections: Question we're answering |
Design (holdout or geo-lift, and why) | How the regions or audiences are split
| Length of the test | Budget | The result that would count as a win, and the
result that would count as no effect | Risks to the result.

Base the test length on our actual weekly conversion volumes, and say plainly
if our volumes are too low for a reliable answer in a reasonable time. List
anything the plan assumes that you couldn't check in the files.
```
