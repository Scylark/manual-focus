The Rider Panel is the market research part of [Light Meter](/light-meter/). It builds a living market of shoppers before a product launches. Each shopper is an AI agent with a written life story and a budget to stick to. They live in real UK towns, through that town's real weather. They see every rival product at its real price, with its real Black Friday deal. They only come across the brands someone like them would meet. Then we put a launch in front of them. Each one decides, month by month, whether to buy.

For our first mock study, we recruited 500 UK cyclists who are shopping for an indoor trainer this season, from a potentially limitless pool of shoppers the Rider Panel has access to. Five hundred is enough to tell a real change from noise.

We gave them a new folding smart trainer at £499. With the big reviews out at launch, 26 of them bought it. Without reviews, 3 did. At £399 for Black Friday, 41 did. Then every one of them told us why.

## Key takeaways

- Without independent reviews, a new trainer brand sold to 3 shoppers in 500 instead of 26. Nobody wanted to be the guinea pig.
- A £399 Black Friday price took it to 41 buyers with 38% more revenue. The shoppers had asked for it.
- Being named by AI engines added a little, from 26 buyers to 33.
- Buyers chose it because it folds away and it's quiet. Most of them had nowhere to keep a trainer.
- Shoppers who walked away were waiting for a sale or didn't trust a brand they'd never heard of.
- The 500 shoppers were recruited from a potentially limitless pool. A brand's study can be as big as the question needs.

## What is the Rider Panel?

Market research usually means asking real people what they would do. That takes weeks to recruit and costs thousands of pounds per study, so most brands only do it for their biggest launches. Everything else gets decided on instinct.

The Rider Panel is part of [Light Meter](/light-meter/), our research tool. Light Meter's AI visibility studies show what AI tells shoppers. The Rider Panel shows what shoppers then do.

Each shopper is an AI agent. A brand describes a launch. Every shopper lives the season month by month and decides whether to buy. Each one explains the choice in their own words. Shoppers who walk away also say what would have changed their mind. It takes days rather than months. It doesn't replace real customers. It shows which plans are worth taking to them.

The panel has no fixed size. We can recruit as many shoppers as a question needs from the same pool, for whichever market it's about.

## Who the 500 shoppers are

They're the people who go shopping for an indoor trainer this season, not every cyclist. Most cyclists never think about a trainer in a given year. A panel of all cyclists would put people in the market who'd never be there.

We built 200,000 UK cyclists first, using survey data. Sport England's [Active Lives survey](https://www.sportengland.org/research-and-data/data/active-lives/active-lives-data-tables) sets who rides. The [English Housing Survey](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2023-to-2024-headline-findings-on-demographics-and-household-resilience) decides who rents or owns their home. Each cyclist then got a chance of shopping this season, based on what they ride indoors now and what's wrong with it. We recruited the 500 from everyone who shops. About 2 in 100 adult cyclists shop for a trainer in a season.

| Part of a shopper's world | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Their life | UK survey data, plus a written life story with a reason to start looking |
| Their kit | The bike computer they own, how much its brand matters to them, their cassette and app |
| Weather | Each town's real monthly weather, September 2025 to March 2026, from Open-Meteo |
| The season | The clocks going back, Black Friday, Christmas costs, the January sales |
| Prices and rivals | 10 real rival trainers at UK prices, with Black Friday and January deals |
| What they come across | Brand familiarity from UK search share, plus each shopper's own shops and apps |

## How a Rider Panel study works

To prove the panel, we invented a brand called Northfold and a folding smart trainer, the DD1. Northfold isn't a real company. The DD1 is quiet and folds flat to 14cm deep. Like its main rivals, it comes with the Zwift Cog and Click. It goes on sale on 16 November at £499. Sigma Sports and Tredz stock it, with a display stand in Halfords.

A new brand can't be better known than an established one in its first season. So we capped Northfold's reach at the awareness of JetBlack, a UK challenger brand. About 110 of the 500 shoppers come across it.

Then we ran the season four times on the same 500 shoppers. Their lives stayed the same. So did the weather and every rival's price.

| Run | What changes | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| The launch as planned | £499 all season, reviews out at launch | The baseline |
| Black Friday price | £399 from 20 to 30 November | What a discount is worth |
| No reviews | No independent reviews this season | What reviews are worth |
| Named by AI | AI engines name Northfold in 45% of answers | What AI visibility is worth |

Each shopper keeps a diary. Here's Lee's season at £499, month by month:

> Knee's calming down. Want to get past the wheel-on this winter but the flat has no room. Asked ChatGPT, said Wahoo.
>
> Wife says we can't justify it while my current one works. Leaving it.
>
> Saw Northfold on a road.cc page. New brand, no thanks after the last one I bought let me down.
>
> Black Friday on Amazon. Tempting but we're stretched before Christmas. Skipped.
>
> Christmas costs. Nothing bought.
>
> Post-Christmas sale. KICKR CORE 2 at £439 on Amazon, wife agreed. Bought it.

## The results of four launch plans

| Launch plan | Northfold buyers in 500 | Change vs the £499 launch | Northfold revenue in the panel |
|---|---|---|---|
| £499 all season, reviews out at launch | 26 | | £12,974 |
| £399 for Black Friday | 41 | +15, a real change | £17,859 |
| No independent reviews | 3 | −23, a real change | £1,497 |
| Named by AI engines | 33 | +7, at the edge of the noise | £16,467 |

A change counts as real when it's bigger than the panel's own noise. The rest of this article takes each plan in turn, starting with the people who bought.

## Why shoppers bought the Northfold

22 of the 26 buyers chose Northfold because it folds away. 21 chose it for being quiet. 20 said the independent reviews convinced them. Most of them would have to put a trainer away after every ride.

> "The small flat, the neighbours below and the hall all pointed to something that folds flat and is quiet. The reviews and the 30-day returns eased my worry about a new brand." Gwen Holroyd, 72, Stockport

> "My trouble is space, and nothing else here folds. A three-year warranty and 30-day free returns let me back a new brand." Jonty Ellerby, 37, police officer, York

> "It's genuinely quiet and folds away, and £499 is far less than the Neo or V6 even in the sale. DC Rainmaker's review sold me on the accuracy." Jamal Rowe, 23, Reading

Notice how often a new brand needed a safety net. Reviews kept coming up. So did free returns and long warranties.

## Why shoppers walked away

65 shoppers considered Northfold at £499 and bought something else, or nothing.

| Reason given by the 65 | Shoppers |
|---|---|
| Waiting for a sale | 44 |
| The price | 40 |
| Not sure a new brand will last | 32 |
| Wanted proven accuracy | 10 |
| Money is tight | 9 |

> "Money is tight, so I waited for the Black Friday price on the best-known name. Northfold costs more and had no discount." Maggie Thorne, 54, builder, Islington

> "Northfold got good reviews, but I've been burned by a new brand and I want one with years behind it. The Wahoo is a safe bet." Hugh Aldridge, 47, lecturer, Croydon

> "For a crit racer the V6's 1% accuracy is the point and Wahoo is the name I trust for returns and repairs. The Northfold looked decent but I'd rather not gamble." Samir Haddad, 37, engineer, Greenwich

That turns a sales number into a to-do list. Each of the 65 also told us what would have changed their mind.

> "If the Northfold had joined the Black Friday sales at around £399, I'd have gone for it over the Rivo." Declan Moran, 54, sales manager, Liverpool

> "If a trusted club mate had actually owned a Northfold for six months and it had held up, I'd have taken a proper look." Adrian Kowalczyk, 49, physio, Leeds

## The effect of a Black Friday price

The first action came straight from the shoppers, so we tested it. 44 of the 65 were waiting for a sale. We ran the season again with Northfold at £399 from 20 to 30 November.

Sales rose from 26 to 41. Revenue rose 38%, even after the price cut. 20 shoppers who hadn't bought it at £499 bought it at £399. A few of the £499 buyers went elsewhere, which is the panel's normal variation showing through. 26 of the 41 bought in November. Most of the new buyers would otherwise have bought a Wahoo KICKR CORE 2, which was on its own deal that week.

Part of the gain was visibility. 9 of the 41 buyers first heard of Northfold through a deal list or a deal email. For a brand nobody knows, being on the Black Friday lists puts it in front of shoppers who'd never have come across it.

> "The noise is the whole problem. The quiet Wahoo and Tacx cost £600, and DC Rainmaker called the Northfold genuinely quiet at £399 and it folds away in our tiny flat." Ife Okonkwo, 30, GP, Islington. At £499 he bought a Wahoo KICKR V6.

> "My living room is too small for the old trainer and this one folds flat. At £399 it matched the CORE 2's price, but it solved my space problem." Alasdair Menzies, 34, Perth. At £499 he put off buying altogether.

Hugh Aldridge, who wanted a brand with years behind it, changed his mind too. At £399 he said the reviews and the 30-day returns made him happy to try it despite getting burned once.

## The importance of independent reviews

This was the biggest change in the study. With no independent reviews this season, from BikeRadar to DC Rainmaker, Northfold sold to 3 shoppers instead of 26.

Most buyers first came across Northfold some other way, such as the launch film or a bike shop. But 20 of the 26 said the reviews were what made them buy. Without them, the shoppers who came across it couldn't tell whether a brand they'd never heard of was any good. Of the 23 buyers it lost, 12 bought a Wahoo KICKR CORE 2 instead.

> "Everyone on Reddit and the AI said Wahoo. It comes with the cog so my older bike works, and £439 was fine. Northfold looked interesting but I didn't want to be the guinea pig." Folasade Adeyemi, 38, project manager, Hackney. With reviews out, she bought a Northfold.

> "A BikeRadar or DC Rainmaker review of the Northfold saying it's good would have made it a serious contender at that price." Idris Walcott, 27, Hackney

## The effect of being named by AI

When AI engines name Northfold in 45% of their answers, sales rise from 26 to 33. 8 of the 33 buyers first heard of it from AI.

> "I have to put it away every ride in a rented flat and my bike is 10-speed. The AI pointed me to the fold-flat one with the Cog included and a 30-day return." Catriona Sim, 42, warehouse supervisor, Aberdeen

That fits our [AI Shortlist study](/blog/which-indoor-cycling-brands-does-ai-recommend/). Being named gets a brand onto the list. Reviews close the sale.

## What Northfold should do next

Tested by running the season again:

1. **Have the big reviews out on launch day.** It's the difference between 3 buyers and 26.
2. **Go to £399 for Black Friday.** It added 15 buyers and 38% more revenue.
3. **Work on being named in AI answers**, after the reviews rather than instead of them.

Still to test:

- **Prove the brand will last.** 32 of the shoppers who walked away didn't trust a new brand. Test a 100-day trial with a five-year warranty on every retailer listing.
- **Plan a January offer.** Lee bought his trainer in the January sales. Test £449 through January.
- **Aim at people with no spare room.** Most buyers had to put a trainer away after every ride. Put the fold in the first second of every video.

## What the results mean in real sales

Each of the 500 shoppers stands for about 284 real UK shoppers. That's set so the £499 run's trainer sales match the size of the UK market this season, about 120,000 trainers on our estimate. On that basis, the £499 launch sells about 7,400 trainers, 6% of the season. The £399 Black Friday plan sells about 11,700. Without reviews it sells about 900.

Those totals rest on our market estimate, which comes from market revenue data with low confidence. Allowing for that, the £499 launch could sell anywhere from about 2,900 to 14,400. A brand can swap in its own market figure. The [interactive report](/reports/rider-panel/) has a slider for it.

## How we checked the results

We wrote the pass marks down before any results came in.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Does owning a Garmin or Wahoo computer sway the choice? | No. Swapping it changed choices no more than running the same shopper again (26% against 30%). Passed. |
| Does a field that shouldn't matter change anything? | No. A shopper's main supermarket made no difference to whether they bought. Passed. |
| Do most in-market shoppers buy something? | Yes. 84% bought a trainer. The rest waited for next season or gave up. Passed. |
| Brand shares | Wahoo took 68% of branded trainer sales. Tacx took 9%, just under our 10% floor and below its 19% of searches. Narrow miss. |
| Timing | Black Friday week carried 45% of the season's sales against our 40% limit. Narrow miss. |

Brand familiarity is set from real search share, so the brand-share check confirms the calibration rather than testing the panel independently. Single shoppers change their minds between runs, so every number is a total. When we ran 100 shoppers twice, Northfold buyers came out at 17 then 16. Ranges come from re-sampling the 500 shoppers 4,000 times.

We're testing it in public too. Before Black Friday we registered four forecasts for the real market with an earlier version of the panel, to be scored in December. A prediction for Garmin's real Tacx Nēra launch will be scored in January 2027.

## What the Rider Panel can't tell you

It doesn't replace talking to real customers. It tells you which launch plans are worth taking to them. It shows what each change is worth. It shows why people say no. Its sales totals are only as good as the market estimate behind them.

## Running the Rider Panel outside the UK

Yes. The first panel is UK shoppers because the first study was a UK launch. For another country, we rebuild every input from that country's own sources, from who cycles there to what the shops charge. A brand can test one launch plan in two markets and compare them.
