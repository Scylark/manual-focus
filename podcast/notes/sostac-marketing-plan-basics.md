A Manual Focus Basics episode. James and Hannah walk through SOSTAC, the
six-stage planning model PR Smith created in the 1990s: Situation, Objectives,
Strategy, Tactics, Action and Control. They cover what each stage answers,
how to tell strategy from tactics, why most plans fail in the Action stage,
and why Control is where a plan gets updated rather than scored. Hannah brings
in PR Smith's own 2025 advice on AI and SOSTAC, that any AI-generated plan is a
first draft only, and they map the stages onto the three layers of AI.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first needs an
assistant that can open web pages, or you can paste the competitor pages in.
The third needs one that can run on a monthly schedule, or you can run it by
hand each month.

### 1. Draft the situation analysis

```text
I lead marketing at [company], which sells [what] to [whom] in [market]. I'm
writing our SOSTAC plan for [period] and need a first draft of the Situation
stage, "where are we now?".

Attached are:
- last year's marketing and sales results against our objectives
- our customer research or survey results
- [any other evidence, such as reviews or search data]

Our main competitors are [names and websites].

Write a situation analysis that ends in three to five findings. For each
finding give: the finding in one sentence | the evidence from the attached
files or competitor sites | what it means for next year's plan.

Then add a short section on each competitor: who they target, how they
position themselves and what has changed recently, from their own websites.

List anything you couldn't verify or open separately, rather than guessing.
This is a first draft for my team to challenge, so flag your weakest
finding.
```

### 2. Check a plan against the six stages

```text
Below is our draft marketing plan. I want to check it follows SOSTAC:
Situation (where are we now), Objectives (where do we want to be), Strategy
(how do we get there), Tactics (the marketing mix that carries the strategy),
Action (who does what, when, with what budget) and Control (how we know we're
getting there).

[paste plan]

Give me a table with the columns: Line or section from the plan | The SOSTAC
stage it belongs in | Is it in the right place? | Problem, if any.

Then list:
- tactics written up as strategy
- objectives that describe activity rather than results
- objectives with no measure in Control
- tactics with no named owner or date in Action

Quote the plan's own words. Don't add objectives or tactics we haven't
written; where something is missing, say so.
```

### 3. Run the monthly control report

```text
Run this on the first working day of each month.

Our marketing objectives for [period] are:
[list each objective with its target and date]

Attached are this month's exports: [name the reports, such as web analytics,
CRM and ad platform exports].

Give me a table with the columns: Objective | Target | Where we are now |
Change since last month | On track? (yes/at risk/no) | What drove the change,
if the data shows it.

Then list three questions the team should discuss at this month's review.
If an export is missing or a number can't be calculated from the files, mark
it "not in data" rather than estimating it.
```
