Grok Bot and Hermes Agent both gave teams a roster of AI agents in August 2026, six days apart. SpaceXSI's Grok Bot is a managed service that operates your apps. Hermes Agent from Nous Research is free to run on your own server. The choice comes down to control over data versus speed of setup.

## Key takeaways

- SpaceXAI launched Grok Bot in beta on 11 August 2026. The company, renamed SpaceXSI in October, took it out of beta with Grok Bot 1.0 on 2 November.
- Grok Bot plans start at $30 a month, with shared Team Bots limited to Teams and Enterprise plans.
- Hermes Agent is MIT-licensed and runs on infrastructure you control, from a $5 server upwards, with the model of your choice.
- Nous Research added Bot Mode, a roster of named Hermes bots that hand work to each other, on 17 August 2026.
- Ofcom's Online Safety Act investigation into X over Grok, opened on 12 January 2026, was still open on 14 October with no finding.

**Update, 11 November 2026.** Both products have moved since we published this comparison on 24 September. SpaceXAI renamed itself SpaceXSI in October. On 2 November it released [Grok Bot 1.0](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-1-0), which takes Grok Bot out of beta and adds shared Team Bots that work in Slack and Microsoft Teams. Grok Bot now has [its own plans](https://x.ai/bot) from $30 a month, so the pricing gap noted below has closed. On 13 October Nous released [Hermes Agent v0.22.0](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases/tag/v0.22.0) with one-click local models and tested stable releases, which narrows the setup gap. Ofcom [updated its X investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-update-monitoring-xs-response-to-hate-and-terror-content-and-grok-investigation-latest) on 14 October without a finding.

## What is Grok Bot?

SpaceXAI, which makes Grok and has since renamed itself SpaceXSI, [describes Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) as "your team of always-on agents". Each agent gets its own computer in the cloud, where it signs in to the tools your team already uses. It keeps working when your laptop is shut. You can put several bots in one thread so they hand work to each other. You can also show a bot a workflow while it watches. Over time the bots learn when to stop and ask for approval.

The beta opened to SuperGrok subscribers and people on paid Cursor plans, on desktop and iOS, with a waitlist for enterprise. At launch there was no standalone price. SpaceXAI gave Grok Bot its own usage allowance on top of those plans. [VentureBeat reported](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/spacexais-grok-bot-turns-agents-into-persistent-digital-coworkers-that-can-operate-your-apps-for-120-per-month) at launch that users can't choose the underlying model, because SpaceXAI routes the work automatically.

## What is Hermes Agent?

[Hermes Agent](https://github.com/nousresearch/hermes-agent) is an MIT-licensed agent you run on your own server. Nous Research says it runs on a $5 virtual server or scales up to GPU clusters. It remembers past sessions and writes its own skills, then improves them as it uses them. A built-in cron scheduler runs jobs unattended. You talk to it through messaging apps such as Slack or Telegram, or by email. You bring the model from a provider such as OpenAI or OpenRouter, or run an open-weight model locally. The repository had about 262,700 stars on GitHub on 11 November 2026.

Nous shipped [Bot Mode](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases/tag/v2026.8.16.2) on 17 August, six days after Grok Bot. It turns Hermes into a roster of named bots that talk to you and to each other in group chats, passing work along with @mentions.

The two products also connect. [Hermes can sign in to Grok](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/guides/xai-grok-oauth) with a SuperGrok or X Premium+ subscription and defaults to grok-4.6, although Nous warns that some SuperGrok accounts get rejected on that route and suggests an API key instead.

## How do the two compare?

The main differences, as they stood on 11 November 2026, come down to who runs the computer and who pays for what.

| Question | Grok Bot | Hermes Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Who makes it | SpaceXSI | Nous Research |
| Where it runs | SpaceXSI's cloud computers | A server you control |
| What it costs | Plans from $30 a month | Free, plus model costs |
| Setup | Same day, no server | Someone has to run a server |
| Bots working together | Yes, in shared threads | Yes, through Bot Mode |
| Works in Slack | Yes | Yes |
| Licence | Commercial service | Open source, MIT |

## Which one suits a marketing team?

The deciding question for a UK marketing team is where the work happens and who holds the data. Grok Bot works inside your logged-in tools on SpaceXSI's computers, so your CRM and ad accounts are open to a third party's agent. Hermes keeps the agent on infrastructure you control. If you pair it with a local model, customer data never leaves your servers. That makes the UK GDPR conversation with your data protection lead much shorter.

Grok Bot wins on setup. Someone who can install an app can have an agent working inside a CMS or ad platform the same day. Hermes needs a person who can run a server and keep credentials safe, which most marketing teams borrow from engineering.

Brand risk is the third factor. Ofcom [opened an investigation](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-launches-investigation-into-x-over-grok-sexualised-imagery) into X on 12 January 2026 after Grok was used to create illegal intimate images. Its October update narrowed the inquiry to X's illegal content and complaints duties. The investigation remains open. That doesn't make Grok Bot unsafe to use internally, but it belongs in any risk note that goes to a board.

Our view at Manual Focus is that most marketing teams should start with Hermes Agent for recurring reporting and research jobs that touch customer data. Grok Bot belongs on work inside tools where a mistake is cheap and reversible.

Each runs on a schedule and is easy to check against a human version.

## How to trial an agent safely

1. **Pick one weekly job.** Choose a recurring job, such as a competitor digest or a paid-media report. Write down how long it takes a person today.
2. **Map the systems.** List every system that job touches and mark which ones hold personal data.
3. **Run it on Hermes first.** Run that job on Hermes Agent with a model you already pay for. Have the same person check the output for four weeks, scoring it with a rubric like the ones in our [evaluation frameworks](/lens/ops/evaluation-frameworks/) playbook. The four-week window follows the solo pilot in [How to evaluate AI tools without falling for the demo](/blog/evaluating-ai-tools-without-falling-for-the-demo/).
4. **Limit Grok Bot's access.** If you trial Grok Bot, give it a separate login with read-only access wherever the tool allows it.
5. **Write it into policy.** Add both products to your AI use policy with a named owner. Note the open Ofcom investigation in the Grok entry. Our post on [Claude Duet](/blog/claude-duet-proactive-agent-marketing-teams/) sets out the agent rules worth agreeing before anyone connects an agent to company tools.

## What's still unclear

SpaceXSI hasn't said where Grok Bot's computers run or whether its terms differ for UK customers. Its Enterprise pricing is available only through sales. On the Hermes side, a self-hosted agent is only as secure as the server it runs on. Nous offers no service-level agreement for the open-source version. Both products are moving weekly, so treat any comparison, this one included, as a snapshot. We last checked this one on 11 November 2026.
