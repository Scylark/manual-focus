Google added a multimodal filter to Search Console on 24 September 2026, so site owners can see which pages appear when people search with a photo in Google Lens or Circle to Search. The filter covers four ways of searching with an image. For product and retail brands, camera-led discovery becomes a channel you can measure by page.

## Key takeaways

- Google added a multimodal search type to the Search Console performance reports on 24 September 2026, rolling out globally from that day.
- It counts web searches that start with an image, from four entry points that include Google Lens and Circle to Search on Android.
- The multimodal filter has no query data, because Google says these searches mostly use images rather than text.
- Google's John Mueller said the data "wasn't previously in the counts", so multimodal impressions are new to the report rather than moved out of Web.
- The same filter works in the generative AI features report, which counts impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode but still shows no clicks.

## What Google added to Search Console

Google [announced the reporting](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc) on its Search Central blog on 24 September, with the rollout starting globally the same day. In the performance report the Web search type now splits into "Text-based" and "Multimodal". The same filter appears in the report for generative AI features, which Google [launched in June](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) and extended to every site worldwide by 31 August. That report counts appearances in Google's AI answers, such as AI Overviews and AI Mode.

The new data covers four entry points.

- Google Lens
- Circle to Search on Android
- Image uploads to Google Search
- The right-click Search this image option in Chrome

You can export the pages with their countries and devices, but not queries, because Google says these searches mostly use images rather than text. Google's [help page for the performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) now defines each search type.

| Report view | What it counts | Queries shown |
|---|---|---|
| Web, text-based | Searches typed into the Google search bar | Yes |
| Web, multimodal | Searches that start with an image | No |
| Generative AI features | Pages shown in AI Overviews or AI Mode | No |

The data is new as well as newly visible. When an SEO asked whether multimodal impressions had been grouped under Web all along, Google's John Mueller checked with the Search Console team and replied that "the data wasn't previously in the counts, the report has new data", [as Search Engine Roundtable reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-console-multimodal-search-type-filter-42156.html).

## Which brands should look first?

Someone points a phone at a pair of trainers on a stranger's feet or a jacket in a shop window. Google then decides which page answers. Before 24 September a brand had no direct way to see whether that page was its own product page or a retailer's listing. The new filter answers that question at page level for every site that gets this traffic.

Physical products with a recognisable look gain the most, such as footwear and outdoor kit. Premium brands whose products get photographed in the wild should look first, which is the audience we wrote the [SEO playbook for premium endurance brands](/blog/seo-playbook-premium-endurance-brands/) for. B2B services and software will see little here.

Our view at Manual Focus is that the multimodal filter matters more for what it shows about where Google sends camera traffic than for its volume. A brand that finds its product photos sending shoppers to a reseller's page has a merchandising problem it can now put a number on. We'd also treat it as another sign that visibility in Google is splitting into streams that need separate reporting, as AI citations already have, which we covered in [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/).

## What to check in your next report

1. **Rebase your comparisons.** Apply the text-based filter whenever you compare September and October with earlier months, so the new multimodal counts don't read as a ranking gain.
2. **Export the multimodal pages.** Pull the page list for the last 28 days and sort by impressions, then mark which are product pages and which are articles or guides.
3. **Look for the wrong landing page.** If Lens sends people to a blog image or an old product rather than the current product page, fix the image and page pairing on your own site first. Then ask retail partners how their listings show up. The [site audit and refresh](/lens/ops/site-audit-and-refresh/) playbook sets out how to review a whole site for problems like this.
4. **Split it by country and device.** Circle to Search runs on Android, so a mobile-heavy market with high multimodal impressions tells you where camera search is already a habit for your customers.
5. **Check the generative AI view too.** Apply the multimodal filter in the generative AI features report and see whether AI Overviews or AI Mode show your pages for image searches. Record what you find so you have a baseline to measure against. The same report helps with brand terms, where [AI Overviews spread to most tracked brand searches](/blog/google-ai-overviews-brand-searches/) within days of this launch.

## What can't the new data tell you?

Without query data you can see which page appeared but not what the person photographed, so you infer intent from the page itself. The generative AI report still shows impressions without clicks, which leaves you unable to tell whether an AI answer for an image search sent anyone to your site. Google says the metrics appear only if your site receives this traffic. Google hasn't said how multimodal searches that turn into text follow-up questions are counted, or whether it will add query-style descriptions later. Bing already shows the grounding queries behind its AI citations, which we covered when it [added Topics Portfolio](/blog/bing-topics-portfolio-ai-citation-gaps/) on 13 October.
