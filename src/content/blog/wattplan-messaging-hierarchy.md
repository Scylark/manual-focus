WattPlan is Manual Focus's own cycling training app, and since 11 September 2026 every line of its copy has traced back to one document, a messaging house. One two-word tagline, Train. Adapt., sits over one product claim, three benefits and a proof row for every feature that names the code behind it. This is how we built it.

## Key takeaways

- The WattPlan messaging house has six levels. A two-word tagline, an internal emotional benefit, a 61-character short description, one core product claim, three benefits and the features that prove each one.
- Every supporting feature in the house carries a reference to the code that ships it, so when the team removes a capability the claim retires the same day.
- The house corrected five stale claims before they reached a store listing, including a workout library of 67 where older documents said more than 100, and a calendar export that had never been built.
- Superlatives stay out of WattPlan copy because the ASA treats an unqualified superlative as a comparison with every competing product.

## Why build a messaging house at all?

[WattPlan](https://wattplan.cc/) builds a cyclist's training plan around their goals and the hours they have, then adjusts it when a ride is missed or goes harder than planned. It has a Coach the rider can talk to, and it syncs and scores rides from Garmin, Strava and Wahoo. That is a lot of product to describe across two store listings, a website, an app and emails, and left alone each surface drifts towards its own version of the story.

A messaging house stops the drift. It is the [communication hierarchy](/blog/what-is-marketing-basics/) from our basics series applied to one product, with one rule added. If a line cannot trace to a benefit in the document, it does not ship. WattPlan is our own app, so this is a worked example from the inside, mistakes included.

## What the house says

The house starts with the feeling WattPlan should create and gives the cyclist more detail as they move towards action. The top is emotional and memorable, the bottom is proof, and copy leads with a feature only when the cyclist already understands the proposition. Each level has a job.

## How two words carry four readings

Cyclists train so their bodies adapt, and WattPlan uses the same word for its core mechanism. The adaptation engine carried the word adapt in the code before the brand did, so the tagline describes the product rather than decorating it.

Two earlier lines were retired. "Your Cycling Coach That Never Switches Off" went because uptime does not sell a training app. "Turn cycling commitment into real performance improvement" went because WattPlan cannot guarantee performance, and the [CAP Code](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html) requires documentary evidence for any claim a consumer would read as objective.

## How was it built from the code?

Traceability is what separates the house from a brand deck. We wrote the proof column first, against the shipped product, so every feature names the function or file that delivers it. Pricing was checked against the subscription configuration on 18 August 2026 and the store field limits against Apple and Google's guidance on 11 September 2026. Writing that way turned up five claims that older internal documents still made and the product no longer supported.

| Stale claim | What the code showed | What the house says instead |
|---|---|---|
| More than 100 workouts | 67 workouts across seven zones | "A structured library across every training zone", because the count will change |
| Calendar export | No iCal or .ics writer exists | Roadmap work, kept out of store and pricing copy |
| Zwift export is Premium | The export ships free | Kept out of the Premium list |
| Ride Review as a feature | Shipped on 2 September 2026 as a Coach review | "A Coach review after every ride", because the benefit is the review |
| Garmin and Wahoo push as Premium | Both ship free | Claim the capability without a tier |

Each would have gone into a store listing. Google Play's [Deceptive Behavior policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077) requires an accurate description of an app's functionality in all parts of the metadata, so an overclaiming listing risks rejection, then refunds.

A routing table then names which benefit leads on each surface. The home hero carries the tagline and short description with Benefit 1 beneath. Onboarding leads with Benefit 3, because watching the plan fill itself in from a connected Garmin is the fastest proof the product offers. Adaptation notifications are Benefit 1, factual. Personal-best notifications are Benefit 2, warm and brief.

Behind the routing sit two feelings every surface should leave. Understood, because the plan takes the rider's real hours and has read their ride. Consistent, because one missed workout does not decide the week.

## What the house refuses to say

<figure class="dg dg-foundation-fig"><div class="dg-head"><span class="dg-kicker">The foundation</span><span class="dg-title">The rules every level obeys</span></div><ul class="dg-list dg-house-base"><li><span class="dg-label">Tone of voice</span>An experienced club rider who knows the science. Warm, direct, one clear recommendation.</li><li><span class="dg-label">Product substantiation</span>Shipped claims with their code references, and a corrections log for the stale ones.</li><li><span class="dg-label">Claims WattPlan does not make</span>Medical, guaranteed gains, a replacement for a human coach, safety, invented popularity.</li></ul><div class="dg-note">Everything above the foundation is copy. The longest section of the document is about silence. WattPlan makes no medical claims, promises no watts, FTP points, times or placings, and never presents the Coach as a replacement for a human coach. Its own instructions call it an advisor, not an autopilot, so the copy cannot say "fire your coach". The house also bans invented popularity, so no "trusted by thousands", and bans advertising a free trial, because there is a permanent free tier instead.

The banned vocabulary runs to three lists. Sport clichés such as epic, pain cave and beast mode. Technology filler such as seamless, disruptive and next-generation. And AI filler, where AI-powered is out because every rival can say it and it tells a rider nothing. A vocabulary table from the product's own screens sits alongside.

| Say | Not | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Programme, schedule, block | The screen is called Plan and the product has no "programme" in it |
| Coach | Assistant, bot, AI | The tab is Coach and the placeholder reads Ask your coach |
| Cyclist, rider | Athlete, user, triathlete | WattPlan is cycling only |

Our view at Manual Focus is that a messaging house earns its keep only when every claim can be traced to something the product does today, because a brand document that cannot be checked against the product drifts from it within a release. The [brand guardrails as code](/lens/ops/brand-guardrails-as-code/) playbook turns a document like this into a check that runs before copy ships.

## How to build one for your product

1. **Write the proof column first.** List what the product does today, with the feature, file or setting that delivers it, and let the benefits come from the list. Anything the list cannot support goes in a corrections log.
2. **Retire lines in writing.** Name each dropped tagline and the reason, so nobody revives it from an old deck. The [tagline system](/lens/brand/tagline-system/) playbook covers generating and testing the replacement.
3. **Put the channel limits in the document.** Apple's [product page guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) and [Google Play's listing help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859152) publish every field's limit. Record how much of each your copy uses.
4. **Route the benefits.** One table naming which benefit leads on each surface ends most arguments about what a page says first.
5. **Write the silence.** The claims you will not make, with the regulation or product limit behind each, protect you from the enthusiastic release-day draft.

## What the house hasn't settled

The document ends with open decisions rather than a flourish. Garmin and Wahoo workout push ship free, so the product gate must change before any Premium claim about them. iOS push notifications stay out of App Store copy until a build passes delivery tests on a physical iPhone. Some product strings still mix British and American spelling. A house that admits what it hasn't decided is easier to trust than one that reads as finished.
