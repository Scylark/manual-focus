Google showed an AI Overview on most of the brand-name searches DemandSphere tracks, according to data it published on 1 October 2026. The share of its tracked branded keywords returning one rose from about 26% in early September to 82.06% on 29 September, so the summary Google writes about your company now sits on your most valuable results page.

## Key takeaways

- DemandSphere reported on 1 October 2026 that AI Overviews appeared on 82.06% of its tracked branded keywords on 29 September, up from 26.12% on 1 September.
- The share peaked at 90.48% on 27 September, after a jump from 29.98% on 25 September to 69.21% on 26 September.
- Ahrefs found AI Overviews on 82.91% of US desktop results for branded keywords on 29 September, up from a 61.3% monthly average in July.
- DemandSphere counted an AI Overview whether or not it cited the brand. Neither study measured clicks.
- Google has not announced or confirmed the change.

**Update, 11 November 2026.** Google has changed where these summaries sit. On 2 November Search Engine Roundtable [reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-brands-right-42440.html) that Google had moved the AI Overviews for many brand searches into the right-hand column on desktop, the space the knowledge panel used to fill, with examples for Apple and Zillow. Google has not commented. An [Ahrefs study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-on-branded-searches/) published on 2 October also found the summary in the top spot for 48 of the 63 brand-name queries in its sample that showed one, which differs from the below-the-listing pattern this post first described.

## How fast did brand AI Overviews spread?

DemandSphere tracks branded keywords once a day per keyword, across all markets and devices, through its DemandMetrics platform. Its [report on branded AI Overviews](https://www.demandsphere.com/blog/branded-ai-overviews-september-2026/), written by Ray Grieselhuber, shows the share between roughly 26% and 35% on most days, with short spikes to 57.43% on 18 September and about 56% on 23 and 24 September. It then jumped to 69.21% on 26 September and 90.48% on 27 September. It stood at 80.23% on 28 September and 82.06% on 29 September. DemandSphere counted an AI Overview whether or not the brand itself was cited in it.

Ahrefs published a study a day later, based on 92.5 million US desktop crawls of branded keywords, which reached almost the same figure for 29 September.

Chris Long spotted the same pattern from a different direction. He posted that "93% of the brands I tested have AI Overviews appearing", naming Salesforce and Adobe among them, [as Search Engine Roundtable reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-large-brand-names-42195.html) on 30 September. Barry Schwartz noted that most of these summaries appeared below the company's own listing, with Adobe an exception where the AI Overview sat above its main page. Google's own name and news publishers were left out. Google has made no announcement.

## What changes on the brand results page

For most companies the branded results page is the one they have controlled most closely. Someone types your name because they already know you, then gets your own ads and your homepage with its sitelinks. Until September that page was mostly assembled from things you wrote or paid for.

With an AI Overview, Google becomes the author. It writes a paragraph about your company from whatever pages it judges relevant, which can include a Reddit thread or a competitor's comparison page. Those pages need not be the ones that rank highest, as we set out in [The citation moved off the page](/blog/the-citation-moved-off-the-page/). A prospect checking you out before a sales call or a journalist confirming a fact may now get Google's summary before they reach your site. If that summary carries an old price or a complaint from 2023, Google has put it on the page you rely on most.

In late September Schwartz found most of these summaries below the homepage listing, where they are less likely to take the click, though Adobe's sat on top. Ahrefs found the opposite in its sample. Google has since moved many of them to the right-hand column on desktop. Separately, Google is testing an official websites section above some AI answers, which we covered in [What Google's new AI Mode links mean for brand websites](/blog/google-ai-mode-official-websites-links/).

## Who should own the brand AI Overview?

Ownership is the harder problem. Brand searches usually belong to SEO, while the summary's content belongs to brand and comms. The sources it cites often belong to PR. Our view at Manual Focus is that the head of brand or comms should own the AI Overview on your brand name, with SEO supplying the monitoring, because what it says is a reputation question before it is a ranking one. Our [positioning audit pipeline](/lens/brand/positioning-audit-pipeline/) is a good starting point for comparing what Google says with what you want said.

## How to audit your brand AI Overview

1. **Search your own brand terms.** Run your company name and main product names in a private window on mobile and desktop, then add queries such as "[brand] reviews" and "[brand] vs [competitor]". Screenshot every AI Overview.
2. **Read each summary for errors.** Mark anything wrong or out of date. List the sources Google links beside it.
3. **Fix the sources you control.** Update your about and product pages first, then your Google Business Profile. Ask your PR team or agency to approach the review sites and directories that carry wrong facts. Request Wikipedia corrections on the article's talk page with your connection disclosed rather than editing it yourself.
4. **Set a branded click baseline.** In Search Console, filter for branded queries and compare click-through rate before and after 26 September. Mark 2 November too, when the desktop layout changed. The generative AI features report, which we covered in [our post on Lens data in Search Console](/blog/search-console-multimodal-lens-reporting/), shows impressions for pages appearing in AI Overviews.
5. **Repeat every fortnight.** Keep the same query list and screenshots so you can see whether the summary changes or Google moves it.

## What the data doesn't show

DemandSphere hasn't said how many branded keywords it tracks or which brands they belong to. It says it is still working on breakdowns by device and market, so there is no UK figure yet. Ahrefs covers US desktop results only. Chris Long's test was a social post covering about 100 brands. None of these sources measures clicks, so the traffic effect is unknown. Google hasn't confirmed the change. A jump this sharp over a few days could be a test it scales back. The brands Long named are large global companies, which may not reflect what a mid-sized company sees.
