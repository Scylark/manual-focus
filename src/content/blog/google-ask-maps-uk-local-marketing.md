Google is bringing Ask Maps, the Gemini chat inside Google Maps, to 30 European countries including the UK, it said on 16 November 2026. In the US the same chat now finds open appointments and phones businesses for the user. For any brand with shops, branches or venues, a conversation in Maps now decides which locations a customer sees.

## Key takeaways

- Google announced on 16 November 2026 that Ask Maps is expanding to 30 countries in Europe, including the UK, Spain and Germany, and is now offered in more than 200 countries and territories.
- Ask Maps can now build day-by-day trip itineraries and search a user's saved lists for places with live availability, wherever Ask Maps is offered.
- In the US, Ask Maps can find beauty, pet care, auto repair and home repair appointments and call local businesses on the user's behalf, with other regions to follow.
- Google said at launch on 12 March 2026 that Maps draws on more than 300 million places and reviews from over 500 million contributors.
- Verified businesses can switch off Google's calls for customer bookings and inquiries in Business Profile advanced settings.

## What Google added to Ask Maps

Google launched Ask Maps on [12 March 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/) in the US and India, as a way to ask Maps questions in plain language. The launch post says Maps analyses information from over 300 million places, including reviews from a community of more than 500 million contributors, and personalises answers to places a user has searched for or saved. Users could already book restaurant reservations from it.

The [16 November post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-holiday-travel-features-2026/), by Google Maps product director Maz Khorasani, adds five things. Ask Maps now builds trip itineraries with a schedule, tips and numbered pins, which people can share or export to Google Docs on desktop. Once the trip starts, the itinerary shows live traffic, whether each stop is open and the atmosphere of the next one, and the user can add a lunch stop on the way. Directions gain parking, EV charging, weather, delay and toll tips. Ask Maps can search a user's saved lists against live availability, with Google's example asking which saved places can seat five people within 30 minutes. The fifth addition is agentic booking, covered below.

## Which features reach the UK first?

| Feature | What it does | Where Google says it is available |
|---|---|---|
| Ask Maps chat | Answers plain-language questions about places | Expanding to 30 European countries including the UK, over 200 countries and territories in all |
| Trip itineraries | Day-by-day plans with stops, tips and live open status | Everywhere Ask Maps is offered |
| Saved-list search | Checks a user's saved places against live availability | Everywhere Ask Maps is offered |
| Route tips | Parking, EV charging, weather, delays and tolls | US first, other regions to follow |
| Appointment finding and calls | Finds open slots and phones businesses for the user | US first, other regions to follow |

So a UK customer will soon be able to ask Maps for a day of Christmas shopping and get back a plan that names specific shops and cafés. Google gives no date for the UK rollout beyond saying the features have begun rolling out on Android and iOS.

## How does Ask Maps choose a place?

Google hasn't said. Its posts name three inputs (places data, community reviews and the user's own searches and saved places) but not how one business is ranked against another. That leaves the reviews and the facts on a Business Profile as the inputs a brand can influence. Google's seat-five example can only be answered from published details such as hours, capacity and what reviewers say.

Google already makes calls like these. Its [Business Profile help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7690269?hl=en) says it may call a business on a customer's behalf to book appointments, check restaurant wait times or confirm the price and availability of products and services. If a business uses an online booking provider, Google may book through that partner or call directly. Where a business shares product details through Merchant Center, Google uses that data instead of calling about inventory. The page lists the calls as US only, and not in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana or Nebraska.

## What changes for multi-site brands?

Local search used to return a list of pins for the customer to choose from. With Ask Maps the customer asks for an outcome (a nail appointment at 4pm, a road trip, a table for five) and Maps comes back with a short answer or a plan. A location that isn't in that answer has lost the visit before the customer saw a list. [Lightspeed's study of AI shopping assistants](/blog/ai-shopping-assistants-favour-big-retailers/) found they picked national chains over smaller rivals up to 94% of the time in head-to-head comparisons, and nothing in Google's posts tells independents that Maps will behave differently.

Google's Ask Maps posts don't name Business Profiles, but the details Ask Maps shows, such as whether a place is open, are the ones a brand sets there, so the profile becomes source material for an AI answer. OpenAI made the ChatGPT profile the same kind of asset when it opened [Businesses in ChatGPT](/blog/businesses-in-chatgpt-business-hub-brands/) on 10 November.

Our view at Manual Focus is that UK brands with physical locations should treat Business Profile accuracy as a December priority, because Ask Maps itineraries show whether each stop is open and Google launched them for holiday trips, so wrong holiday hours now cost a place in the plan as well as a wasted journey. The booking calls matter for US locations today. For UK teams they are a preview, and the time to decide on booking partners and call settings is before Google switches them on here.

## Five checks before Christmas

1. **Fix holiday hours on every location.** Ask Maps shows whether stops are open, so set Christmas and New Year hours in each Business Profile now, and give one person the job of updating them when plans change.
2. **Ask Maps the questions your customers ask.** Once it appears on a UK phone, run 10 to 20 real requests for your category and towns, such as a family day out or a same-day repair, and note which of your locations appear and what Maps says about them. The [Category Entry Points playbook](/lens/demand/category-entry-points/) helps pick the situations worth testing.
3. **Read your reviews as AI input.** Answers draw on community reviews, so check whether yours mention the details people ask about, such as space for groups, parking or wait times, and reply where facts are wrong.
4. **Connect booking and inventory data.** If you take appointments, make sure your booking provider is linked to your Business Profile, and if you sell in store, keep local product data in Merchant Center current, so Google reads your data rather than phoning a busy shop floor.
5. **Set the call policy for US locations.** Decide whether to leave Bookings and inquiries from customers switched on, then brief front-of-house staff that Google may phone to book or check availability, and that a caller can be asked to stop.

## The gaps in Google's post

Google gives no UK date, no list of the 30 European countries beyond Spain, Germany and the UK, and no timing for appointment finding outside the US. It doesn't say how Ask Maps ranks businesses, whether ads appear in Ask Maps answers, or whether Ask Maps calls follow the Business Profile call settings. Nor has Google said whether Business Profile reports will separate Ask Maps visits from ordinary Maps searches, so for now you can't measure what the chat sends you. Until Google reports it, the nearest substitute is tracking which brands AI engines name, as [Light Meter](/blog/manual-focus-light-meter-ai-visibility/) does across six AI engines.
