The gravel opportunity

Five years of data on gravel, road, mountain bike and track cycling, globally and in the UK, and where gravel's growth could bring more people into regular cycling.

11 October 2026Global, UK focus2021 to 2026By Manual Focus

00Scope and data sources

This report compares gravel, road, mountain bike and track cycling over the five years from autumn 2021, globally and in the UK. It sets out where gravel's growth could bring more people into regular cycling, and what is missing for that to happen.

01Executive summary

Gravel is the only bike category selling above pre-pandemic levels in the UK. Gravel bike sales in the year to July 2024 were 284% above pre-pandemic levels while total bike sales fell to their lowest level in around 50 years, although gravel remains a small part of the market, at about 30,000 of around 1.45 million bikes sold in 2024. Within the same bike brands, gravel models now account for nearly 30% of UK searches for drop-bar models, up from about 26% in 2022/23. Most of that growth came early, and the category has developed steadily rather than rapidly since.

The growth is visible in events as well as sales. The UCI Gravel World Series went from 11 events in 2022 to 47 in 32 countries in 2026. The Gralloch in Scotland grew from 1,230 riders in 2023 to more than 3,000 from 47 nations in 2026, and Dirty Reiver sold out its 2026 edition in six hours. The most searched UK gravel organiser, Glorious Gravel, now draws more searches than the Dragon Ride, although total searches for named gravel events have been flat since 2022/23.

This matters beyond the sport. England has 11.8 million inactive adults, 24.7% of the adult population, and the number of adults cycling for leisure has fallen from 7.3 million in 2019/20 to about 6.1 million. Large studies link regular cycling with lower mortality, lower heart disease and better mental health. In the Department for Transport's latest survey, 48% of adults named safety as a reason they do not cycle more, and 52% said off-road and segregated paths would encourage them. Gravel riding takes place largely on forest tracks, byways and quiet lanes, so it is one route into regular cycling that involves less riding alongside motor traffic. This report does not suggest gravel alone will close the activity gap. It sets out where gravel's growth could reach people who do not ride today.

The opportunity covers people, places and products. The people include adults who stopped riding for leisure after 2020 and the 26% of women who do not cycle but would like to. The places include England's public forests, which recorded 313 million visits in 2024/25, although cyclists can legally use only 22% of the rights of way network in England and Wales. On products, most UK gravel events and content are aimed at experienced riders, and Google's demographic data shows gravel bike searches skew more male than road or mountain bike searches.

The organisations that could grow participation have only started. British Cycling recognised gravel as a racing category in mid-2025 and held its first National Gravel Championships in August 2026. Gravel does not appear as a category on its website or in its strategy to 2029, and the site earns an estimated 24 visits a month from 20 common UK gravel searches, against more than 8,000 for the top-ranking bike retailer. The growth so far has come from independent organisers, retailers and brands.

02Health benefits and barriers to cycling

The evidence is compelling. Large studies consistently find that people who cycle live longer and report better mental health than people who do not.

These studies measure associations rather than proving cause and effect, but the pattern holds across different countries, study designs and more than a million participants.

Gravel is not yet diverse. Google's demographic data for UK searches shows women's interest in gravel bikes scoring 74 against 100 for men, lower than for road bikes (84) or mountain bikes (88). Health and economic value

  • Health. The Walking and Cycling Index 2025 values walking, wheeling and cycling across 17 UK areas at £9.56bn a year, saving the NHS £346.7m and preventing 28,037 serious conditions. These figures cover all modes, not cycling alone.
  • Rural economy. Scotland's mountain bike sector, the closest off-road precedent, generated £105m of GVA in 2015 and was projected to reach £158m by 2025. Gravel events bring riders to the same kind of rural places. Unbound's 2025 field brought an estimated $21.8m to one Kansas county.
  • Industry. Gravel is the bike category that has held up through the downturn in the UK, Germany and Austria, so growth in riders feeds straight into shops and brands.

04What gravel cycling is and how it grew

Gravel is drop-bar riding on mixed surfaces, including forest roads, byways, farm tracks and quiet lanes. It sits between road and mountain bike. The bike looks like a road bike with wider tyres and more clearance, and it works as an all-rounder. The racing ranges from short UCI qualifiers to 200-mile endurance events, and most events let amateurs start alongside professionals.

How the discipline grew

  • Events came before governing bodies. The format came from the US Midwest. Unbound Gravel had 34 starters in 2006 and has run with a field of about 5,000 since 2024. The UCI created its Gravel World Series and World Championships in 2022, 16 years after Unbound began.
  • Industry investment. SRAM launched its XPLR gravel groupset in August 2021 and Shimano launched GRX Di2 1x12 in June 2025. Nearly every major road and mountain bike brand now sells dedicated race and endurance gravel bikes.
  • Strava activity. Strava's 2023 report recorded a 55% rise in athletes logging a gravel ride, against 13% for mountain biking and 23% for e-bikes.
  • Professional racing and prize money. The Life Time Grand Prix purse rose from $250,000 in 2022 to $590,000 for 2026. WorldTour riders now race gravel worlds, and in the US the number of UCI-level road teams fell from 19 to 11 over a decade while gravel grew.
i

Constraints on the largest events. Several high-profile events have stopped growing for reasons of capacity rather than demand. Unbound runs with a field of about 5,000, and its organiser has said the limit is the accommodation available in Emporia. Half its places now go through qualifying events. SBT GRVL was capped at 1,800 riders by its county permit for 2025. The Gravel Earth Series fell from about 25 events in 2025 to 12 in 2026 after several events left the series, reportedly over fees, while the UCI Gravel World Series grew from 33 to 47 events over the same period. The 2026 World Championships in Australia drew a smaller elite field because of the long-haul venue.

The pattern is the same in every market with published category data. The total market shrinks after the pandemic boom, road and mountain bike lose share, and gravel holds or grows. One UK shop told Cycling Weekly its gravel sales rose 110% in a year while road sales fell 45%, which is a single example but matches the national figures.

PoliticalRoad racing needs police support. One organiser was quoted £35,000 for 30 police motorbikes in 2024, against £3,500 for six in 2019, and cancelled. Gravel runs on private estates and forestry land with no traffic management.

What event data shows

  • First-time racers. More than 40% of the 1,230 riders at the first Gralloch in 2023 were in their first gravel race.
  • British riders at the World Championships. 345 British riders entered the 2025 Gravel World Championships in the Netherlands, more than from Germany, France or the USA. The only UK qualifying event is The Gralloch.
  • Travel. The Gralloch drew riders from 47 nations in 2026, and Unbound's 2025 field brought an estimated $21.8m of economic impact to one Kansas county. Gravel riders travel for events, which makes them relevant to regional tourism and economic development bodies.

What UK search data shows

Generic searches such as "gravel bike" and "road bike" are a weak guide to a maturing category. As riders learn the market, they search for specific bikes and events by name instead, and generic searches fell for every bike type in the last year. This section therefore uses three measures, all as actual Google Ads search counts for the UK in September to August years. These are model-name searches within the same brands, named event searches, and generic searches.

Model-name searches within the same brands

Comparing a brand's gravel models with its own road models removes the effect of brand popularity. Across 11 brands that sell both, gravel models' share of drop-bar model searches rose from 25.6% in 2022/23 to 29.7% in 2025/26. Searches for the gravel models rose 12%, from 256,580 to 287,940 a year, while searches for the road models fell 9%, from 746,520 to 680,560. Most of the gravel gain came in the first year. Since 2023/24 gravel model searches have been broadly flat, at around 290,000 a year.

Searches for events by name show how gravel events compare with established road sportives. Generic searches

Generic searches tell a similar story. UK searches for "gravel bike" fell from 331,500 to 281,800 a year and searches for "road bike" from 268,200 to 240,100, so gravel has stayed level with or ahead of road, while both declined with the market. Generic event searches, such as "gravel race" and "sportive", still favour road by 3.4 to 1, down from 5.7 to 1 in 2022/23. Generic terms understate gravel because a growing share of gravel interest goes to model and event names.

→

What the search data shows. Gravel's main growth happened before and around 2022. Since then it has held its position and gained share slowly. Gravel models now account for nearly 30% of drop-bar model searches at the brands measured, and the leading gravel event names draw search volumes comparable to established road sportives. The category has grown, and its development is now steady rather than rapid.

Independent organisers built the UK's gravel calendar, mostly in Scotland and the north of England. Glorious Gravel alone runs a race series and more than 30 rides a year, and it is also the most visible gravel organiser in UK search. Lessons from road racing. Road race organisers have blamed rising costs for safety, insurance and policing, and circuit races fell from about 700 to 600 between 2022 and 2025. Building organiser support into gravel from the start would help it avoid the same decline.

Mediaroad.cc runs a dedicated off-road section, and BikeRadar, Cycling Weekly and GCN cover gravel regularly. Unbound's 2026 livestream reported 470,000 views.

Lloyds already backs the national gravel championships. A national gravel series would give the partnership a mass-participation property with sell-out events and a higher share of women in gravel racing interest than road racing.

12British Cycling's current position on gravel

British Cycling recognised gravel as a racing category in mid-2025 and held its first Lloyds National Gravel Championships at Ae Forest in August 2026. After that weekend its events calendar listed no other gravel events, and its strategy to 2029 does not mention gravel.

  • Website structure. The British Cycling website has Road, Off-Road and Track menus. Off-Road contains BMX Freestyle, BMX Racing, Cyclo-cross and Mountain bike. Gravel does not appear as a category anywhere in the site's navigation (checked 12 October 2026). During the build-up to the 2026 championships, Manual Focus could not find the event from the site's navigation.
  • The championships. The 2025 British Gravel Championships, run independently by Red On Sports at Dalby Forest, drew hundreds of riders and streamed its elite races free on YouTube. Neither the 2025 nor the 2026 event has published an entry total. Our assessment, based on results and coverage of both, is that the 2025 field was at least three times the size of the 2026 field, which road.cc described as relatively small.
  • Comparison with track. British Cycling runs six national track series and has a dedicated Track section on its website. UK searches for gravel bikes outnumber searches for track bikes by about 17 to 1, at 281,800 against 16,800 in the 12 months to August 2026.

13SWOT analysis of gravel as a route into cycling

Strengths

  • Takes place mostly away from motor traffic, on forest tracks, byways and quiet lanes
  • Exercise and time in nature on the same ride
  • The only bike category above pre-pandemic sales in the UK, with flagship events selling out in hours
  • A growing global structure, from 11 to 47 UCI series events in four years

Weaknesses

  • Gravel bike search interest skews more male than road or mountain bike search interest
  • Cyclists can use only 22% of rights of way in England and Wales
  • No UK participation data specific to gravel
  • Almost no beginner products, and governing body structure that is one year old

Opportunities

  • 52% of adults say off-road paths would get them cycling more
  • 26% of women would like to cycle but do not
  • 313 million visits a year to England's public forests, an audience already outdoors
  • Health, tourism and land partners all have reasons to back it

Threats

  • Growth stays racing-led and never reaches beginners
  • Access conflicts with walkers, riders and landowners if numbers rise without management
  • Capacity limits at the largest events, as seen at Unbound and SBT GRVL
  • Household budgets squeezed, as British Cycling's own membership fall shows

14Recommendations

The five recommendations are in order, because each one makes the next easier. None of them touches the UK Sport funding for the Olympic programme, which is tied to Olympic disciplines.

Priority 1 · Strategy

Add gravel to participation plans with targets

British Cycling and Sport England should name gravel in their participation plans, with measures attached. The right measures are inactive adults brought into regular riding, the share of women and first-time riders, and the number of beginner rides and routes, as well as race entries.

Priority 2 · Access

Open and signpost places to ride

Waymarked beginner gravel loops at public forests, starting from car parks with bike hire. Template agreements with Forestry England, Forestry and Land Scotland and private estates. Support for Cycling UK's case for wider legal access to rights of way in England and Wales, where cyclists can use only 22% of the network today.

Priority 3 · Beginners

Build the beginner pathway

Led rides, skills sessions and women-led groups, run through clubs, shops and forests. The 29% of women who cite confidence as a barrier need skills and company more than they need a race. Shops selling 30,000 gravel bikes a year are the natural place to start.

Priority 4 · Events

Add beginner and non-competitive event formats

Short, non-competitive distances and women's starts at existing events, then a national series for those who want to race, with The Gralloch as the UCI qualifier. Organisers need insurance that fits mixed-surface events and support with land access, so that gravel does not repeat road racing's cost spiral.

Priority 5 · Search

Improve search visibility for gravel

A UK gravel route and event finder, a beginner hub and regional guides. Today the searches where new riders start go to listing sites, organisers and retailers. British Cycling's domain already ranks and earns AI citations when the content exists, so the shortfall is content.
