Google now shows an AI Overview on most searches for a brand's own name, according to data DemandSphere published on 1 October 2026. The share of its tracked branded keywords returning one rose from about 26% in early September to 82.06% on 29 September, so the summary Google writes about your company now sits on your most valuable results page.

## The September numbers

DemandSphere tracks branded keywords once a day per keyword, across all markets and devices, through its DemandMetrics platform. Its [report on branded AI Overviews](https://www.demandsphere.com/blog/branded-ai-overviews-september-2026/), written by Ray Grieselhuber, shows the share holding between roughly 26% and 35% for most of the month before it jumped to 69.21% on 26 September and 90.48% on 27 September. It stood at 80.23% on 28 September and 82.06% on 29 September. DemandSphere counted an AI Overview whether or not the brand itself was cited in it.

Nectiv's Chris Long spotted the same pattern from a different direction. He posted that "93% of the brands I tested have AI Overviews appearing", naming Reddit, Salesforce, Amazon and Adobe among them, [as Search Engine Roundtable reported](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-large-brand-names-42195.html) on 30 September. Barry Schwartz noted that most of these summaries appear below the company's own listing, with Adobe an exception where the AI Overview sat above its main page. Google's own name and news publishers were left out. Google has made no announcement.

## Your brand search has a new narrator

For most companies the branded results page is the one they have controlled most closely. Someone types your name because they already know you, then gets your homepage, your sitelinks, perhaps a knowledge panel and your own ads. Until September that page was mostly assembled from things you wrote or paid for.

With an AI Overview, Google becomes the author. It writes a paragraph about your company from whatever pages it judges relevant, which can include a review site, a Reddit thread, an old press story or a competitor's comparison page. A prospect checking you out before a sales call, a journalist confirming a fact or a candidate reading up before an interview may now get Google's summary before they reach your site. If that summary carries an old price, a discontinued product or a complaint from 2023, Google has put it on the page you rely on most.

Ownership is the harder problem. Brand searches usually belong to SEO, the summary's content belongs to brand and comms, and the sources it cites often belong to PR. Our view at Manual Focus is that the head of brand or comms should own the AI Overview on your brand name, with SEO supplying the monitoring, because what it says is a reputation question before it is a ranking one. Our [positioning audit pipeline](/lens/brand/positioning-audit-pipeline/) is a good starting point for comparing what Google says with what you want said.

For now Google mostly places the summary below your homepage listing, where it is less likely to take the click than it would be above it, though the Adobe result shows Google will put it on top for some brands.

## Five checks for this week

1. **Search your own brand terms.** Run your company name, your main product names and "[brand] reviews", "[brand] pricing" and "[brand] vs [competitor]" in a private window on mobile and desktop. Screenshot every AI Overview.
2. **Read each summary for errors.** Mark anything out of date, wrong or unflattering, and list the sources Google links beside it.
3. **Fix the sources you control.** Update your about page, product pages, Google Business Profile and any Wikipedia or directory entries the summary leans on, and ask PR to approach third-party pages that carry wrong facts.
4. **Set a branded click baseline.** In Search Console, filter for branded queries and compare click-through rate before and after 26 September. The generative AI features report, which we covered in [our post on Lens data in Search Console](/blog/search-console-multimodal-lens-reporting/), shows impressions for pages appearing in AI Overviews.
5. **Repeat every fortnight.** Keep the same query list and screenshots so you can see whether Google's summary, its sources and its position move.

## Gaps in the evidence

DemandSphere hasn't said how many branded keywords it tracks or which brands they belong to, and says it is still working on breakdowns by device and market, so there is no UK figure yet. Chris Long's test is a social post, not a published study. Neither source measures clicks, so the traffic effect is unknown. Google hasn't confirmed the change, and a jump this sharp over a few days could be a test it scales back. The brands Long named are large global companies, which may not reflect what a mid-sized company sees.
