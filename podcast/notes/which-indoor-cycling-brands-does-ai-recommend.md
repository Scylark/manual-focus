James and Hannah walk through the first Manual Focus AI Shortlist study: six AI
engines, 309 UK questions on smart trainers, training apps and smart bikes,
each asked three times, for 5,562 answers. Wahoo leads trainers at 83% and
Zwift leads apps at 88%, with Wahoo and Wattbike close on smart bikes. But the
leader changes with the buying moment, the engines disagree on challenger
brands, the same question gives the same brands only 20% of the time, and
cycling media supplies over half the citations. They finish with three jobs to
hand to AI and what the study can't tell you. The interactive report and a
free one-page AI Shortlist card for brands in the study are on
manual-focus.co.uk.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. The first needs file
uploads. The second needs an assistant that can run searches on other engines
or a person to paste the answers in, plus a schedule. The third needs web
browsing.

### 1. Map your buying moments

```text
I run marketing for [brand], which sells [category] in the UK.

I want the questions buyers ask before they choose a brand in my category,
grouped by the buying moment behind them. Use these moments: value,
beginner, performance, problem (something isn't working), and any others the
data shows.

Attached are:
- a Search Console export of queries for the last 3 months
- our customer service tags or ticket subjects for the same period

From these, write the 20 questions a buyer in [category] asks most, phrased
the way a UK buyer would type them into an AI assistant, with no brand names
in the question. Tag each with its buying moment.

Give me a table with the columns: Question | Buying moment | Evidence (the
queries or tags it came from) | Why it matters for us.

Then name the one buying moment we most need to win and say why. Don't
invent search volumes; if a figure isn't in the files, say so.
```

### 2. Check each engine separately

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs on the first Monday of each month.

Here are 10 unbranded buyer questions for [category] in the UK:
[paste questions]

For each question, get the answer from each AI engine we track: [list the
engines you can reach, for example your own assistant plus Google's AI
answers]. Ask each question three times per engine, in a fresh
conversation each time, set to a UK location where possible. If you can't
reach an engine yourself, tell me and give me a simple form so a person can
paste the answers in.

Record which brands each answer names. Then give me a table with the
columns: Engine | Question | Brands named (all three runs) | Times [brand]
was named (0 to 3) | Times each of [competitor 1], [competitor 2] and
[competitor 3] was named.

Finish with our shortlist rate per engine (answers naming us divided by all
answers), the change since last month, and the engine where we're weakest.
Say plainly which runs you couldn't complete.
```

### 3. Audit the sites AI cites

```text
I run marketing for [brand] in [category], UK.

Search for the buyer's guides, reviews and round-ups most likely to be cited
when someone asks an AI engine to recommend a [category product] in the UK.
Start with the specialist titles for [category], then any others that rank for
these questions: [paste 5 of your buyer questions].

For each guide, give me a table with the columns: Site | Guide title | URL |
Date last updated | Is [brand] included? | Which of our products, and is the
detail current (price, specs, model name)? | Who is listed instead of us.

Then give me a short hit list: the five guides where getting included or
corrected would matter most, and why. Don't guess at a guide's contents;
if you couldn't open it, mark it "not checked".
```
