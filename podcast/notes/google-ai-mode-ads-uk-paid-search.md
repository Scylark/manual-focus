James and Hannah talk through Google's 28 October 2026 announcement that ads
in AI Mode are scaling to ten countries now, with the UK, Brazil and Mexico
due in the fourth quarter, and that multiturn ads have launched in the US.
Google's help page says existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max
campaigns are eligible automatically, there's no separate opt-out, and results
are reported only in aggregate. They cover why the UK launch lands in peak
trading, what it means for Shopping and search teams, and three jobs to hand
to AI before December.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or
Copilot. Swap the bracketed parts for your own details. All three need file
uploads. The third also needs an assistant that can open web pages and run on
a schedule.

### 1. Build the baseline before the UK launch

```text
I run paid search for [brand], [one line on what you sell], advertising
mainly in [market].

Google has said ads in AI Mode will reach the UK in the fourth quarter of
2026. Existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns become eligible
automatically, and Google reports the results only in aggregate, so I need a
clear "before" picture to compare against.

Attached are:
- a campaign performance export for the last 8 weeks (Search, Shopping and
  Performance Max), by week and campaign
- the search terms report for the same period, with clicks, cost and
  conversions

Give me:
1. A table with the columns: Campaign | Type | Weekly average spend |
   Clicks | Conversions | Cost per conversion | Conversion rate.
2. A second table that splits search terms into length bands (1 to 2 words,
   3 to 4, 5 to 7, 8 or more) with the columns: Length band | Share of
   clicks | Share of cost | Share of conversions | Cost per conversion.
3. Three sentences on what a shift towards longer queries would look like in
   these numbers.

Use only the figures in the files. If a column is missing or a campaign has
too little data to say anything, mark it "not in data" rather than estimating.
```

### 2. Draft negative keywords for conversational queries

```text
I manage Google Ads for [brand] in [market]. Google is bringing ads into AI
Mode, where queries run much longer and more conversational than classic
searches. Negative keywords and brand exclusions are the only controls Google
lists for those placements, so I want my negatives in order first.

Attached is the search terms report for the last [8] weeks with clicks, cost
and conversions.

Find search terms that are informational or comparison phrasing (for example
"what is", "how to", "vs", "best ... for", "review") which have spent money
and never converted. Group them into themes.

Give me a table with the columns: Theme | Example search terms | Cost in the
period | Conversions | Suggested negative keyword | Match type | Why.

Then list any themes you would NOT exclude because some terms in them did
convert, with the converting examples. Don't invent search terms that aren't
in the file, and say which rows you couldn't classify.
```

### 3. Check the top products in the feed every week

```text
Set this up as a routine that runs every Monday morning until the end of
January.

I run ecommerce marketing for [brand] at [website]. Our Shopping ads are
built from our Merchant Center feed, and Google is bringing sponsored product
listings into AI Mode answers in the UK.

Attached is a Merchant Center product export for our top 50 products by
revenue, with title, description, key attributes (size, colour, material and
so on), price, availability and product page URL.

For each product, open the product page and check whether the feed matches
it on title, price, availability and key attributes. Also flag titles that
leave out the attributes a shopper would name in a long question, such as
size, fit, material or use.

Give me a table with the columns: Product | Revenue rank | Issue | Feed
value | Page value | Suggested fix. Rank it by revenue, and compare with last
week's run to list what's new, fixed or still open.

If you can't open a page or a field is empty, say so in that row rather than
guessing.
```

### And one for you

Brief finance before peak. Tell your CFO that Google may add a new placement
to UK Search in the fourth quarter, that Google Ads won't report it
separately, and that shifts in cost per click or conversion rate in December
may not be seasonal.
