Owned media is any channel a brand controls, such as its website, email list and app. Paid media is attention it buys, such as ads and sponsorship. Earned media is what other people choose to say about it, from press coverage to reviews. Forrester set out the three roles in 2009 and argued that no media should stand alone.

## Key takeaways

- Owned is what you control, paid is what you buy, earned is what others give you by choice.
- Forrester's December 2009 report, No Media Should Stand Alone, argued the three work best together, with paid media acting as a catalyst.
- Social profiles sit awkwardly. You control the posts, the platform controls the reach, so many teams treat them as rented.
- Gini Dietrich's PESO model, launched in 2014, adds shared media for social conversation.
- In the UK, the CAP Code requires paid content such as advertorials to be obviously identifiable, so paid can't pass as earned.

## Where does the framework come from?

Forrester's Sean Corcoran published the research that made the three terms standard in December 2009. The report, [No Media Should Stand Alone](https://www.forrester.com/report/No-Media-Should-Stand-Alone/RES54869), set out the roles of owned, earned and paid media in online marketing. Its summary argues that all three can play specific roles in meeting marketers' objectives, especially when they work together, and advises looking for chances to shift short-term paid media into the role of catalyst while building a solar system of long-term owned touchpoints.

That metaphor is still the best way to picture it. Owned media sits at the centre, paid media pushes attention towards it, and earned media orbits around it in conversations the brand doesn't control.

| | Owned | Paid | Earned |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definition | Channels the brand controls | Attention the brand buys | What others say or share by choice |
| Examples | Website, email list, app, blog, podcast | Search, social and display ads, TV, sponsorship | Press coverage, reviews, word of mouth, citations in AI answers |
| Main strength | Permanent, cheap to reuse, full control | Reach on demand, fast, measurable | Credibility the brand can't buy |
| Main weakness | Slow to build an audience | Stops when the money stops | Can't be controlled or guaranteed |
| Best measured by | Audience you can reach again | Cost per result | Quality of coverage and who cites you |

## What is owned media?

Owned media is any channel where the brand decides what appears and keeps the audience. Your website, email list, app, blog and podcast are the clearest examples. The value builds over time, because every subscriber or regular reader is someone you can reach again without paying a platform.

Seth Godin's idea of [permission marketing](https://seths.blog/2008/01/permission-mark/) explains why owned audiences matter. He describes it as the privilege of delivering anticipated, personal and relevant messages to people who want to get them, and argues it is a privilege rather than a right. An email list built on that permission is worth more than a large list of people who never asked to hear from you.

The AI-era version of owned media is covered in [owned channels in the AI era](/blog/owned-channels-website-to-platform/), which argues that a website should ship and test like a product. The [lifecycle journey builder](/lens/demand/lifecycle-journey-builder/) and [subscription and membership](/lens/demand/subscription-membership/) playbooks show how to turn owned audiences into repeat revenue.

## What is paid media?

Paid media is attention you buy. Search ads, social ads, display, online video, television, radio, outdoor and sponsorship all count. Its strength is control over timing and reach, since you can be in front of a chosen audience tomorrow. Its weakness is that the reach stops when the budget does.

Paid media is also where the [above-the-line and below-the-line](/blog/above-the-line-below-the-line-basics/) split mostly lives, from mass television to tightly targeted retargeting. How platform bidding agents now run most of the auction is covered in [paid channels in the AI era](/blog/paid-channels-bidding-agents/).

Paid has to look like paid. In the UK, rule 2.1 of the [CAP Code](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html) says marketing communications must be obviously identifiable as such, and rule 2.4 says marketers and publishers must make clear that advertorials are marketing communications. Sponsored articles and paid influencer posts are paid media, whatever they look like.

## What is earned media?

Earned media is what other people choose to say, share or publish about you. Press coverage, reviews, social mentions, recommendations and word of mouth all count, and so do mentions and citations in AI answers. You can't buy it or guarantee it, which is why audiences trust it more.

Earned media mostly follows from something worth talking about, such as a remarkable product, a sharp opinion or original data. [Earned channels in the AI era](/blog/earned-channels-pr-in-the-ai-era/) covers how journalists' use of AI has changed what earns coverage, and the Basics guide to [PR 101](/blog/pr-101-basics/) covers how public relations works. The [earned-media pitch generator](/lens/content/earned-media-pitch-generator/) playbook matches stories to the journalists who cover the beat.

## Is social media owned, paid or earned?

All three, which is why it causes arguments. Your brand's own profile is owned content on a platform that controls its reach, so many marketers call it rented. Boosted posts and social ads are paid. Other people's posts, shares and comments about you are earned.

Gini Dietrich's [PESO model](https://spinsucks.com/communication/peso-model-breakdown/), launched with her book Spin Sucks in 2014, handles this by adding shared media as a fourth type, covering content and engagement on social platforms. PR teams often prefer PESO, while media planners tend to stick with the original three.

## How should the three work together?

The strongest plans use paid and earned media to grow owned audiences. A paid campaign that builds the email list keeps paying off after the budget stops. Press coverage that links to a useful page on your site brings readers you can keep. Owned content that is clear and well sourced gives journalists and AI answers something to cite.

Our view at Manual Focus is that most brands under-invest in owned media because its returns are slow, then overspend on paid to make up for an audience they never built. Start the audit with owned, and judge every paid and earned activity partly by how much owned audience it leaves behind.
