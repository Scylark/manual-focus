James and Hannah talk through Google's 16 November 2026 update to Ask Maps,
the Gemini chat inside Google Maps, which is expanding to 30 European
countries including the UK. They cover the new trip itineraries and
saved-list search that come with it, the US-only appointment finding that
phones businesses for the user, what Google does and doesn't say about how
Ask Maps picks a place, and why holiday hours on every Business Profile are
the job for December. Then three jobs to hand to AI before Christmas.

## Get AI on this

Paste these into whichever AI assistant you use. Swap the bracketed parts for
your own details. All three need file uploads, and the second works best as a
fortnightly routine.

### 1. Audit every location before Christmas

```text
I run marketing for [brand], which has [number] locations in [market]. Our
store finder is at [store finder URL].

Google's Ask Maps can build trip itineraries that show whether each stop is
open, so I want every location's details right before the Christmas period.

Attached are:
- an export of our Business Profile locations (name, address, phone, regular
  hours, special hours, website link)
- our store finder data or a copy of each location page
- our Christmas and New Year opening plan by location

Compare the three sources for every location. Give me a table with the
columns: Location | Field | Business Profile value | Website value | Opening
plan value | Correct value to set | Action needed.

Only list rows where the sources disagree or a value is missing. Sort by the
first date affected, earliest first. If a location appears in one source but
not another, list it separately. Don't fill in any value you can't see in the
files. Mark it "missing" instead.
```

### 2. Track what Ask Maps says about us

```text
Set this up as a routine I run every other week.

We are [brand], with locations in [towns or regions]. Our main competitors
locally are [competitors].

Help me track how Google's Ask Maps answers customer requests in our
category. First, write 15 requests a real customer might type into Ask Maps
for [category] in [towns], covering different situations (a family day out,
a same-day need, a group booking, a specific product). Keep each one under 20
words.

Each fortnight I'll paste in the answers Ask Maps gave on a UK phone. For
each answer, record:
- which of our locations appeared, and in what position
- which competitors appeared
- anything the answer said about us that is wrong or out of date

Give me a table with the columns: Date | Request | Our locations named |
Competitors named | Errors about us. Then compare with the previous run and
list what changed. If I haven't pasted an answer for a request, mark it "not
checked" rather than guessing.
```

### 3. Read reviews the way Maps might

```text
We are [brand]. Attached are the last [number] months of customer reviews
for [location or locations], exported with the date, rating and text.

AI answers in Google Maps draw on community reviews, so I want to know which
practical details our reviews cover.

1. List the practical details customers mention (for example parking, space
   for groups, wait times, accessibility, opening hours), with how many
   reviews mention each one, by location.
2. List the details our customers would likely ask about that no review
   mentions.
3. Find reviews that state something factually wrong about us (wrong hours,
   a service we don't offer, a closed branch) and draft a short, polite reply
   to each, correcting the fact.

Give parts 1 and 2 as one table with the columns: Detail | Mentions by
location | Mentioned or missing. Give part 3 as a list of review quotes with
the draft reply under each. Don't invent facts about our business for the
replies. Leave a [check] placeholder where you'd need information I haven't
given you.
```
